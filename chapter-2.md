# First Light

At dawn the ship was gone, and Idris told himself that was the good outcome — a vessel that had simply started moving again in the night, some engine trouble sorted, some sleeping crew waking to finish their crossing. He wrote that in the log too, because a keeper writes down the hopeful explanation as readily as the strange one, and lets the record decide later which was true.

It was the small rowboat washing up on the rocks an hour later that changed the shape of the morning. Empty. Oars still shipped, neat, as if someone had climbed out onto the rocks with great care rather than great panic. Beside it, half-buried in the shingle, a logbook of its own — waterlogged, but the last entry still legible, written in a hand that wasn't quite steady:

*Saw the light. Rowing toward the light. Someone must be keeping it — someone always is.*

No name. No ship's name either, not on the boat, not anywhere Idris could find. He carried the logbook back up to the lighthouse and set it beside his own, and for a long time he simply stood at the window, looking out at a sea that had apparently sent someone toward him and then, somewhere between the ship and the rock, taken them quietly back.

He thought about all the keepers before him, and their identical note in the log. *The light matters more than you do.* He'd always read it as a warning about duty. Standing there that morning, the strange logbook open beside his own, he wondered for the first time if it had actually been a warning about something else entirely — that the light was the thing people found their way toward, whether or not there was still a keeper, whether or not there was still a shore worth reaching, and that the keeping of it mattered a great deal more than anyone standing in the tower ever fully understood.

He trimmed the lamp early that evening, and wrote one line in his own log before he climbed back down.

*Kept the light. Someone was rowing toward it. I intend to still be here the next time someone is.*
