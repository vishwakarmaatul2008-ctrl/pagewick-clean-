# Chapter 5 — The Pressure Chamber

The clock on the wall read 06:42 AM. The rain outside had settled into a steady, grey drizzle, casting long, muted shadows across Arjun's tiny living room.

Outside, the first local trains were already clattering along the Western Line tracks near Dadar Station. Millions of commuters were waking up, checking their phones, and preparing for the workday, completely oblivious to the fact that by 09:00 AM, three major regional banks holding their life savings would be pushed into an artificial death spiral.

Inside Flat 402, Arjun's ThinkPad was running on its secondary battery.

Knowing the enemy was Aegis Capital gave him a target, but it also stripped away his last remaining illusion of safety. Aegis wasn't a solitary hacker operating out of a basement; it was a multi-billion-rupee institutional titan with direct lines to telecommunication backbones, high-level political connections, and private security teams.

Arjun didn't attempt to breach Aegis's primary firewalls. That would be suicide. Instead, he targeted their internal communication bridge—the private, encrypted VoIP system used by desk managers at their BKC headquarters to coordinate trade executions before market open.

Using a leaked administrative API key from an old Aegis compliance report he had archived months ago, Arjun tapped into the audio stream for Desk 04.

He pulled his headphones over his ears and adjusted the frequency filter.

Static hissed in his ears for four seconds, followed by the crisp, unhurried voice of a man speaking over a high-end desktop microphone.

"...the short allocations are locked at eighty-five percent leverage," the voice said calmly. The speaker was sipping something from a ceramic mug; Arjun could hear the faint clink of a spoon against glass. "When the RBI clearinghouse synchronizes at 09:00 AM, the fractional siphon automatically settles into the primary market-maker account. The sell orders fire three seconds later. By 09:15 AM, Maharashtra Central's stock drops forty percent. The panic will do the rest."

A second, younger voice joined the line. "What about the Dadar auditor? The one who flagged the four-paisa exception?"

A short, dry laugh came through the earpiece.

"Vane? He's neutralized. He tried a primitive denial-of-service attack two hours ago. We routed his cellular tower, dropped his main gateway, and flagged his personal IP address with a synthetic transaction signature. If he opens his mouth or tries to send a packet anywhere near the RBI server, an automated fraud report drops onto the Economic Offences Wing desk with his name on it. He's boxed in. He can take his four crore or go to Taloja Jail. He knows how the world works."

Arjun's jaws clenched until his teeth ached.

They hadn't just blocked him; they had built a complete, ironclad trap. If he did nothing, Aegis crushed the regional banks and bought the wreckage for pennies. If he reported them, Aegis's pre-packaged evidence would frame Arjun as the rogue operator who built the siphon in the first place.

Two hours and fifteen minutes until market open.

Arjun took off his headphones and set them on the desk. His hands were completely still.

The adversary's entire strategy relied on a single, arrogance-driven assumption: that Arjun's only two choices were greed or fear. Take the bribe, or face ruin.

They assumed he wanted to save himself. They assumed he wanted to destroy the code.

"You built a perfect machine," Arjun whispered to the dim room, a cold, razor-thin smile touching his lips. "You made sure the script couldn't be stopped, couldn't be deleted, and couldn't be blocked."

He opened a fresh, local text editor on the ThinkPad.

"So I won't stop it. I'll just teach it how to read."

He didn't need to break Aegis's encryption. He didn't need to shut down the ghost vault. Aegis's automated execution loop was scheduled to fire at precisely 08:59:58 AM, sending the short-sell orders into the national exchange while routing the stolen ₹8.4 Crore into their market-maker escrow node.

Arjun began writing a low-level memory injection payload—not to attack Aegis, but to hijack the metadata headers attached to the transaction payload itself.

If Aegis wanted to execute the biggest financial heist in the state's history at 09:00 AM, Arjun was going to make sure they signed their full legal name on every single byte.
