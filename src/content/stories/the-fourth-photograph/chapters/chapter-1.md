# Chapter 1 — The Empty Album

The apartment still smelled like her grandmother.

Naina stood in the doorway for a long moment before she made herself step inside.

Dust hung in the afternoon light.

The furniture hadn't moved in years.

She had expected grief.

She hadn't expected how loud silence could be.

The lawyer had given her the keys three days ago.

*Everything belongs to you now,* he'd said. *She left no other family.*

Naina had nodded, signed the papers, and driven four hours to a town she barely remembered visiting as a child.

Now she was standing in a dead woman's living room, wondering where to even begin.

She started with the bookshelf.

Old paperbacks. A sewing box. A tin of buttons.

And, on the bottom shelf, a photo album bound in cracked brown leather.

Naina sat down on the dusty carpet and opened it.

The first page held a single photograph.

Naina, maybe five years old, sitting on her grandmother's lap on this very couch.

She didn't remember it being taken.

That wasn't unusual. She'd been five.

The second page held another photograph.

Naina at seven, standing in front of a birthday cake, candles lit.

Also unremembered. Also unremarkable.

The third page held a third photograph.

Naina at maybe nine, standing alone in what looked like a garden she didn't recognize.

She frowned at that one for a while.

Then she turned the page, expecting more.

There was nothing.

Blank cardboard, page after page, all the way to the back cover.

Three photographs. Dozens of empty pages.

Naina closed the album and set it on the coffee table.

She told herself it didn't matter.

Old albums were like that. Unfinished projects. Things people meant to get back to.

She spent the rest of the day sorting through boxes, calling movers, making lists.

By the time she stopped, it was past midnight, and she was too tired to drive back to the city.

She made up the bed in the guest room and fell asleep almost immediately.

She woke at 3 a.m. for no reason she could name.

The apartment was silent.

But something felt different.

She lay still for a moment, listening.

Nothing.

She almost went back to sleep.

Then she remembered the album, sitting on the coffee table in the next room, and she didn't know why, but she got up to check on it.

She didn't turn on the light. The streetlamp outside was enough.

The album was exactly where she'd left it.

She almost turned around.

Instead, she picked it up and opened it.

Page one. The couch photograph.

Page two. The birthday candles.

Page three. The garden.

Page four.

There was a photograph on page four.

Naina stood completely still, staring at it.

It hadn't been there hours ago. She was certain of that. She had counted the blank pages herself.

The photograph showed her, perhaps six years old, standing in the doorway of this apartment.

She was smiling at whoever was holding the camera.

Naina didn't remember it.

But that wasn't what made her hands go cold.

It was the shadow.

At the edge of the frame, just inside the door, there was a shape.

Tall. Still. Watching the little girl in the photograph.

Not her grandmother. The shadow was too tall, too broad.

Naina brought the album closer to the window light.

The shadow had no face.

Just a shape where a face should be.

She closed the album.

She told herself it was a trick of old film, a double exposure, something explainable.

She set it back on the table and returned to bed.

She didn't sleep again that night.
