# The Log Book

Every keeper before Idris had left a note in the log for whoever came next, and every note said some version of the same thing: *the light matters more than you do.* He'd laughed the first time he read it. He wasn't laughing three winters later, alone on the rock, watching a single ship's lights blink against a horizon that hadn't seen another vessel in eleven days.

The radio had gone quiet a week ago — not broken, just quiet, the way a house goes quiet when everyone in it has finally fallen asleep. He kept climbing the stairs anyway, kept trimming the lamp, kept writing in the log because a keeper who stops writing in the log is a keeper who has decided the coast doesn't need him anymore, and Idris was not ready to decide that.

Tonight the ship's lights hadn't moved in six hours. Not anchored — anchored ships still show a certain restlessness, the small correction of a hull against water. This one sat as still as a photograph.

He wrote it in the log, the way he wrote everything: *Unnamed vessel, bearing unchanged since 2100. No response to signal. Will investigate at first light if it hasn't moved.*

Then, because the lamp was due for trimming and the coast, whatever else might be true tonight, still needed its light, he climbed the stairs one more time.
