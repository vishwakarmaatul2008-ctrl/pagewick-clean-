# Chapter 2 — The Cost of Sovereignty

The metal floor of the cargo room was freezing, but the air between them was searing, suffocating heat.

Julian was on his knees, his massive frame hunched over Leo, his knuckles pale as he drove his weight down into the sterile gauze against his brother's shoulder. His white shirt was soaked in dark blood at the sleeve, his Kevlar vest straining against his chest with every ragged breath he took. The stoic, unreadable enforcer was gone, replaced by a man hanging on by the bare thread of his own restraint.

Aria dropped to her knees directly across from him. The space was dangerously tight; their knees brushed, her black sweater pressing against his side as she ripped open her trauma kit.

"Hold the pressure point, Julian," Aria commanded, her voice dropping into the steady, clinical register of a lead surgeon. "Don't slide your grip. I need to ligate the vessel."

Julian didn't speak. He shifted his weight, his large, calloused hands surrounding hers over the wound. His fingers brushed against her knuckles—rough, burning hot, and trembling with a microscopic, desperate tension.

"I've got him," Julian growled, his slate-gray eyes locking onto hers. They were inches apart. She could smell the metallic tang of blood, the sharp scent of gunpowder, and the raw heat radiating off his body. "Just keep him breathing."

"Look at me, Leo," Aria murmured, pulling a curved suture needle and heavy nylon thread from her pack.

Leo's eyelids fluttered open, his face pale beneath the harsh strip lights of the cargo bay. A weak, pain-racked smile touched his lips. "You patched me... in the rain," Leo rasped. "You're getting... a habit of this, Sterling."

"Then stop getting shot in front of me," Aria replied. Her hands were terrifyingly steady, but beneath her ribs, her heart was hammering a furious rhythm against her sternum.

She worked with swift, surgical precision, driving the needle through the soft tissue to clamp the torn vein. Julian stayed pinned to her side, his heavy shoulder bracing against hers, providing an anchor in the chaotic room. Every time she moved, the fabric of her sweater rubbed against his arm, sending a wave of electric friction straight down her spine.

"Done," Aria breathed, pulling the knot tight.

The heavy spurt of arterial blood immediately died down to a slow, manageable bleed. She layered a thick pressure dressing over the incision and wrapped a heavy elastic bandage around Leo's shoulder, pulling it taut.

"Bleeding is controlled," Aria said, her breath sweeping across Julian's jaw as she spoke. "He needs an OR and a unit of packed cells, but he isn't going to die tonight."

Julian let out a low, ragged exhale that sounded like a roar caught in his throat. He leaned down, resting his forehead against his brother's uninjured shoulder for three long seconds, his broad shoulders shaking with silent, violent relief.

When he looked back up, the vulnerable brother vanished. The cold, lethal authority of the Cassian syndicate head snapped back into place—but his eyes remained fixed on Aria.

"Captain," Julian called into his radio, his voice a low, gravelly baritone that vibrated through the steel floorboards. "Extract Leo to the private clinic on Fifth. Full tactical escort."

Two guards moved in, carefully transferring Leo onto a portable stretcher. As they lifted him, Leo reached out, catching the edge of Aria's sleeve with a weak grip.

"Thank you... sister-in-law," Leo whispered, his voice fading as the guards carried him down the gangway into the fog.

The title hung in the quiet, ruined room like heavy smoke.

Aria stood up slowly, her knees slightly weak as the adrenaline began to bleed off. She peeled off her blood-stained surgical gloves, tossing them aside.

Julian rose to his feet beside her. He towered over her, his six-foot-three frame cutting off the faint light from the doorway. He stepped closer—so close she had to tilt her head back to look into his eyes.

He didn't say a word. He reached into his pocket, pulled out a clean white handkerchief, and brought it to her face.

His touch was agonizingly slow. He held the back of her neck with one large, warm hand, his fingers buried in the dark hairs at the base of her skull, while his thumb gently wiped a smear of blood from her cheekbone. The contrast was maddening—a man who had just executed three men on the gangway holding her with a reverence that made her breath hitch in her throat.

"You didn't flinch," Julian whispered, his thumb lingering against the soft skin of her cheek, stroking the high curve of her bone until her pulse spiked violently beneath his fingers. "Not when the glass shattered. Not when his blood was on your hands."

"I told you in the boardroom, Cassian," Aria breathed, her hazel eyes burning into his slate-gray gaze. She reached up, placing her hand flat over his heavy chest, feeling the wild, hard thrum of his heart beneath his Kevlar vest. "I don't play games. I handle what's in front of me."

Julian's hand tightened subtly in her hair, pulling her a fraction of an inch closer until her breasts brushed the firm line of his chest. His gaze dropped to her lips, his dark brows drawing together as if fighting a war against his own restraint.

"Valenti's main force is retreating toward the northern warehouse," Julian growled low, his voice raspy with an overwhelming, dangerous heat. "He knows the shipment is ash, and he knows his chemical paper is in your hands."

"Then we finish it," Aria whispered, refusing to back down, her lips inches from his. "Where is he?"

A dark, dangerous spark lit deep within Julian's gray eyes—a look of pure, possessive claim. He slid his hand from her hair down to the small of her back, pressing her firmly against his side as he turned toward the door.

"It's time you took your place at the top of this city, Mrs. Cassian."
