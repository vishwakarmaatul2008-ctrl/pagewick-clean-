# Chapter 1 — Counter-Offensive

The rain had stopped by 4:00 AM, leaving the estate wrapped in a suffocating, freezing fog.

In the study, two guards hauled Marcus out, leaving behind the heavy, metallic stench of spent powder and damp wool. Julian stood behind the walnut desk, stripped of his suit jacket. His white dress shirt was unbuttoned three buttons down, the sleeves rolled past his elbows, exposing the dark syndicate ink tracing down his forearms.

Aria stepped up to the edge of the desk. She had traded her crimson silk dress for a fitted black ribbed sweater that hugged every line of her torso, and dark trousers. Her hair was pulled back into a sleek, dark knot, leaving her throat bare.

Julian didn't look up immediately, but his body registered her presence instantly—his jaw tightening, his chest expanding as he took in a slow, controlled breath.

"His perimeter is open," Aria said. Her voice was low, smooth, cutting through the silence. She leaned over the walnut surface to look at the glowing grid, intentionally placing her hand inches from his.

Julian's slate-gray eyes flicked sideways to her fingers, then tracked up the pale length of her arm to her eyes. The space between them shrank until the heat radiating from his broad frame washed over her skin.

"Valenti committed forty percent of his local muscle to the breach," Julian said. His voice was a raspy, gravelly baritone, deeper than usual, rough from smoke and something far more lethal. "He expects me to spend the next twelve hours in defensive triage."

"Which gives us an eight-hour blind spot," she murmured, turning her head toward him.

They were standing so close she could see the subtle pulse jumping beneath the dark ink on his jawline, the fine stubble lining his square chin, and the dark, dangerous storm brewing in his eyes. He smelled of rain, cedar, and raw adrenaline.

"If a trauma patient is hemorrhaging, you don't wait for the vitals to drop before you operate," Aria continued, her hazel eyes holding his without a single flinch. "You seal the artery while the pressure is still fluctuating."

Julian didn't answer with words. He took one slow, deliberate step around the corner of the desk, closing the remaining distance between them until her back pressed lightly against the edge of the dark wood.

He didn't touch her, but his height and massive frame completely trapped her in his shadow. The sheer physical presence of him was overwhelming—a wall of muscle, heat, and absolute authority.

"You're lecturing me on timing, Miss Sterling?" Julian whispered, leaning down slightly until his lips were bare inches from the shell of her ear. The heat of his breath sent a sharp, involuntary shiver down her spine.

"I'm giving you a surgical opinion, Mr. Cassian," she shot back, her pulse spiking against her collarbone—a rapid, fluttering rhythm she knew he could see. "Unless you're hesitant."

Julian let out a quiet, dark rumble in his chest that was almost a growl. He reached out, his broad hand coming up to rest flat against the desk right beside her hip, his knuckles brushing the soft knit of her sweater.

"I don't hesitate," Julian murmured, his gaze dropping to her mouth for a long, agonizing second before rising back to meet her eyes. The sexual tension in the room was so thick it felt physically heavy, pulling at the air in her lungs. "At dawn, Valenti's primary shipment docks at Pier 14. Thirty-two crore worth of unrefined narcotics and arms. The exact figure of your father's debt."

"You're going to destroy it," she breathed.

"I'm going to burn it to the ground," Julian corrected softly. He lifted his other hand, his calloused fingers coming up to cup the side of her neck. His skin was burning warm against her cool skin. His thumb rested directly over her throbbing pulse point, feeling the wild, rapid beat beneath her surface composure.

He didn't pull her in, and he didn't kiss her. He held her right on the razor-thin edge of restraint, letting the agonizing pull between them build until her chest heaved against his.

"And I need someone with steady hands to read the manifests before his men dump the evidence," Julian added, his thumb lightly stroking the smooth skin behind her ear, sending a deep, electric ache straight through her lower stomach. "Are your hands still steady, Aria?"

Aria reached up, her cool fingers wrapping around his wrist—feeling the dense bone and heavy muscle beneath his pulse point. She didn't push him away. She pulled him a fraction of an inch closer.

"My hands don't shake, Julian," she whispered, her voice laced with a lethal, breathless confidence. "My father's name gets cleared completely. No secondary papers."

Julian stared down at her, his slate-gray eyes darkening into complete, possessive shadow. The urge to crush her mouth beneath his was practically vibrating through his entire body, but he pulled back just an inch, maintaining the agonizing slow burn.

"Your father's name is already clean," Julian growled softly, his hand lingering on her neck before sliding slowly down her shoulder, his touch leaving a trail of fire. "Now get in the car."
