# Chapter 3 — Burning the Bridges

The thermite charges detonated with a low, gut-wrenching thrum that shook the harbor concrete beneath their feet.

Behind them, Valenti's thirty-two-crore vessel erupted into a roaring wall of fire, bright orange flames devouring the steel hull and casting long, dancing shadows across the slick dock. The evidence of the organophosphate poisonings, the illegal arms, and the forged debt notes dissolved into smoke and ash.

Aria sat in the passenger seat of Julian's armored SUV as he tore onto the coastal highway, the orange glare of the fire fading in the rearview mirror. The interior of the vehicle was silent, heavy, and thick with a suffocating, unspoken heat.

"Where are we going?" Aria asked, her voice steady, though her lungs felt tight. She turned to look at him in the dim dashboard light.

"Valenti's private penthouse at the Ironwood Tower," Julian said, his voice a low, gravelly baritone. His hands gripped the leather wheel with white-knuckled intensity. The sleeves of his dark shirt were still pushed up, revealing the heavy veins and dark syndicate ink on his forearms. "He's clearing his liquid accounts to flee the country. The council will turn on him the second the harbor fire hits the news."

"He won't go quietly," Aria said, her hazel eyes tracking the sharp profile of his jaw, the subtle twitch of muscle near his ear. "A cornered predator burns his own lair just to leave a scar."

Julian pulled the heavy vehicle into a private, subterranean parking bay beneath the Ironwood Tower. The garage was empty, illuminated only by the cold, flickering hum of fluorescent lights against dark concrete pillars. He shifted into park, but he didn't turn off the engine.

Silence descended over the car—dense, electric, and terrifyingly intimate.

"Stay in the vehicle, Aria," Julian said softly. He didn't look at her immediately; his gaze was fixed on the concrete wall ahead, his chest rising and falling in heavy, controlled breaths. "The tactical team is securing the lobby. I'm going up alone."

Aria unbuckled her seatbelt with a sharp click, turning her entire body to face him in the tight space. "You're still bleeding under that vest, Julian. You took a blunt kinetic hit three days ago, and you just spent two hours clearing a cargo ship. If Valenti has heavy security on the seventy-second floor—"

"Aria," Julian interrupted.

He turned his head slowly, his slate-gray eyes locking onto hers with a dark, terrifying intensity that silenced the air in her throat.

He unbuckled his own belt and leaned across the center console, invading her space until his massive frame completely blocked out the dim garage light. He reached out, his long, calloused fingers wrapping firmly around the back of her neck, pulling her forward until their foreheads almost touched.

The physical heat radiating off him was overwhelming—the scent of rain, gunpowder, and pure, unfiltered adrenaline filling her senses.

"I have spent three years watching you from the shadows," Julian whispered, his baritone dropping into a raspy, agonizing register that vibrated straight through her chest. His thumb pressed against the hollow beneath her ear, tracking the furious, wild rhythm of her pulse. "Three years waiting for the right moment to pull your family out of the mud without destroying you in the process."

Aria's breath hitched, her fingers instinctively coming up to clutch the dark fabric over his chest, feeling the rock-hard muscle and the frantic thrum of his own heart beneath her palm.

"I am not dying in a penthouse lobby," Julian growled softly, his lips bare millimeters from hers, his breath scorching hot against her mouth. "Not when I finally have you standing beside me."

The raw, possessive confession hung between them, thick with an agonizing, delayed friction that made her stomach tighten with a deep, liquid ache. Her hazel eyes searched his slate-gray gaze, refusing to pull away, her lips parting on a quiet, ragged breath.

"Ten minutes, Cassian," Aria whispered, her voice laced with a lethal, breathless authority, her hand tightening over his chest. "If you aren't back down here in ten minutes, I'm coming up there with a surgical kit and a sidearm."

A dark, dangerous shadow of a smile touched the corner of Julian's lips. His gaze dropped to her mouth, lingering there for one agonizing, breathless second where she thought he was going to crush his lips against hers—before he pulled back just an inch, his hand sliding slowly down her neck, leaving a searing trail of warmth in its wake.

"Yes, Mrs. Cassian," he murmured softly.

He pulled his sidearm from his shoulder holster, racked the slide with a smooth, deadly motion, and stepped out into the shadowed garage. The heavy door of the SUV clicked shut behind him as he vanished toward the private elevator bank.

Aria sat alone in the quiet car, her hand still resting over her own chest where her pulse was hammering wild and fast.

06:12 AM.

06:15 AM.

She checked her field pack, her fingers steadying as the clinical focus pushed back the frantic heat humming in her veins.

At 06:21 AM, two muffled, heavy cracks of gunfire echoed down through the central ventilation shaft of the parking garage, followed by a sudden, absolute silence.

Aria's hand instantly clamped onto the interior door handle.

Ten seconds later, the reinforced doors of the elevator lobby swung open.

Julian emerged into the cold light. His dark jacket was gone, his white dress shirt torn at the shoulder and stained with grease, but his stride was unbroken, commanding, and absolute. He carried a heavy leather briefcase in his left hand—Valenti's master ledger.

He walked straight to the SUV, pulled open the driver's side door, and slid inside. He tossed the briefcase into the backseat, turned, and met her eyes.

"Valenti?" she asked softly, her gaze scanning his chest and arms for fresh injuries.

"Valenti is dead," Julian said flatly, his baritone carrying the unyielding weight of an executioner. "The debt is gone. The city is clean."

He reached across the console, his large, warm hand covering hers on her lap. His fingers laced through hers, squeezing tight, possessive, and unshakeable.

"Now," Julian whispered, his slate-gray eyes burning into hers with a quiet, dangerous promise, "we take the throne."
