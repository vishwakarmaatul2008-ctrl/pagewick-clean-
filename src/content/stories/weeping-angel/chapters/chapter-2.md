# Chapter 2 — The Rule

"Don't turn around."

Aria stood completely still.

Every muscle in her body tightened.

The voice had been quiet.

Almost gentle.

But there was something wrong with it.

It sounded as though the words had been spoken directly into her ear, yet she could feel no breath against her skin.

She stared straight ahead.

The cemetery stretched before her beneath the fading evening light.

Rows of graves.

The old chapel.

The rusted iron gate.

Nothing moved.

Behind her, the breathing continued.

Slow.

In.

Out.

In.

Out.

Aria swallowed.

"Who's there?"

The breathing stopped.

For several seconds, there was complete silence.

Then a whisper came again.

"Don't."

Aria's hands began to tremble.

She wanted to turn.

Every instinct told her to turn around and look.

But she remembered the statue.

She remembered Joseph.

And she remembered the warning carved beneath its feet.

SHE CRIES FOR THE ONES WHO LOOK AWAY.

Aria slowly closed her eyes.

Maybe someone was playing a joke.

Maybe Joseph had hired someone to frighten her.

Maybe she was exhausted.

There had to be an explanation.

There always was.

She took one step forward.

Nothing happened.

Another.

Still nothing.

The breathing had disappeared.

Aria opened her eyes.

She didn't look back.

She walked quickly toward the caretaker's office.

Once she reached the door, she turned around.

The cemetery was empty.

The statue stood beneath the banyan tree.

Exactly where it had been.

Aria released the breath she had been holding.

"Ridiculous."

She unlocked the office and stepped inside.

Then she stopped.

There was a folded piece of paper on the desk.

She hadn't seen it earlier.

Her name was written on the front.

ARIA.

She picked it up.

The handwriting was old and uneven.

She unfolded the paper.

There were only four lines.

Don't touch the angel.

Don't wipe her tears.

Don't stand behind her.

And if she moves---

The final sentence had been scratched out.

Aria stared at it.

Her heart began beating faster.

She turned the paper over.

Nothing.

Then she noticed something written very faintly at the bottom.

Never look away from her.

Aria frowned.

She read it again.

Never look away.

That didn't make sense.

If she was told not to look away, then why had the voice told her not to turn around?

She folded the paper and put it into her pocket.

---

That night, Aria stayed in the caretaker's office.

She didn't want to return to the apartment yet.

She told herself she was working late.

In reality, she didn't want to be alone.

At 9:40 PM, she called Joseph.

He answered after several rings.

"Hello?"

"Joseph."

A pause.

"Aria?"

"I need to ask you something."

His voice became quieter.

"What happened?"

She looked through the office window.

The cemetery was almost completely dark.

"Did my uncle ever tell you anything about the angel?"

Silence.

Then Joseph asked,

"Why?"

"Because I found a note."

Another silence.

"What note?"

Aria read it to him.

When she finished, Joseph didn't speak.

"Joseph?"

"Where did you find it?"

"On my desk."

"That is impossible."

"Why?"

"Because your uncle wrote that note."

Aria stared at the phone.

"He did?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"I don't know."

Joseph's breathing became unsteady.

"He gave it to me the week before he died."

"Then why was it on my desk?"

"I don't know."

Aria looked toward the window.

The angel was barely visible through the darkness.

"Joseph, what happened to my uncle?"

He didn't answer.

"Please."

Finally, he said,

"He broke the rule."

"What rule?"

"The one everyone forgets."

Aria waited.

Joseph whispered,

"Never let the angel see you turn away."

The call ended.

Aria slowly lowered the phone.

---

At midnight, she finally decided to go home.

She locked the office.

Then she stepped into the cemetery.

The path toward the gate was only a few minutes long.

She kept her flashlight pointed toward the ground.

She didn't look toward the statue.

She didn't want to.

But halfway to the gate, she heard it.

Sob.

Aria stopped.

Another.

Sob.

She closed her eyes.

The sound was coming from behind her.

From the angel.

She forced herself to keep walking.

Sob.

The crying became louder.

Aria walked faster.

Then she heard footsteps.

Behind her.

One step.

Then another.

She stopped.

The footsteps stopped too.

Aria's breathing became shallow.

She slowly raised her flashlight.

Her hand was shaking.

She pointed it toward the ground.

And there, beside her shoes, she saw something strange.

Water.

A thin trail of droplets stretched across the stone path.

It continued from behind her.

All the way from the direction of the statue.

Aria stared at it.

The droplets were fresh.

Still moving.

Still falling.

She slowly lifted her eyes.

The statue stood beneath the banyan tree.

But something was different.

The angel's hands were no longer completely covering her face.

A small portion of her mouth was visible.

And from between her stone fingers---

a tear rolled down.

It fell onto the ground.

Drip.

Aria couldn't move.

Another tear.

Drip.

Then the angel whispered.

Not from behind Aria.

Not from the trees.

Not from the cemetery.

The voice came from the statue itself.

"You looked away."

Aria's flashlight went out.

And somewhere in the darkness---

stone feet took one step toward her.
