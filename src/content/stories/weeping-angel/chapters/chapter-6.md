# Chapter 6 — What Her Mother Knew

Aria couldn't move.

The angel stood at the end of the study.

Its wings were folded tightly against its back.

Its hands covered most of its face.

Only one eye was visible between its fingers.

A tear slipped from the corner of that eye.

It rolled down the pale skin of its face.

Then disappeared beneath its chin.

Aria remembered what the angel had said.

"Ask your mother what happened here."

She swallowed.

"How do you know my mother?"

The angel didn't answer.

Instead, it slowly lowered its hands.

Aria's heart pounded.

For the first time, she saw the angel's face.

It wasn't monstrous.

That somehow made it worse.

The woman looked young.

Perhaps thirty.

Her features were delicate and almost peaceful.

But her eyes were filled with unbearable sadness.

She looked like someone who had been crying for years.

The angel stared at Aria.

Then whispered,

"She promised she would come back."

"Who?"

"Your mother."

Aria's breathing stopped.

The angel raised one hand.

A single tear fell from her fingertips.

"Ask her."

Then the lights came back on.

The angel was gone.

---

Aria ran.

She didn't stop until she reached her apartment.

She locked the door.

Then she locked it again.

Her hands were shaking so badly that she nearly dropped her phone.

She called her mother.

Once.

No answer.

Again.

Nothing.

Again.

Straight to voicemail.

Aria stared at the screen.

Her mother had never ignored her calls.

Never.

She called again.

This time, someone answered.

"Aria?"

Her mother's voice sounded tired.

"Mom."

"What happened?"

"Where were you?"

A pause.

"At home."

"Were you at St. Mary's Cemetery?"

Silence.

Aria closed her eyes.

Her mother finally whispered,

"Who told you about that place?"

"So you know it."

"Aria..."

"Why were you there?"

Another silence.

Then her mother said,

"You need to leave that apartment."

Aria's heart sank.

"Why?"

"Just leave."

"Mom, tell me the truth."

"I can't."

"You knew the angel."

Her mother stopped breathing.

Aria heard it.

A sharp intake of breath.

Then---

"Don't say that word."

"Why?"

"Because she listens."

Aria looked toward her bedroom.

The corner was dark.

Much darker than it should have been.

"Mom..."

Her voice became smaller.

"What happened to you?"

Her mother began crying.

"I thought I could protect you."

"From what?"

"I made a mistake."

"What mistake?"

"I brought her home."

Aria froze.

"What?"

"I thought she was a statue."

Her mother's voice trembled.

"She wasn't."

Aria couldn't speak.

Her mother continued.

"She was a woman."

The room suddenly felt colder.

"A woman who disappeared."

"Who?"

"I don't know her name."

"Then why does she know me?"

Her mother began sobbing.

"Because she was there when you were born."

Aria's eyes widened.

"What are you talking about?"

"I was twenty-three."

Her mother's voice broke.

"I had just had you."

"I was scared. Your father was gone. I didn't know what to do."

"So I went to St. Mary's."

Aria listened.

"I thought the cemetery was empty."

Her mother whispered,

"But she was standing there."

"Who?"

"The angel."

---

Aria sat down slowly.

Her mother continued.

"She was crying."

"Like the statue?"

"Yes."

"I asked her what was wrong."

"She said she had been waiting."

"For what?"

"For someone to remember her."

Aria's heart pounded.

"What happened next?"

"I touched her."

Her mother started crying again.

"And everything changed."

"What changed?"

"The angel stopped crying."

Aria stared at the dark corner of her room.

"Then what?"

"She looked at me."

"What did she say?"

Her mother's voice dropped to a whisper.

"She said my daughter would belong to her."

Aria felt the blood drain from her face.

"Mom..."

"I thought she was threatening me."

"So I ran."

"Then I heard you crying."

Aria's eyes filled with tears.

"You were at home?"

"No."

Her mother took a shaky breath.

"You were with me."

Aria stopped.

"What?"

"You were six months old."

Her mother whispered,

"And when I looked down..."

"I saw that you weren't in my arms anymore."

Aria couldn't breathe.

"Where was I?"

"At the cemetery."

"Beside the angel."

---

Neither of them spoke.

For several seconds, only the sound of Aria's mother's breathing could be heard.

Then Aria asked,

"How did I come back?"

"I made a deal."

"With the angel?"

"Yes."

"What did you give her?"

Her mother didn't answer.

"Mom."

"I gave her something she wanted."

"What?"

"My memories."

Aria frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"She said she would let you go if I forgot her."

Aria stared at the wall.

"So you forgot?"

"For a while."

"But you remembered."

"Yes."

"When?"

"Yesterday."

Aria's stomach tightened.

"Why yesterday?"

Her mother whispered,

"Because you moved into the apartment."

Aria looked toward the bedroom.

The dark corner seemed to have grown larger.

"Mom..."

Her voice trembled.

"What does my apartment have to do with her?"

Her mother didn't answer.

"Mom?"

Finally, she said,

"Your apartment was never an apartment."

Aria felt a cold sensation move through her body.

"What?"

"It used to be part of the cemetery."

Aria stared at the walls.

"No."

"The building was constructed over the old grounds."

"No..."

"The room you're sleeping in..."

Her mother's voice cracked.

"...was where they kept the angel before they moved her to the cemetery."

Aria slowly looked toward the corner.

The darkness seemed to breathe.

Her mother whispered one final sentence.

"Aria, whatever you do tonight..."

"Don't let her cry alone."

The call ended.

Aria lowered the phone.

For several seconds, she sat completely still.

Then she heard it.

A soft sound.

From the bedroom.

Sob.

Aria slowly turned toward the doorway.

Another sob.

Sob.

Then a woman's voice whispered from inside the dark room.

"Aria..."

She stood.

The voice continued.

"I remember you."

Aria's heart pounded.

She stepped toward the bedroom.

One step.

Then another.

The crying became louder.

She reached the doorway.

The room was empty.

Except for the corner.

And on the wall above it---

words were slowly appearing.

Not written in ink.

They were appearing through the moisture gathering on the paint.

One letter at a time.

YOU LEFT ME HERE.

Aria stared at the message.

Then she saw another line appear beneath it.

NOW REMEMBER.
