# Chapter 1 — The Statue

The statue had been crying for forty-three years.

Nobody knew when it had started.

Nobody knew who had carved it.

And nobody in the village was willing to touch it.

It stood at the edge of St. Mary's cemetery, beneath an enormous banyan tree whose roots had slowly swallowed the old stone wall.

The statue was made of pale grey stone.

A woman.

An angel.

Her wings were folded behind her back, almost completely covering them. Her head was bowed, and both hands covered her face.

But the most disturbing thing about her was not her expression.

It was the water.

Thin trails of water ran continuously from beneath her fingers.

They travelled down her face, over her cheeks, and disappeared beneath her chin.

Like tears.

People called her the Weeping Angel.

And everyone in the village knew one rule.

Never wipe away her tears.

---

Twenty-three-year-old Aria Sen had never believed in that rule.

She didn't believe in ghosts.

She didn't believe in curses.

And she certainly didn't believe that a stone statue could cry.

She believed in things that could be explained.

That was why she had accepted the job at St. Mary's Cemetery.

Her uncle had been the caretaker for almost thirty years.

After his death, the cemetery needed someone to maintain it.

The pay wasn't great.

The work was lonely.

But Aria needed the money.

On her first morning, the old caretaker who had temporarily been looking after the cemetery walked her around the grounds.

His name was Joseph.

He was seventy-two years old and had worked there since he was a teenager.

When they reached the eastern side of the cemetery, he stopped.

Aria followed his gaze.

She saw the statue.

She stared at it.

"That's the angel?"

Joseph nodded.

Aria stepped closer.

The statue was taller than she expected.

Nearly seven feet.

Its stone wings were enormous.

The face was hidden behind its hands.

Water glistened beneath its fingers.

Aria frowned.

"There's actually water coming from it."

Joseph didn't answer.

She looked at him.

"Is there a pipe inside?"

"No."

"Some kind of drainage problem?"

"No."

"Then where does it come from?"

Joseph looked at the statue.

"I don't know."

Aria smiled.

"You've worked here for fifty years."

"Fifty-five."

"And you don't know?"

Joseph's expression remained serious.

"No."

He stepped backward.

"Don't touch it."

Aria looked at him.

"Why?"

Joseph didn't answer immediately.

Then he said quietly,

"Because your uncle did."

Aria's smile disappeared.

"My uncle?"

Joseph nodded.

"Three days before he died."

The wind moved through the banyan tree.

A few dry leaves fell between them.

Aria looked back at the statue.

"What happened?"

Joseph stared at the angel.

"He wiped its face."

"And?"

Joseph's eyes moved toward her.

"He stopped coming home."

Aria frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"He stayed here."

"Here?"

Joseph nodded.

"Every night."

Aria waited.

"Why?"

Joseph lowered his voice.

"He said someone was standing behind him."

Aria looked around the cemetery.

There was nobody there.

"Who?"

Joseph shook his head.

"He never saw them."

A pause.

"He only heard them crying."

---

That afternoon, Joseph left.

Aria remained behind.

She spent several hours cleaning the cemetery, cutting weeds and removing fallen branches.

By sunset, she was exhausted.

She sat on the stone bench near the caretaker's office and drank water.

The cemetery became strangely quiet after dark.

No birds.

No traffic.

Only the occasional rustling of leaves.

Aria looked toward the eastern side.

The angel was barely visible beneath the tree.

She stood.

Something caught her attention.

The statue seemed different.

She walked closer.

The water was still running down its face.

But now there were two trails of wetness beneath its hands.

One on each cheek.

Aria stared.

She raised her phone and took a photograph.

The flash illuminated the statue.

For a moment, she thought she saw something between the angel's fingers.

An eye.

Open.

Looking directly at her.

Aria lowered the phone.

Her heart jumped.

She stared at the statue.

Its hands were still covering its face.

She laughed nervously.

"I'm tired."

She turned away.

Then she heard it.

A sound behind her.

A quiet sob.

Aria froze.

She slowly turned around.

The angel hadn't moved.

Of course it hadn't.

It was stone.

She looked at her phone.

The photograph was still open.

She zoomed in.

Her smile disappeared.

Between the angel's fingers---

there was no eye.

Only darkness.

Aria exhaled.

"Okay."

She put the phone away.

Then she noticed something else.

A small inscription had been carved into the stone beneath the angel's feet.

She had never seen it before.

She crouched down.

The letters were old and almost worn away.

But she could still read them.

SHE CRIES FOR THE ONES WHO LOOK AWAY.

Aria stared at the words.

Behind her---

the angel made a sound.

Not a sob.

Not a whisper.

Something much softer.

Almost like stone scraping against stone.

Aria slowly stood.

She didn't turn around.

Because suddenly, she remembered what Joseph had told her.

He said someone was standing behind him.

And then Aria heard breathing.

Directly behind her.

She closed her eyes.

The breathing continued.

Slow.

Patient.

Waiting.

Then a woman's voice whispered beside her ear:

"Don't turn around."
