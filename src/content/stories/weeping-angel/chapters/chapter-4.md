# Chapter 4 — The Woman Who Looked Away

Aria didn't move.

She stood at the cemetery gate, staring at the words carved into the stone.

SHE IS ALREADY OUTSIDE.

The wet footprints continued beyond the gate.

Across the pavement.

Toward the road.

Aria's phone trembled in her hand.

The screen still showed the photograph she had taken.

She looked at it again.

The figure behind her was gone.

Only the cemetery remained.

Empty.

Silent.

The statue was nowhere in sight.

Aria slowly stepped through the gate.

The moment her foot touched the pavement outside, the crying stopped.

Completely.

She turned toward the cemetery.

The gate stood open behind her.

The banyan tree moved gently in the wind.

And beneath it---

the angel was back.

Aria froze.

She hadn't seen it return.

It stood exactly where it had been when she first arrived.

Head bowed.

Hands covering its face.

Wings folded.

Crying.

As if nothing had happened.

Aria stared at it from across the cemetery.

Then something fell from between its hands.

A small piece of white paper.

It landed at the base of the statue.

Aria didn't go back for it.

She ran home.

---

She didn't sleep.

At 4:12 AM, Aria sat on the edge of her bed with every light switched on.

She had locked the door twice.

Then checked it again.

Her phone was beside her.

She kept staring at the photograph.

The figure behind her was still there.

Sometimes she thought she could make out its face.

Sometimes she thought she could see long hair.

Sometimes---

she thought it looked like her.

At 5:03 AM, someone knocked on her door.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Aria stopped breathing.

She didn't move.

Another knock.

Knock.

She looked at the clock.

5:04 AM.

"Who is it?"

No answer.

Aria slowly approached the door.

She looked through the peephole.

The hallway was empty.

She stepped back.

Then she heard a whisper from the other side.

"Aria."

She covered her mouth.

It was the same voice.

The voice from the cemetery.

She didn't open the door.

The whisper came again.

"You forgot something."

Aria's eyes moved toward the floor.

Something had been pushed beneath the door.

A piece of paper.

She crouched down.

Her hands shook as she picked it up.

The paper was wet.

She unfolded it.

There was only one sentence.

THE FIRST WOMAN LOOKED AWAY.

---

At 7 AM, Aria called Joseph.

He answered immediately.

"You saw her."

It wasn't a question.

Aria swallowed.

"I saw the angel."

Joseph was silent.

"It moved."

"No."

"I watched it move."

"No," Joseph repeated. "You watched it appear somewhere else."

Aria frowned.

"What difference does that make?"

"A very big one."

She sat down.

"Tell me the truth."

Joseph breathed slowly.

"You want the truth?"

"Yes."

"Then come to my house."

---

Joseph lived six streets away in a small, old house surrounded by overgrown plants.

When Aria arrived, he was waiting at the door.

He looked exhausted.

He didn't invite her inside immediately.

Instead, he asked,

"Did you look behind you?"

Aria shook her head.

"No."

"Good."

"Why?"

Joseph looked toward the street.

"Because once you see her face, she can see yours."

Aria felt a chill.

"Who is she?"

Joseph finally opened the door.

"Come inside."

His living room was filled with old photographs.

Family photographs.

Photographs of the cemetery.

Photographs of the chapel.

And photographs of the angel.

Hundreds of them.

Aria walked slowly around the room.

Some photographs were decades old.

Others looked recent.

The angel appeared in every one.

Always crying.

Always with her face covered.

Aria picked up one photograph.

It showed three people standing beside the statue.

A young man.

An elderly woman.

And a little girl.

On the back was a date.

June 17, 1981.

Aria looked at Joseph.

"Who are they?"

"My parents."

She looked at the little girl.

"You?"

Joseph nodded.

"And that's my sister."

"What happened to her?"

Joseph didn't answer.

Aria looked again.

The little girl was staring at the camera.

Her sister wasn't.

She was looking directly at the angel.

Aria turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

SHE LOOKED AWAY FIRST.

Aria's stomach tightened.

"What does that mean?"

Joseph took the photograph from her.

"My sister disappeared that night."

"How?"

"We don't know."

"Did she die?"

Joseph looked at the angel's photograph.

"No body was ever found."

Aria remembered the woman behind her.

The woman who had called herself Aria.

She whispered,

"Was she the first?"

Joseph looked at her.

"No."

His voice became almost inaudible.

"She was the forty-third."

Aria stared at him.

"Forty-third what?"

Joseph didn't answer.

Instead, he walked to an old wooden cabinet and unlocked it.

Inside were dozens of small objects.

Buttons.

Necklaces.

Rings.

Hair clips.

Photographs.

Personal belongings.

Each item had a date attached to it.

Aria recognized the silver button.

It was there.

Among the others.

She pointed at it.

"That was in my bedroom."

Joseph closed his eyes.

"It shouldn't have been."

"Why?"

"Because those belong to the people who disappeared."

Aria stepped backward.

The room suddenly felt too small.

She looked at Joseph.

"Then why did it come to me?"

Joseph's face had gone pale.

"Because the angel has chosen you."

Aria shook her head.

"No."

Joseph looked toward the window.

Outside, the morning sunlight had begun fading beneath thick clouds.

"That's what happened to your uncle."

Aria went silent.

"My uncle was chosen?"

Joseph nodded.

"And what happened to him?"

Joseph looked directly into her eyes.

"He came home one morning."

Aria waited.

"He was crying."

"Why?"

Joseph's voice broke.

"Because he had finally seen her face."

A long silence followed.

Then Aria asked the question she had been afraid to ask.

"What happens when you see her?"

Joseph looked toward the old photographs.

"You stop being afraid of the angel."

"Why?"

"Because you realize..."

He swallowed.

"...the angel isn't crying for you."

A sound came from upstairs.

Sob.

Aria and Joseph both froze.

Another sob.

Sob.

Joseph slowly looked toward the ceiling.

His face drained of colour.

Aria whispered,

"What is that?"

Joseph didn't answer.

The crying came again.

This time, much louder.

And then---

a woman's voice whispered from upstairs.

"Joseph..."

He closed his eyes.

Aria stared at him.

"You told me you lived alone."

Joseph didn't move.

The voice came again.

"Joseph..."

Then a second voice joined it.

Aria's voice.

"Please..."

Joseph grabbed Aria's arm.

"Don't look upstairs."

Aria stared at the staircase.

The crying continued.

Joseph whispered,

"Whatever you hear..."

He tightened his grip.

"...do not look away from me."
