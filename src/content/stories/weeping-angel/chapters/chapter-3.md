# Chapter 3 — The Footsteps

Aria couldn't see anything.

The cemetery had disappeared into darkness.

Her flashlight was dead.

She pressed the power button again.

Nothing.

Again.

Nothing.

Then she heard it.

Step.

A slow, heavy sound against the stone path.

Aria's breath caught in her throat.

She didn't move.

Step.

Closer.

She could hear the faint scrape of something dragging against the ground.

She wanted to run.

Every part of her body screamed at her to run.

But the note came back to her.

Never let the angel see you turn away.

Aria stared into the darkness ahead.

She couldn't see the statue.

She couldn't see anything.

But she could hear it.

Step.

She closed her eyes.

"No," she whispered.

Another step.

Step.

This time it sounded much closer.

Aria opened her eyes.

Nothing.

The cemetery was silent.

She slowly reached into her pocket and pulled out her phone.

The screen lit up.

1:03 AM.

She turned on the flashlight.

The beam flickered.

For a moment, it showed the path.

Empty.

Aria exhaled.

Then the light moved slightly to the left.

And she saw something.

A footprint.

Wet.

Bare.

On the stone path.

Aria stared at it.

Another wet footprint appeared several feet ahead.

She watched.

Nothing touched the ground.

Yet another footprint slowly formed.

Step.

Aria stumbled backward.

The next footprint appeared.

Closer.

Then another.

Closer.

The invisible footsteps were walking toward her.

Aria turned.

She ran.

---

She didn't look back.

Branches scratched her arms as she pushed through the cemetery.

Gravestones blurred past her.

Her shoes slipped on wet grass.

Behind her---

Step.

Step.

Step.

The footsteps followed.

Not fast.

Never fast.

They didn't need to be.

Aria reached the main path.

The cemetery gate was visible ahead.

She could see the street outside.

She was almost there.

Then the footsteps stopped.

Aria stopped too.

She looked toward the gate.

Nothing.

She slowly turned her head.

The cemetery was empty.

The statue was still beneath the banyan tree.

Seven feet tall.

Hands covering its face.

Wings folded.

Perfectly still.

Aria stared at it.

Then she noticed something.

The angel was facing the wrong direction.

When she had first seen it, its body had been angled toward the chapel.

Now it was facing the path.

Facing her.

Aria's stomach tightened.

She blinked.

The statue hadn't moved.

It couldn't have.

Stone couldn't move.

She looked away for only a second.

When she looked back---

the angel was closer.

Not much.

Perhaps ten feet.

Aria's mouth went dry.

She stepped backward.

The angel didn't move.

She looked at her phone.

The screen was still lit.

1:04 AM.

She raised the phone and took a photograph.

The flash exploded across the cemetery.

For one instant, everything became white.

Then darkness returned.

Aria looked at the photograph.

Her hands began shaking.

The statue was standing exactly where it had been.

But there was something else in the picture.

A figure.

Behind her.

Tall.

Thin.

Its face was hidden.

Aria slowly lowered the phone.

She didn't turn around.

She couldn't.

The voice came again.

Right beside her ear.

"Don't look."

Aria squeezed her eyes shut.

"Who are you?"

The voice whispered:

"Someone who looked."

A cold tear rolled down Aria's cheek.

She wiped it away.

Then she realized something.

The tear wasn't hers.

She touched her face.

Her fingers were wet.

Behind her, someone began crying.

Not the statue.

Not the voice.

A woman.

A real woman.

She was sobbing directly behind Aria.

"Please," the woman whispered.

Aria's eyes filled with tears.

"Please help me."

Aria wanted to turn.

She wanted to help.

But the voice beside her ear whispered again.

"Don't."

The crying became louder.

"Please..."

Aria clenched her fists.

"Who are you?"

The woman answered.

"My name is Aria."

Aria's blood went cold.

She stared at the cemetery gate.

The crying woman behind her continued.

"My name is Aria."

Then another voice came from the statue.

A voice that sounded exactly like hers.

"And you're standing in my place."

Aria opened her eyes.

The statue was no longer beneath the banyan tree.

The space beneath the tree was empty.

She looked toward the path.

Nothing.

She looked toward the gate.

Nothing.

Then she noticed the wet footprints.

They weren't behind her anymore.

They were in front of her.

A trail of footprints leading out of the cemetery.

And beside the first footprint was a single line scratched into the stone.

SHE IS ALREADY OUTSIDE.
