# Chapter 8 — The Last Tear

The door stood in the darkness.

YOUR TURN NOW.

Aria stared at it.

The little girl sat on the floor behind her, silently crying.

Aria knew that girl.

She remembered her now.

The frightened child.

The child who had followed her mother into the cemetery.

The child who had touched the angel.

The child who had been forgotten.

Aria turned toward her.

"What happens if I open it?"

The girl shook her head.

"You already did."

Aria looked at the door again.

It was slightly open.

A thin line of darkness separated it from the frame.

Something was crying on the other side.

Aria stepped forward.

The little girl grabbed her hand.

"Don't leave me again."

Aria looked down.

"I won't."

She pushed the door open.

---

There was no room behind it.

There was only darkness.

And within that darkness stood dozens of people.

Men.

Women.

Children.

All completely still.

All with their heads lowered.

All crying.

Aria recognized some of them from the photographs.

The forty-three missing people.

They weren't dead.

They weren't alive either.

They were trapped somewhere between the two.

At the center stood the angel.

Her wings stretched across the darkness.

Her face was uncovered.

And she looked exactly like Aria.

Except older.

Much older.

Tears covered her face.

Aria stepped closer.

"Why do you look like me?"

The angel smiled sadly.

"Because you were always going to become me."

Aria shook her head.

"No."

"You saw me."

"I was a child."

"You touched me."

"My mother made a deal."

The angel nodded.

"She did."

"What did she give you?"

"Not her memories."

Aria stared at her.

"Then what?"

The angel looked toward the little girl.

"She gave me your fear."

Aria felt a chill.

"My fear?"

"Every time you were afraid, I felt it."

The angel's voice trembled.

"Every nightmare. Every lonely night. Every time you cried and nobody came."

Aria couldn't speak.

The angel continued.

"I became the place where you put everything you couldn't carry."

Aria looked at the people standing behind her.

"Then why did they disappear?"

"They looked at me."

"And?"

"They gave me their fear too."

Aria finally understood.

The angel wasn't feeding on bodies.

She was feeding on something much deeper.

Fear.

Grief.

Loneliness.

Everything people tried to hide.

"But why do you cry?"

The angel looked at her.

"Because nobody ever leaves me with anything else."

A tear fell.

Then another.

Aria stepped forward.

"What do you want from me?"

The angel whispered,

"Nothing."

Aria frowned.

"Then why bring me here?"

"I didn't."

The angel pointed behind Aria.

Aria turned.

Her mother was standing in the doorway.

She was crying.

"Mom?"

Her mother stepped forward.

"I was wrong."

Aria stared at her.

"I thought I could protect you by making you forget."

She looked at the angel.

"But every memory I buried became hers."

Aria understood.

The deal had never freed her.

It had only moved the pain somewhere else.

Her mother walked toward the angel.

"Take me instead."

The angel shook her head.

"You already gave me what you had."

"Then take the rest."

"No."

"Why?"

The angel looked at Aria.

"Because she came back."

Aria's mother began crying harder.

"Aria, run."

Aria didn't move.

She looked at the angel.

Then at the forty-three people behind her.

Then at the little girl.

For years, everyone had been trying to escape the angel.

Her mother had run.

Her uncle had run.

The others had run.

And every time someone looked away---

the angel moved.

Aria finally understood the rule.

It wasn't about keeping your eyes on the statue.

It was about refusing to abandon what frightened you.

She stepped toward the angel.

"I won't run."

The angel's expression changed.

"You should."

"No."

"I'm afraid."

"I know."

"I'll make you remember everything."

Aria's eyes filled with tears.

"Then I'll remember."

She reached out.

The angel didn't move.

Aria touched her hand.

Cold.

Then---

the crying stopped.

Every voice in the darkness disappeared.

The forty-three people lifted their heads.

The angel's wings began to crumble.

Not like stone.

Like ash.

Pieces of darkness fell from them and disappeared before touching the ground.

The angel looked at Aria.

For the first time, she wasn't crying.

"Thank you."

Then she vanished.

---

Aria woke beneath the banyan tree.

Morning sunlight touched her face.

The cemetery was silent.

Joseph was sitting beside her.

Alive.

He looked exhausted.

Aria sat up.

"Where is she?"

Joseph looked toward the statue.

The Weeping Angel was still there.

But something had changed.

The stone was completely dry.

No tears.

No water.

Nothing.

Joseph whispered,

"It's over."

Aria looked at the statue.

For the first time, its face seemed peaceful.

She stood.

Then she noticed something lying at the base of the statue.

A small silver button.

She picked it up.

It was warm.

---

Three months later, Aria moved out of the apartment.

She never returned to St. Mary's.

Her mother never spoke about the angel again.

Joseph retired.

The cemetery was eventually closed.

The statue was removed.

At least, that was what the village believed.

Aria tried to forget everything.

She almost succeeded.

Until one rainy evening.

She was unpacking boxes in her new apartment when she noticed something strange.

There was a small dark corner beside her bedroom wardrobe.

She stared at it.

Nothing happened.

She smiled nervously.

"It's just a shadow."

Then she heard it.

A quiet sob.

Aria froze.

She looked at the clock.

2:17 AM.

Another sob.

She slowly walked toward the corner.

The darkness remained still.

Then something appeared on the wall.

Letters formed through the moisture.

YOU REMEMBERED ME.

Aria stepped closer.

A second sentence appeared.

SO I CAN FINALLY REST.

The crying stopped.

Aria stood there for a long time.

Then she noticed one final line appearing beneath the others.

Her heart stopped.

BUT SOMEONE ELSE IS STILL CRYING.

From somewhere inside the wall---

a child whispered her name.

"Aria..."

She closed her eyes.

And smiled sadly.

Because this time---

she wasn't afraid to look.

The End.
