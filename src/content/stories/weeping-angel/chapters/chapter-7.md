# Chapter 7 — The Memory

The words on the wall remained for several seconds.

YOU LEFT ME HERE.

Then the moisture beneath them began to move.

The letters slowly disappeared.

A second message appeared.

NOW REMEMBER.

Aria stood in the doorway, unable to breathe.

She wanted to leave.

She wanted to call her mother again.

She wanted to wake up and discover that none of this had happened.

But something inside her knew that the messages were not asking her to understand.

They were asking her to remember.

Aria stepped into the bedroom.

The temperature dropped immediately.

Her breath became visible.

She looked toward the corner.

It was completely dark.

Not shadow.

Darkness.

A small sound came from inside it.

A child crying.

Aria froze.

The crying was familiar.

Too familiar.

She had heard it before.

In old recordings.

In her mother's stories.

In the memories she couldn't quite remember.

A baby crying.

Her baby crying.

Aria's hand moved toward her mouth.

"No..."

The crying continued.

Then she heard another sound.

A lullaby.

Very quiet.

Her mother's voice.

Aria knew the song.

She hadn't heard it since childhood.

She closed her eyes.

And suddenly---

she remembered.

---

She was six years old.

She was standing in the cemetery.

Her mother's hand was wrapped tightly around hers.

It was raining.

The angel stood beneath the banyan tree.

But it wasn't a statue.

It was a woman.

A woman with enormous wings.

She was crying.

Young Aria remembered being afraid.

"Mommy?"

Her mother had knelt beside her.

"Don't look at her face."

"But she's sad."

"I know."

"Why is she crying?"

Her mother hadn't answered.

Instead, she had pulled Aria closer.

The angel slowly raised her head.

Her face was covered by wet hair.

She whispered,

"Come here."

Aria's mother stepped backward.

"No."

The angel's crying became louder.

"You promised."

"I changed my mind."

"You promised me her."

Aria remembered her mother's hand shaking.

Then the angel looked at her.

Not at her mother.

At her.

And Aria remembered something she had completely forgotten.

She had walked toward the angel willingly.

She had reached out.

She had touched her hand.

The world had gone silent.

---

Aria opened her eyes.

She was back in the apartment.

Her heart was pounding.

She looked at the corner.

The crying had stopped.

Then someone spoke behind her.

"You remember."

Aria slowly turned.

Her mother stood in the bedroom doorway.

Aria stared at her.

"You came here."

Her mother nodded.

"When?"

"Years ago."

"No."

Aria shook her head.

"You came here today."

Her mother looked confused.

"I didn't."

"You're lying."

"I swear I'm not."

Aria pointed toward the corner.

"You brought me here when I was six."

Her mother went pale.

"How do you know that?"

"I remember."

Her mother covered her mouth.

"Oh God."

Aria stepped closer.

"You made a deal."

"Yes."

"You gave her your memories."

"Yes."

"And you promised her me."

Her mother began crying.

"Yes."

"Why?"

Her mother looked toward the corner.

"Because she was going to take you anyway."

Aria stopped.

"What?"

"She doesn't take people because she wants them."

Her mother's voice trembled.

"She takes them because they look at her."

Aria didn't understand.

"She was never supposed to be seen."

"What happens when someone sees her?"

Her mother looked at her.

"They become part of her."

A cold silence filled the room.

Aria whispered,

"Then why am I still here?"

Her mother didn't answer.

The corner behind them became darker.

Her mother slowly turned.

Aria grabbed her arm.

"Don't."

Her mother froze.

The crying began again.

But this time it wasn't coming from the corner.

It was coming from the hallway.

Then the kitchen.

Then the living room.

Multiple voices.

Children.

Women.

Men.

All crying at once.

Her mother whispered,

"She has opened the house."

Aria stared at her.

"What does that mean?"

"She isn't inside the corner anymore."

The lights flickered.

Her mother grabbed Aria's hand.

"We have to leave."

They ran toward the front door.

Aria pulled the handle.

It wouldn't open.

Her mother tried.

Nothing.

She pulled harder.

The door didn't move.

Then they heard footsteps behind them.

Slow.

Bare.

Wet.

Step.

Step.

Step.

Aria closed her eyes.

Her mother whispered,

"Don't turn around."

The footsteps stopped.

Aria opened her eyes.

The hallway mirror was directly in front of them.

She saw herself.

She saw her mother.

And behind them---

the angel.

Standing in the hallway.

Her wings were open.

Her hands covered her face.

Aria stared at the reflection.

The angel didn't move.

Her mother grabbed Aria's face.

"Don't look at her."

But Aria couldn't look away.

The angel slowly lowered her hands.

Her face appeared in the mirror.

And Aria finally understood why everyone had been afraid to see it.

The angel's face wasn't frightening.

It was familiar.

It was Aria's face.

Older.

Tired.

Covered in tears.

The angel looked at her through the mirror.

Then whispered:

"You didn't leave me here."

A tear rolled down her cheek.

"I left you here."

The mirror cracked.

One line.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

Aria screamed.

The apartment disappeared.

And for a moment, she was standing somewhere else.

A dark room.

A little girl was sitting on the floor.

Crying.

Aria looked at her.

The girl raised her head.

It was six-year-old Aria.

She stared at the older Aria.

Then whispered:

"She never wanted your mother."

Aria stepped closer.

"Then what did she want?"

The little girl pointed behind her.

Aria slowly turned.

The angel was standing there.

Crying.

And behind the angel was a door.

A door Aria had never seen before.

On it were three words:

YOUR TURN NOW.
