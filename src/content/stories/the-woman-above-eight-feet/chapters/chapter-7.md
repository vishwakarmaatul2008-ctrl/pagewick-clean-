# Chapter 7 – The Last Encounter

Ren couldn't move.

His grandmother stood in the doorway.

Behind her was something impossibly tall.

Its head nearly touched the ceiling.

Ren stared at his grandmother.

Then at the woman behind her.

Then back at his grandmother.

"Grandma..."

She shook her head.

"Don't trust what you see."

The tall figure placed one hand on her shoulder.

His grandmother's face remained completely still.

Then she whispered:

"Run."

Ren didn't hesitate.

He grabbed the photograph and ran toward the staircase.

Behind him, something laughed.

Po... po... po...

He reached the front door.

Locked.

He pulled the handle again.

Nothing.

His grandmother's voice came from behind him.

"Ren!"

He turned.

She was standing halfway down the stairs.

Alone.

The tall woman was gone.

"Grandma?"

"Come here!"

Ren took one step toward her.

Then stopped.

Something was wrong.

Her feet weren't touching the stairs.

She was floating.

Ren stepped backward.

The woman appeared behind him.

Her voice was almost gentle.

"You finally learned."

Ren closed his eyes.

He remembered the rules.

Don't look at her.

Don't answer her.

Don't let her know you're alone.

But there was one rule nobody had told him.

He opened his eyes.

The photograph was still in his hand.

His grandfather had written something on the back that Ren hadn't noticed.

He turned it over.

SHE CANNOT TAKE SOMEONE WHO KNOWS THE TRUTH.

Ren looked toward the woman.

For the first time, he wasn't afraid.

"You aren't Hachishakusama."

The woman's smile disappeared.

"You were never the ghost."

Silence.

"You were the door."

The house trembled.

The walls began cracking.

The woman slowly raised her hand.

Behind her, the staircase seemed to stretch endlessly downward.

Ren understood.

The legend wasn't about a woman who took people.

It was about a woman who led people somewhere.

And his grandfather had discovered where.

Ren held up the photograph.

"You've been using this house to bring people here."

The woman's expression changed.

For the first time...

she looked afraid.

Ren stepped toward the front door.

The lock clicked.

He pulled it open.

Sunlight flooded the hallway.

The woman screamed.

Not loudly.

Not like a monster.

Like a person who had been trapped for a very long time.

Ren ran outside.

Behind him, the house shook violently.

His grandmother followed.

They reached the road.

The old shopkeeper was waiting there.

He looked at Ren.

Then at the house.

"It's over."

Ren breathed heavily.

"What happens now?"

The old man looked at the photograph.

"Now we burn it."

He struck a match.

The photograph caught fire.

The image of the house disappeared first.

Then the villagers.

Then Ren.

But the woman remained.

She stared from the burning paper.

Her expression was calm again.

Then the photograph turned completely black.

The house stopped shaking.

Everything became silent.

---

Three days later, Ren left the village.

He never returned.

His grandmother moved to the city.

The old house was eventually demolished.

Nobody ever found a basement beneath it.

Nobody found Ren's grandfather.

And nobody ever saw the tall woman again.

At least...

that's what Ren believed.

Until one year later.

Ren was cleaning his room when his phone received a notification.

Unknown number.

One photograph.

He opened it.

His hands went cold.

It was a picture of him sitting at his desk.

Taken from outside his bedroom window.

Ren slowly looked toward the window.

Nothing.

Then another message arrived.

Three words.

Po. Po. Po.

Ren dropped the phone.

His screen went black.

For several seconds, there was silence.

Then---

Tok.

A knock came from the window.

Ren didn't move.

Tok.

Another knock.

He slowly looked toward the glass.

A shadow stood outside.

It was impossibly tall.

Ren closed his eyes.

Then he heard a familiar voice.

His grandfather.

"Ren."

A pause.

"Don't look outside."

Ren's eyes opened.

The voice continued:

"Because this time..."

"...she isn't outside."

Ren looked down.

A shadow was standing beneath his chair.

Po... po... po...

And somewhere behind him...

something smiled.

THE END.
