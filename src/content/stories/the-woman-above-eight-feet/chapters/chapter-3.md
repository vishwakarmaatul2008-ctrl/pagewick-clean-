# Chapter 3 – Don't Answer

The knocking continued.

Tok.

Ren sat perfectly still.

His grandmother stood beside the bedroom door, one hand gripping the photograph.

Tok.

The sound came from the window again.

Ren's eyes stayed fixed on the floor.

"Grandma..."

"Don't speak loudly."

"But what is she?"

His grandmother's voice trembled.

"I don't know."

Another knock.

Tok.

Then silence.

Ren waited.

Five seconds.

Ten.

Nothing.

He finally looked at his grandmother.

"Is she gone?"

She shook her head.

"No."

"How do you know?"

His grandmother pointed toward the window.

"The rain stopped."

Ren listened.

She was right.

The rain had suddenly stopped.

But something else had started.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Heavy.

Step.

A pause.

Step.

Another pause.

They weren't coming from the road.

They were coming from the side of the house.

Ren felt his heartbeat in his throat.

His grandmother pulled him away from the window.

"Listen carefully."

She opened the wooden box again and removed a small piece of paper.

Written on it were three rules.

DO NOT LOOK AT HER.

DO NOT ANSWER HER.

DO NOT LET HER KNOW YOU ARE ALONE.

Ren stared at the last line.

"What happens if I break them?"

His grandmother folded the paper.

"People disappear."

"Disappear where?"

"We don't know."

"Then how do you know they're dead?"

She looked at him.

"We don't."

A cold silence filled the room.

Ren suddenly understood why everyone in the village spoke about the woman as if she were still alive.

They had never found the bodies.

---

The footsteps stopped.

Right outside the house.

Ren held his breath.

His grandmother slowly moved toward the bedroom door.

Then came a voice.

A woman's voice.

Soft.

Almost playful.

"Po..."

Ren's grandmother immediately covered his mouth.

The voice came again.

"Po... po..."

Then it changed.

It sounded closer.

Almost directly outside the bedroom.

"Ren."

His eyes widened.

His grandmother's hand tightened over his mouth.

The woman knew his name.

"Ren..."

The voice sounded exactly like his grandmother.

"Open the door."

Ren looked at his grandmother.

She was standing right beside him.

Her face had gone pale.

The voice outside continued.

"Ren, it's okay."

His grandmother shook her head violently.

Don't answer.

Ren nodded.

Then the woman whispered:

"I'm cold."

Silence.

"I'm lonely."

Another pause.

Then---

"Please."

Ren felt tears forming in his eyes.

The voice sounded so human.

So helpless.

He almost spoke.

Almost.

His grandmother squeezed his shoulder.

Then something slid underneath the bedroom door.

A photograph.

Ren stared at it.

His grandmother slowly picked it up.

It was the same photograph from earlier.

The old photograph of the villagers.

Except something had changed.

Ren leaned closer.

There was now one more person in the picture.

A boy.

Standing beside the villagers.

Ren recognized him immediately.

It was him.

Same clothes.

Same face.

Same expression.

His grandmother dropped the photograph.

Ren whispered:

"That's impossible."

From outside the door came a quiet laugh.

Then the voice said:

"You're already in the picture."

Ren stopped breathing.

The door handle slowly began to turn.

Click.

His grandmother grabbed his hand.

"Run."

"Where?"

"Anywhere."

The handle turned again.

Click.

His grandmother pulled him toward the back door.

But before they could reach it---

A shadow appeared beneath the bedroom door.

It was impossibly tall.

The shadow stretched across the floor...

Across the wall...

And nearly reached the ceiling.

Ren looked up.

For the first time, he understood why the villagers never described her face.

They were too afraid to look that high.
