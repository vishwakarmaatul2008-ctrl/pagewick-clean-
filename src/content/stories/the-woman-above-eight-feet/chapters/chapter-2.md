# Chapter 2 – The Woman

Ren didn't sleep that night.

He stayed in the living room with his grandmother until sunrise, wrapped in a blanket while she sat silently beside the window.

Neither of them spoke about what had happened.

When morning finally came, the village looked completely ordinary.

Birds sang.

A farmer drove a small truck down the road.

A woman swept the pavement outside her house.

Ren almost convinced himself that the previous night had been a dream.

Almost.

At breakfast, he finally asked.

"Grandma... who was outside?"

His grandmother placed her cup down.

"You heard her?"

"Yes."

Her expression tightened.

"What exactly did you hear?"

Ren hesitated.

"Po... po... po..."

The cup slipped slightly from her fingers.

She stared at him.

"Don't say it again."

"Why?"

"Because you're not supposed to imitate her."

Ren laughed nervously.

"Who is she?"

His grandmother looked toward the window.

Then she locked the curtains.

"There are stories about a woman who visits this village."

"Ghost stories?"

"Call them whatever you want."

She lowered her voice.

"People say she is impossibly tall."

Ren smiled.

"How tall?"

His grandmother didn't.

"Eight feet."

The smile disappeared from his face.

"Eight feet?"

"Some say taller."

Ren shook his head.

"That's impossible."

His grandmother looked directly at him.

"That's what everyone says the first time."

---

That afternoon, Ren went into the village.

He needed groceries, but secretly he wanted to ask someone about the woman.

The village had only one small convenience store.

An elderly man stood behind the counter.

Ren bought some drinks and casually asked:

"Excuse me. Do people around here have any stories about a tall woman?"

The man's expression changed immediately.

"Who told you about her?"

"My grandmother."

The man stopped moving.

"You're staying at her house?"

"Yes."

He looked toward the shop window.

Then he lowered his voice.

"You should leave."

Ren laughed.

"Everyone keeps saying strange things."

"I'm serious."

The man leaned closer.

"Her name is Hachishakusama."

Ren had heard the name before.

"Eight-foot woman?"

The man nodded.

"She doesn't come for everyone."

"Then who does she come for?"

The old man didn't answer.

Instead, he asked:

"Did she see you?"

Ren thought about the previous night.

"I don't know."

The man stared at him.

"Then pray that she didn't."

---

Ren returned home before sunset.

His grandmother was waiting at the entrance.

"You went into the village."

"Yes."

"Did you speak to anyone?"

"Just the shopkeeper."

She closed her eyes.

"What did he tell you?"

Ren didn't answer.

His grandmother already knew.

That night, she placed a small wooden box beside Ren's bed.

Inside was an old photograph.

It showed a group of villagers standing outside the house decades ago.

Ren looked carefully.

At first, nothing seemed strange.

Then he noticed someone standing behind them.

A woman.

She was impossibly tall.

Her head rose above everyone else.

But the strangest part wasn't her height.

It was her face.

She was smiling.

Ren looked closer.

The photograph was old and damaged.

Yet the woman's face was perfectly clear.

His grandmother whispered:

"Your grandfather took that photograph."

Ren looked at her.

"Where is she now?"

His grandmother didn't respond.

Ren turned back toward the photograph.

The woman's smile suddenly seemed different.

Almost wider.

Then he noticed something he'd missed before.

The woman wasn't standing behind the villagers.

She was standing behind the house.

Exactly where Ren's bedroom window was now.

His breathing stopped.

He slowly looked toward the window.

The curtains were closed.

He didn't move.

Then---

Tok.

A knock came from the glass.

Ren's blood ran cold.

His grandmother grabbed the photograph from his hands.

"Don't look."

Another knock.

Tok.

Then a third.

Tok.

His grandmother whispered:

"Whatever happens..."

The knocking stopped.

"...do not open the curtains."

Ren stared at them.

And from outside came the sound again.

Po... po... po...

This time...

It was much closer.
