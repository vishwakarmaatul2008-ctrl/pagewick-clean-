# Chapter 4: The Last Thing She Remembered

Nandini’s body jerked.

Sana pulled Arjun away as Nandini’s fingers curled against the dusty floor. Her breathing became shallow and uneven. The dark veins spreading from her bite had reached her shoulder.

“Mum?” Arjun whispered.

Nandini turned toward his voice.

For a second, her eyes softened.

“Arjun,” she murmured. “My little boy…”

He tried to crawl toward her, but Priya held him back, tears streaming down her face.

“Let me go! She's my mother!”

“I know, sweetheart,” Priya whispered. “I know.”

Nandini struggled to sit up. Her eyes moved across the group, stopping at Aarav.

“The photograph,” she said.

Aarav leaned closer. “What photograph?”

“Phoenix Mall… the security room.”

Her voice cracked. She pressed a trembling hand against her chest.

“The man they dragged inside… he was carrying something. A small black case. The guard took it from him.”

Aarav remembered the bloodied stranger being pulled through the staff entrance. He remembered the guard looking toward their group.

“You saw us there,” Aarav said. “Did you see what happened to the case?”

Nandini nodded weakly.

“They opened it. There were photographs… names… and a map.”

“Of what?” Kabir asked.

“The city.”

Her expression changed. For a moment, fear replaced the confusion in her eyes.

“Then the alarms started. Everyone ran. The guard said the outbreak had begun earlier than expected.”

Silence filled the workshop.

Dev shook his head. “Earlier than expected? What does that even mean?”

“Maybe the outbreak wasn't as sudden as we thought,” Meera replied.

Imran crouched beside Nandini.

“Did you hear where they were taking the case?”

She swallowed.

“Railway yard.”

Priya looked at the message from her brother again.

The old railway yard wasn't simply another dangerous location. It was where the people collecting survivors were taking them—and where the black case might have ended up.

Before anyone could ask another question, Nandini's breathing stopped.

“Nandini?” Priya touched her shoulder.

Nothing.

Then Nandini's eyes opened.

They were cloudy and unfocused.

She lunged.

Imran grabbed her arm before she could reach Priya. Nandini snapped her teeth at him, making a horrible, animal-like sound.

“Get back!” he shouted.

Kabir pulled Priya away. Sana covered Arjun's eyes, but the boy fought her grip.

“MUM!”

Nandini twisted violently, knocking Imran against a sewing machine. Aarav and Dev rushed forward, helping him restrain her without getting too close to her mouth.

For one terrible moment, Nandini looked directly at Aarav.

He could almost recognize the frightened woman who had begged them to protect her son.

Then she snapped at his face.

Aarav stumbled backward.

Imran looked at the group, his expression hardening.

“We can't keep her here.”

Nobody answered.

They all understood what he meant.

Priya was crying openly now. Meera held her shoulders while Kabir stood between Arjun and his mother.

Aarav stared at the floor.

They had promised Nandini they would protect her child. They had promised they wouldn't abandon her.

But the woman who had made them those promises was gone.

Imran found a length of rope among the workshop supplies and used it to secure Nandini to a heavy metal support. It would not hold her forever.

“We have to move,” he said quietly. “Those men will search every building.”

Arjun slipped out of Sana's arms.

He walked toward his mother.

“I'm sorry, Mum,” he whispered.

Nandini thrashed against the rope.

Arjun began to cry.

Sana lifted him and carried him toward the back exit. This time, he didn't fight her.

Before leaving, Aarav looked at Nandini one last time.

“I won't let them take him,” he promised.

They escaped through the workshop's rear door into a narrow lane.

The city beyond was waking beneath a grey sky. Smoke rose between the buildings, and distant sirens echoed through the streets. Somewhere nearby, infected people were screaming.

Imran led them toward a small service road that ran beside the railway tracks.

“We're not going to the yard,” Kabir said.

“No,” Imran replied. “Not through the main entrance. If your friend’s mother was telling the truth, the place is already compromised.”

“Then where do we go?” Meera asked.

Imran pointed toward a cluster of abandoned railway buildings.

“There’s an old signal cabin overlooking the maintenance yard. We can see what they're doing without walking straight into their hands.”

Priya wiped her face.

“And my brother?”

“If he's alive, that may be our best chance of finding him.”

They continued in silence.

Arjun walked beside Sana, holding her hand. Every few steps, he looked back toward the workshop.

Aarav stayed near the front, replaying Nandini's words.

The black case. The photographs. The map.

The outbreak had begun earlier than expected.

Someone had known something was coming.

And somehow, six ordinary friends who had simply gone shopping at Phoenix Mall had become part of it.

As they approached the signal cabin, a burst of static came from Imran's radio.

Then a familiar voice spoke.

“Attention, recovery teams. The Phoenix Mall survivors are still unaccounted for. The child must be recovered alive.”

A pause followed.

Then another voice answered.

“What about the others?”

The reply came without hesitation.

“Bring them in if possible. If not, eliminate them before they reach the records.”

Imran switched off the radio.

Nobody spoke.

Across the railway yard, several transport trucks were parked beside a long industrial building. Armed guards patrolled the fences. Civilians stood in lines beneath floodlights, even though daylight had begun to spread across the city.

And on the roof of the largest building stood a man wearing a white identification band.

He was holding a photograph.

Even from a distance, Aarav could recognize the faces in it.

Six young friends.

Taken inside Phoenix Mall, minutes before the outbreak began.

The man lowered the photograph and spoke into his radio.

“They've arrived.”

Then he smiled.

“Begin the second phase.”
