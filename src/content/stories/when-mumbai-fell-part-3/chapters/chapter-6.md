# Chapter 6: The Last Light

The railway tracks stretched ahead beneath a sky stained with smoke.

Aarav ran until his lungs burned. Behind him, the others struggled to keep pace. Gunshots echoed from the industrial building, followed by the distant wail of sirens.

Priya kept looking back.

“Sameer is still in there,” she said. “We can't leave him.”

Imran caught her arm before she could turn around.

“He stayed because he wanted us to expose the truth. If we go back now, we'll all be captured.”

“He's my brother!”

“And you're his reason to keep fighting,” Imran replied. “Don't let his sacrifice mean nothing.”

Priya pulled away, tears running down her face, but she continued forward.

They reached an abandoned railway carriage beside a broken signal post. Dev forced open its door, and the group climbed inside to catch their breath.

Arjun sat beside Sana, clutching the small backpack she had carried for him. He hadn't spoken since seeing the child trapped in the transport truck.

Aarav placed Sameer's folder on the seat.

“We have one name,” he said. “Dr. Vikram Sethi.”

Kabir opened the folder again.

“There's an address here. An evacuation facility outside the city. If Sethi is in charge, that's where we'll find him.”

“Or where they'll lock us up,” Meera said.

“Either way, it's where the answers are.”

Rishi looked through the carriage window. “We also need food, water, and somewhere to hide. We can't keep running forever.”

For once, nobody argued.

They had spent days searching for safety. Every shelter had become another danger, every stranger another question. They had lost Nandini, and now Sameer was trapped behind enemy lines.

They couldn't save everyone.

But they could still try to save those who remained.

---

An hour later, the group reached a small railway station on the outskirts of the industrial district.

The platform was deserted except for a few abandoned bags and a broken vending machine. A notice board hung crookedly above the entrance.

EMERGENCY EVACUATION SERVICES SUSPENDED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE.

Someone had written beneath it in red paint:

THEY ARE NOT COMING TO SAVE YOU.

Aarav stared at the words.

“Someone wanted people to know.”

“Or someone wanted people to panic,” Dev replied.

Imran checked the road beyond the station.

“We need transport. Walking to the facility would take too long.”

They searched the station master's office and found a half-empty bottle of water, a torch, and a radio with a damaged antenna. Kabir managed to repair the antenna using a piece of wire.

Static filled the room.

Then a voice emerged.

“—all remaining civilian groups are instructed to report to designated evacuation points. Repeat, all remaining civilian groups—”

The message cut out.

A second voice followed, quieter and hurried.

“If anyone can hear this, do not approach the white-band transport teams. They are transferring people to the eastern quarantine facility. The facility is not a hospital. I repeat, it is not a hospital.”

A burst of static swallowed the rest.

Meera leaned closer to the radio.

“Who was that?”

“Someone who escaped,” Imran said.

The transmission returned for a few seconds.

“Dr. Sethi knows about the original incident. The records are inside the control room. If the files are destroyed, no one will ever know what happened.”

Then the signal died.

Aarav looked at the others.

“The control room. That's where we need to go.”

Priya nodded. “And we find a way to get my brother out.”

“And the people in those trucks,” Sana added.

Kabir gave a tired laugh without humour.

“We're six ordinary people with barely enough supplies to survive the day. We're not an army.”

“No,” Aarav said. “But we know what they're doing now. And we know they're counting on everyone being too frightened to question them.”

He picked up the folder.

“We don't have to defeat an army. We just have to get the truth out.”

---

By late afternoon, they found an abandoned delivery van behind a roadside warehouse. Its windows were cracked, but the engine started after Dev and Imran worked on it for nearly twenty minutes.

They drove along the outskirts of Mumbai, avoiding the main roads. Burned vehicles blocked several intersections. In one neighbourhood, smoke rose from a row of shops. In another, dozens of infected wandered aimlessly around a deserted bus depot.

The city they knew was disappearing behind them.

Arjun eventually fell asleep against Sana's shoulder.

Priya sat beside him, staring out of the window.

“Do you think Nandini knew what would happen to her?” she asked quietly.

Aarav looked at the road ahead.

“I think she knew she was losing control. She was frightened, but she still tried to protect her son.”

“She asked us to keep him safe.”

“And we will.”

He glanced at Arjun.

For the first time since the outbreak, Aarav understood that survival couldn't simply mean staying alive. If that were all they cared about, they could abandon everyone else and keep running.

But every time they chose to help someone, they risked losing another person.

There were no easy answers anymore.

Only choices.

---

As the sun began to set, the facility appeared beyond a tall concrete wall.

Floodlights illuminated watchtowers. Armed guards patrolled the gates. Several transport trucks stood in a line outside the entrance, waiting to unload their passengers.

A large sign hung above the gate.

MUMBAI EMERGENCY QUARANTINE AND RECOVERY CENTRE.

From the road, it almost looked legitimate.

Almost.

Aarav noticed the windows first. Every building had reinforced glass and metal shutters. Cameras watched the road, the gate, and the surrounding fields.

Then he noticed the people behind the fence.

They were not walking freely. They stood in crowded enclosures, watched by armed guards.

A woman pressed both hands against the wire barrier.

“Please!” she shouted as the van passed. “Tell my husband I'm here!”

A guard pulled her away.

Sana turned her face from the window.

“They're prisoners.”

Imran drove past the main entrance and turned onto a narrow service road. He parked behind a cluster of trees, far enough away that the guards couldn't easily see the van.

“That's as close as we go,” he said.

The group climbed out and crouched behind the trees.

Aarav unfolded the map from Sameer's folder.

A small building near the rear of the facility was marked ADMINISTRATION AND CONTROL.

Beside it, someone had written a note.

DR. VIKRAM SETHI — COMMAND OFFICE.

“We get inside, find the records, and get out,” Aarav said. “No unnecessary fights. No splitting up.”

Kabir looked toward the watchtowers.

“And if we can't get out?”

Aarav had no answer.

Then the loudspeakers crackled.

“Attention, recovery personnel. Priority subjects have been sighted approaching the outer perimeter.”

The group went still.

A guard stepped out of the nearest watchtower and looked directly toward the trees.

A second guard raised a radio.

“Confirm identities.”

A voice answered through the loudspeaker.

“Six Phoenix Mall survivors. One child. Recover all seven alive if possible.”

Priya grabbed Arjun's hand.

“They know we're here.”

“No,” Imran whispered, staring at the gate. “They knew you were coming.”

The floodlights swung toward the trees.

Aarav pushed the folder into Meera's hands.

“Run for the drainage ditch!”

They scattered as a siren erupted across the facility.

Gunfire tore through the branches above them. Dev and Rishi pulled Arjun toward the ditch while Sana stayed beside Priya. Kabir stumbled down the muddy slope, and Aarav reached back to help him.

They slid into the shallow drainage channel just as armed men reached the trees.

“Search the area!” a guard shouted. “The doctor wants them alive!”

The group crawled through the ditch until they reached a concrete culvert beneath the perimeter road.

Inside, they finally stopped.

Their clothes were soaked. Their hands were shaking. Arjun was crying silently against Sana.

Aarav listened to the boots passing overhead.

They had reached the facility, but they hadn't even crossed its outer fence.

Then a small speaker inside the damaged radio crackled.

A voice came through, calm and unmistakable.

“Hello, Aarav.”

He froze.

The voice continued.

“I know you can hear me. I have been waiting a long time to meet you.”

A pause.

“My name is Dr. Vikram Sethi. And if you want to understand why Mumbai fell, you need to hear what happened before the first infected person entered Phoenix Mall.”

Aarav looked at his friends.

Above them, the searchlights swept across the road.

Sethi spoke one final sentence.

“Because the outbreak you survived was never supposed to happen in public.”

The radio went silent.

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Then Aarav tightened his grip on the folder.

They had spent days searching for the truth.

Now the man who might hold every answer was waiting on the other side of the wall.

And they had only one night to reach him.
