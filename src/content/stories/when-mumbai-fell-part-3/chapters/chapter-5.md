# Chapter 5: The Truth Beneath the Yard

The six friends watched the railway yard from the broken windows of the signal cabin.

Below them, armed men moved between transport trucks. Hundreds of civilians stood behind metal barriers. Some were injured. Others looked exhausted, their clothes stained with blood and dust.

Every few minutes, guards called out names from a clipboard.

Those whose names were called were taken inside the industrial building.

Those whose names were missing were pushed toward a separate line.

Nobody returned.

“That's not a shelter,” Meera whispered.

“No,” Imran replied. “It's a sorting centre.”

Priya pressed her hands against the window.

“My brother was working here. If he discovered what they were doing, he might still be inside that building.”

Kabir studied the fences. “We can't just walk in. There are too many guards.”

“We don't have to walk through the front,” Imran said. “There's an old maintenance tunnel beneath the tracks. It connects to a drainage passage behind the signal office.”

Dev stared at him.

“You know an awful lot about this place.”

Imran didn't answer immediately.

Then he reached into his jacket and pulled out a folded photograph.

It showed him standing beside a younger man in railway work clothes. The younger man had a familiar face.

Priya snatched the photograph.

“That's my brother!”

“He helped me escape from the warehouse three days ago,” Imran explained. “We were both looking for a way to expose what was happening. When the guards discovered us, we separated. He told me to find you if I could.”

“Why didn't you tell me earlier?”

“Because I didn't know whether you were being followed. And because I wasn't sure who I could trust.”

Priya's anger faded into fear.

“Did he escape?”

“I saw him being taken toward the railway yard. That's the last time I saw him.”

For a moment, Priya closed her eyes.

Then she folded the photograph carefully and slipped it into her pocket.

“We're going in.”

Aarav looked at the others.

Nobody seemed happy about the idea, but nobody argued.

They had spent days running from the infected, hiding from strangers, and trusting rumours because they had no other choice. Now they finally had a chance to learn why people were hunting them.

They couldn't walk away.

---

The maintenance tunnel smelled of stagnant water and rust.

Imran led the way, using a small torch. The others followed in single file. Sana held Arjun's hand while Aarav kept looking behind them, expecting to see someone following.

The tunnel ended at a metal hatch.

Imran pushed it open carefully.

They emerged inside a disused equipment room beneath the industrial building.

Voices echoed through the ceiling.

“Transfer begins in ten minutes.”

“Priority subjects first.”

“Keep the child separate.”

Sana immediately pulled Arjun closer.

Aarav exchanged a glance with Kabir.

They had found the right place.

Through a narrow gap in the door, they could see a corridor guarded by two armed men. Beyond them stood several locked rooms.

A woman inside one room was banging on the door.

“Please! My daughter is in the other room! You said we would stay together!”

A guard walked past without looking at her.

Meera covered her mouth.

“They're separating families.”

“Some of them might be infected,” Dev whispered.

“Then why are they taking healthy people too?” Rishi asked.

Nobody had an answer.

Imran pointed toward a staircase at the end of the corridor.

“The records office should be upstairs. If we find your brother's notes, we'll know what they're planning.”

They waited until the guards turned away, then slipped into the corridor.

They had almost reached the staircase when a door opened behind them.

“Hey! You!”

A guard spotted them.

“Stop!”

Imran pushed the others toward the stairs.

They ran.

Boots thundered behind them. A gunshot struck the wall, scattering plaster across the steps. Aarav stumbled, but Kabir grabbed his arm and pulled him onward.

At the top, they burst into a room filled with filing cabinets, radio equipment, and computer monitors.

A man in railway work clothes stood beside a desk.

Priya froze.

“Bhai?”

The man turned.

For a moment, neither of them moved.

Then Priya ran into his arms.

Her brother, Sameer, held her tightly.

“I thought I'd never see you again,” she sobbed.

“I thought they'd caught you too.”

Sameer looked over her shoulder at the others. When he saw Aarav, his expression changed.

“You're the Phoenix Mall group.”

Aarav nodded. “You know us?”

Sameer crossed to the desk and opened a drawer. Inside lay a black folder filled with photographs, maps, and printed reports.

“I found your names in their files. They were tracking you before the first infected person appeared at the mall.”

He spread six photographs across the desk.

Each showed one of the friends.

Aarav's photograph had been taken outside a college building. Kabir's showed him buying food at a roadside stall. Dev's came from a security camera. Rishi, Meera, and Sana had been photographed in different parts of Mumbai.

They weren't random pictures.

Someone had been watching all six of them.

“Why us?” Sana asked.

Sameer shook his head.

“I don't know everything. But I found a report about the man brought into Phoenix Mall that morning. He was carrying research documents connected to an experimental infection. The authorities were already searching for him.”

“And the outbreak?” Meera asked.

“The documents say the infection was spreading before the public was warned. Someone tried to contain the information. When the first cases reached the mall, the emergency response failed.”

“Failed?” Dev repeated. “Or was it allowed to fail?”

Sameer looked at him.

“That's the question I couldn't answer.”

He opened another file.

Inside was a photograph of the black case Nandini had described. Beside it was a list headed:

PHOENIX INCIDENT — WITNESSES TO BE RECOVERED

The six friends' names appeared beneath it.

Beside each name was a note.

POTENTIAL WITNESS. LOCATION UNKNOWN.

Aarav felt cold.

“We saw the man being dragged into the staff corridor,” he said. “We saw the guard take him away.”

“Exactly,” Sameer replied. “Someone believes you saw something important. They don't know how much you remember, so they're trying to recover all six of you.”

“Why take Arjun?” Priya asked.

Sameer turned to another page.

His face went pale.

“Because his mother was listed as a witness too.”

Everyone fell silent.

Nandini had been involved before they met her. She had seen the black case, heard the guards, and understood that something had gone wrong.

And now the people hunting them wanted her son.

A sudden alarm blared through the building.

A voice rang out over the loudspeakers.

“Security breach in the records office. All units converge immediately.”

Sameer grabbed the folder and shoved it into Aarav's hands.

“Listen carefully. The records aren't the only thing here. They're preparing to move the survivors before sunset.”

“Where?” Kabir asked.

Sameer pointed to a map on the wall.

A red circle marked a location beyond the industrial zone.

“An evacuation centre outside Mumbai. They're calling it a quarantine facility, but I found a message from someone who worked there.”

“What did it say?” Meera asked.

Sameer swallowed.

“No one sent there has been allowed to leave.”

The office door shook under a heavy blow.

Imran raised his pistol.

Sameer pulled a key from his pocket and opened a narrow door behind the cabinets.

“There's a service bridge leading to the eastern platform. You can escape through the railway tracks.”

Priya grabbed his arm.

“Come with us!”

“I can't. If I leave, they'll move everyone before anyone can help them.”

“We're not leaving you here,” she said.

Sameer looked at his sister, then at the others.

For the first time, he seemed uncertain.

Then Arjun stepped forward.

“Please help them,” the boy whispered. “I don't want anyone else to disappear.”

Sameer's expression softened.

He knelt in front of Arjun.

“I promise I'll try.”

The door began to splinter.

Sameer stood and pressed a small radio into Aarav's hand.

“If you reach the evacuation centre, find the control room. The person in charge knows why you were selected.”

“Who is it?” Aarav asked.

Sameer hesitated.

Then he spoke a name.

“Dr. Vikram Sethi.”

The door burst open.

Armed men flooded the office.

Sameer shoved Priya through the hidden doorway and followed the others onto the service bridge. Behind them, gunfire erupted inside the records room.

Priya screamed her brother's name, but Imran dragged her forward.

They raced along the narrow bridge as the morning sun rose over the railway yard.

At the far end, Aarav looked back.

Through the windows of the industrial building, he saw guards forcing civilians into transport trucks.

One truck carried a sign on its side:

QUARANTINE TRANSFER — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY.

The truck doors slammed shut.

And just before the vehicle pulled away, Aarav saw a small hand pressed against the rear window.

A child was inside.

Arjun stared at it, trembling.

“They're taking children too,” Sana whispered.

Aarav tightened his grip on the folder.

They finally knew why they were being hunted.

But the truth was worse than any of them had imagined.

The outbreak had turned Mumbai into a graveyard.

And someone was using the chaos to decide who deserved to survive.
