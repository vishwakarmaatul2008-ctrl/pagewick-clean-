# Chapter 3: The List of the Missing

The pharmacy shutter shook violently.

“Open up!” a man shouted from outside. “We know you’re in there. We’re here to take you somewhere safe.”

The middle-aged man holding the pistol didn’t move.

“Everyone away from the entrance,” he whispered.

Aarav pulled Arjun behind a shelf. Sana crouched beside the boy, covering his mouth gently to keep him from crying. Nandini sat against the wall, struggling to breathe, while Priya held her hand.

Kabir looked at the armed stranger.

“Who are those people?”

“People who don’t take no for an answer.”

The shutter rattled again. A metal tool scraped against the lock.

Dev grabbed a heavy box of medicine bottles and positioned himself beside the entrance. Rishi searched the back of the pharmacy for another exit, while Meera helped Priya lift Nandini to her feet.

Then the stranger spoke.

“My name is Imran. Your friend’s brother contacted me three days ago. He said a group of six young survivors would eventually be marked for recovery.”

Priya stared at him.

“You knew my brother?”

“I met him near the industrial zone. He was helping move injured civilians when he discovered what was happening inside that warehouse.”

“What was happening?” she demanded.

Imran glanced at the shutter.

“They weren't treating everyone. They were separating people into groups. Some were sent into trucks. Others were taken into a building underground.”

“Underground?” Meera whispered.

“Your brother found records showing that certain survivors were being collected deliberately. He copied a list before they discovered him.”

Priya’s eyes filled with tears.

“Is he alive?”

“I don’t know.”

The lock snapped.

“Enough talking!” Imran shouted. “Back door. Now!”

Rishi found a narrow door behind a stack of cartons. It opened into a cramped storage alley, barely wide enough for two people to walk side by side.

They hurried through it as the front shutter crashed open.

“Check every room!” someone ordered.

Imran was the last to leave. Before following the others, he placed the list in Priya’s hand.

“Your brother wanted you to find this.”

She unfolded the paper while running.

Beneath the heading RECOVERY PRIORITY — EASTERN SECTOR, several names had been crossed out. Others were marked with short codes.

Then she found her brother’s name.

PRIYA SHARMA — RELATED CONTACT: EASTERN INDUSTRIAL STAFF.

Below it was another line.

ARJUN — PRIORITY SUBJECT.

Priya stopped so suddenly that Kabir nearly collided with her.

“They want Arjun.”

Aarav looked back at the six-year-old boy.

“Why? He’s just a child.”

“Maybe that’s exactly why they want him,” Imran said. “Or maybe someone knows something about his mother.”

Nandini heard him.

“Please,” she whispered. “Don't let them take my son.”

Sana wrapped an arm around Arjun’s shoulders.

“They won’t.”

A loud engine started at the other end of the alley.

A black transport van rolled into view, blocking their escape. Two men stepped out wearing dark uniforms and white identification bands.

One of them raised a hand.

“Stop! We’re here to escort you to the safe zone.”

Imran lifted his pistol, but his hand trembled.

“Keep moving,” he muttered. “There's another way through the buildings.”

The men began advancing.

“Your names are on our recovery list,” the taller one called. “You can come peacefully, or we can do this the difficult way.”

Aarav felt his stomach tighten.

They knew their names. They knew about Arjun. They had followed them from the apartment.

This wasn’t a random rescue team.

It was a hunt.

Dev spotted a rusted ladder attached to the wall. It led to a low rooftop above the neighbouring shop.

“Up there!” he shouted.

Rishi climbed first and reached down to pull Meera up. Kabir helped Priya and Nandini toward the ladder while Sana carried Arjun’s small backpack.

The uniformed men broke into a run.

A gunshot cracked through the alley.

The bullet struck the wall beside Imran’s head.

“Go!” he yelled.

Aarav pushed Arjun toward the ladder. Sana climbed after him, and Kabir helped Nandini up with Priya supporting her from below.

Imran fired once into the air—not to hit anyone, but to make the pursuers hesitate.

It worked for only a second.

By the time he reached the rooftop, the men were already climbing after them.

The group crossed the roof and dropped into an abandoned tailoring workshop. Dust covered the machines. Old clothes hung from the ceiling like lifeless bodies.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Priya unfolded the list again.

There was writing on the back.

A message in her brother’s handwriting.

PRIYA, IF YOU FIND THIS, DON'T TRUST THE RESCUE TEAMS. THE WAREHOUSE IS ONLY THE FIRST LOCATION. THEY'RE MOVING EVERYONE TO THE OLD RAILWAY YARD.

Below it was a second sentence, written shakily.

THE SIX FROM PHOENIX MALL AREN'T ON THE LIST BY ACCIDENT. ONE OF THEM SAW SOMETHING BEFORE THE OUTBREAK SPREAD.

Everyone looked at one another.

“What does that mean?” Meera asked.

“I don’t know,” Priya said, her voice breaking. “But my brother knew about us before we ever heard his message.”

Aarav searched his memory. Phoenix Mall. The screaming. The first infected man crashing through the glass doors. The emergency lights. Their desperate escape through the service corridor.

He remembered something else.

Just before the first screams, a security guard had dragged a bloodied man through a staff-only entrance. The man had been wearing a white identification band.

Aarav had assumed he was injured.

But the guard had looked directly at him—and then at the others.

He had taken a photograph.

Aarav slowly raised his eyes.

“I think I know why we’re on that list.”

Before anyone could ask him what he meant, a radio crackled from Imran’s pocket.

A calm voice came through.

“Recovery team, report. Have you secured the six survivors and the child?”

Imran snatched the radio and switched it off.

Outside, engines roared as more vehicles entered the street.

Then Nandini collapsed.

Priya dropped beside her. Nandini’s skin had turned frighteningly pale, and dark veins were spreading from the bite on her arm.

“Mum?” Arjun whispered, shaking her shoulder.

Nandini opened her eyes.

For one brief moment, she looked at her son with complete recognition.

Then her gaze shifted toward Aarav.

“Don’t take him to the railway yard,” she whispered.

Aarav knelt beside her.

“Why?”

Her lips trembled.

“Because that’s where they take the ones who are still alive.”

Her body stiffened.

And from somewhere deep in her throat came a sound none of them wanted to hear.
