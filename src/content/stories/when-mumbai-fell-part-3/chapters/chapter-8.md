# Chapter 8: When the Last Light Fades

The control-room door shook under another heavy impact.

Aarav held the folder against his chest. Sana kept Arjun close while Priya stared at the small data card in the boy's hand.

Outside, armed men were preparing to enter.

Inside, Dr. Vikram Sethi looked like a man who had finally run out of excuses.

“There's a maintenance passage behind the control panel,” he said. “It leads to the emergency generator room. From there, you can reach the vehicle yard.”

Kabir didn't move.

“And what happens to everyone trapped here?”

Sethi lowered his eyes.

“If the facility loses power, the electronic locks on the civilian enclosures will release. But the security doors will also unlock.”

“Meaning the infected could get inside,” Dev said.

“Yes.”

Rishi looked toward the screens. “How many infected are being held here?”

“Twenty-three in the lower containment wing. More may have been brought in today.”

Meera stared at him.

“You put hundreds of civilians in a building with infected people?”

“I didn't authorize that arrangement.”

“But you stayed,” she replied. “You knew people were suffering, and you stayed.”

Sethi had no answer.

A third impact struck the door. A thin crack appeared near the lock.

Aarav made his decision.

“We're releasing the survivors. All of them.”

Imran nodded. “Then we need to move quickly.”

Sethi opened the maintenance passage. One by one, they climbed through, carrying the folder, the data card, and the radio.

Before following them, Sethi looked back at the control-room monitors.

For a moment, he seemed to hesitate.

Then he pulled a small control unit from the wall and followed the others into the passage.

Behind them, the door burst open.

A man's voice shouted from the control room.

“Find Sethi! Secure the card! Nobody leaves this facility!”

---

The emergency generator room was hot and filled with the smell of diesel.

Sethi led them past a row of electrical cabinets to a metal staircase descending toward the lower levels.

“The main power switch is here,” he explained. “If I shut down the system, the enclosure locks will release. But the backup generators will keep the outer gates operating for several minutes.”

“Several minutes?” Kabir repeated.

“Enough to open the civilian enclosures. Not enough to guarantee everyone escapes.”

Aarav looked at his friends.

They had survived the mall, the infected streets, the apartment building, and the railway yard. Every time they found somewhere to hide, danger had followed.

Now they had a chance to help hundreds of people.

But they might not make it out themselves.

“Do it,” Aarav said.

Sethi pulled the switch.

The lights went out.

For one second, the facility was silent.

Then alarms erupted throughout the building.

Emergency lights flickered on, bathing the corridors in red. Doors clicked open. Somewhere below, people began shouting.

“The locks are released!” Sethi yelled.

Imran pointed toward the stairs.

“Go! I'll lead the civilians toward the vehicle yard.”

Priya caught his arm.

“We're not leaving you.”

“Someone needs to get those people moving. Go find your brother's colleagues and tell them the western gate is the exit.”

Priya hesitated, then nodded.

The group rushed down the stairs.

They emerged into a long corridor where frightened civilians were spilling out of their rooms. Some were injured. Others clutched children or supported elderly relatives.

A guard attempted to block their way.

“Return to your rooms!”

Nobody listened.

The crowd surged past him.

Aarav helped an injured man stand while Dev carried a young girl whose ankle had been hurt. Meera and Rishi directed people toward the staircase, while Kabir searched for signs leading to the vehicle yard.

Sana stayed with Arjun.

“Don't let go of my hand,” she told him.

He nodded, gripping her fingers tightly.

Then a scream erupted from the far end of the corridor.

An infected man stumbled through an open containment door.

Behind him came two more.

The crowd panicked.

People pushed toward the stairs. Someone fell. A woman screamed for her husband.

“Keep moving!” Aarav shouted. “Don't stop!”

Imran appeared at the opposite end, waving his arms.

“This way! Follow me!”

He led a group of civilians through a service corridor while the others helped the remaining people escape the infected.

Aarav saw a little boy trapped beneath a fallen metal barrier.

Without thinking, he ran toward him.

“Dev, cover me!”

Dev grabbed a metal chair and slammed it against the approaching infected to draw their attention. Aarav pulled the barrier aside and lifted the child free.

The boy clung to him.

“Where's my mother?”

A woman pushed through the crowd and embraced him, sobbing.

Aarav handed the boy over and hurried after his friends.

They had no weapons worth mentioning. No military training. No guarantee that they would survive the next five minutes.

But they kept moving.

And for the first time since the outbreak began, they weren't running only to save themselves.

---

At the vehicle yard, chaos had taken over.

Transport trucks stood with their doors open. Civilians poured through the gates as alarms continued to sound. Several guards had abandoned their posts, while others tried desperately to restore order.

Priya spotted a man in railway work clothes helping people climb into an old supply truck.

“Sameer!”

He turned.

For one terrifying moment, she thought she had mistaken him for someone else.

Then her brother ran toward her.

Priya threw her arms around him.

“You're alive!”

Sameer held her tightly.

“I told you I'd try.”

She pulled back, crying and laughing at the same time.

“You idiot. You could have told me where you were going!”

“I didn't have time.”

Behind them, Aarav and the others reached the yard with Arjun and a group of rescued civilians.

Sameer looked at the child, then at the data card.

“You found it.”

“Nandini kept it safe,” Aarav replied.

Sameer's expression darkened at the mention of her name.

“I'm sorry,” he said quietly.

Priya closed her eyes.

There was no time to explain everything that had happened. No time to mourn properly. The facility was collapsing into chaos around them.

Sameer pointed toward the supply truck.

“Get everyone aboard. The western road leads away from the main checkpoints.”

“What about Sethi?” Kabir asked.

The doctor appeared behind them, breathing heavily. His white coat was stained with dust, and a cut ran across his forehead.

“I'm coming with you,” he said.

Aarav stepped in front of him.

“You're going to explain everything. Every record, every order, every name.”

“I will.”

“And if you lie?”

Sethi looked at the fleeing civilians.

“Then you should make sure the truth survives long enough for people to judge me.”

Aarav studied him for a moment, then stepped aside.

They climbed aboard the truck.

Sameer took the driver's seat, while Imran helped the remaining civilians into the back. Priya sat beside Arjun, holding him close. Sana settled beside them, exhausted but relieved.

As the engine started, gunshots rang out near the administration building.

A figure appeared on the upper balcony.

The man who had ordered the recovery teams stood behind the railing, surrounded by armed guards.

He raised a loudspeaker.

“Dr. Sethi! You are committing treason! Stop that vehicle immediately!”

Sethi stood in the back of the truck and looked up at him.

“No,” he called back. “I've been committing cowardice for too long.”

The man raised his weapon.

Imran slammed the truck's rear door shut.

“Everyone down!”

A shot struck the side panel.

Sameer accelerated.

The truck burst through the western gate as the last security lights flickered out. Behind them, the facility disappeared into darkness, filled with alarms, shouting, and the distant sounds of the infected.

Nobody spoke for several minutes.

They had escaped.

Not everyone had made it. They knew that. They had left people behind, and there were still infected across the city. Mumbai remained broken, and the people responsible for the disaster had not all been caught.

But hundreds of survivors were now free from the facility.

And the truth was no longer locked inside its walls.

---

Near midnight, the truck stopped at an abandoned roadside shelter.

The survivors climbed down and gathered beneath the roof. Sameer found bottled water and food in a storage room. For the first time in days, the group ate without someone shouting for them to run.

Arjun sat beside Priya, holding the small data card.

“Will my mum be proud of me?” he asked.

Priya knelt beside him.

“She already was.”

His eyes filled with tears, but he nodded.

Sana wrapped a blanket around his shoulders.

Aarav stood outside, looking toward the distant glow of Mumbai.

Kabir joined him.

“What now?”

Aarav watched smoke rise over the skyline.

“Now we make sure people learn the truth.”

Meera stepped outside with the folder. The original records, the photographs, and the list of officials had survived. Sameer had also recovered a portable transmitter from the facility.

“We can broadcast the files,” she said. “If the signal reaches other survivor groups, they can copy everything.”

Dev looked uncertain.

“And if the people behind this find us?”

“They already know who we are,” Rishi replied. “Hiding didn't protect us before.”

Sethi joined them, carrying the transmitter.

“The records can prove the containment failure and the attempts to conceal it. They won't explain every infection or bring the dead back. But they can stop the people responsible from pretending this never happened.”

Aarav looked at the six friends who had entered Phoenix Mall together, never imagining that an ordinary day would become the beginning of the end of their old lives.

They were exhausted. Frightened. Changed in ways they could never fully explain.

But they were still together.

Sameer connected the transmitter to a battery.

The device crackled.

Aarav stepped forward and began speaking.

“My name is Aarav. If anyone can hear this, we have evidence about the Phoenix Mall incident and the quarantine facilities. The outbreak was concealed, survivors were imprisoned, and families were separated. We are sending the records now. Copy them. Share them. Don't let anyone erase what happened.”

Meera began transmitting the files.

One by one, the documents were sent into the night.

For several seconds, there was only static.

Then a voice answered.

“This is a survivor group near Thane. We received your transmission.”

Another voice followed.

“Survivors in Navi Mumbai, we have the files too.”

Then another.

“We're sharing them now.”

The messages continued, growing louder and more frequent.

For the first time since the outbreak, the group heard something other than warnings, screams, and desperate pleas for help.

They heard people answering one another.

Aarav looked at the sky. The first pale light of dawn was beginning to appear beyond the clouds.

The world had not returned to normal.

The infected were still out there. The city was still wounded. Their families, their homes, and their old lives could not simply be restored.

But the truth had escaped.

And with it came a chance—not to undo the disaster, but to build something after it.

---

Three weeks later, a small settlement had formed near the outskirts of Mumbai.

Survivors repaired houses, shared food, guarded the roads, and searched for missing relatives. Sameer helped organize supply runs. Priya cared for injured children. Imran trained volunteers to protect the settlement without becoming the kind of people they had fought against.

Dr. Sethi remained under guard while survivors reviewed the evidence. He would have to answer for his decisions, including the times he had stayed silent.

Aarav, Kabir, Dev, Rishi, Meera, and Sana helped rebuild the settlement one day at a time.

Arjun began laughing again.

Sometimes, he still asked about his mother. Sometimes, the answer left him quiet for hours.

But he was safe.

One evening, the six friends stood together on a rooftop overlooking the damaged city.

The Mumbai skyline glowed faintly in the distance.

“Do you think we'll ever get our old lives back?” Rishi asked.

Kabir shook his head.

“No.”

Dev looked at the others.

“Maybe that's not the point anymore.”

Aarav watched the last sunlight disappear behind the buildings.

They had entered Phoenix Mall as ordinary friends.

They had escaped as survivors.

And now, after everything they had lost, they were choosing to become something more.

Sana rested her head against Meera's shoulder. Priya stood beside them, with Arjun holding her hand. Sameer called from below, telling everyone that dinner was ready.

For the first time in weeks, nobody was running.

Aarav took one final look at Mumbai.

The city had fallen.

But its people were still here.

And as a new dawn rose over the ruins, he understood what the last light had truly meant.

It wasn't the light of a world that had ended.

It was the light of a world that refused to give up.
