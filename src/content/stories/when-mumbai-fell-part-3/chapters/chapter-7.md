# Chapter 7: The Man Behind the Outbreak

The radio remained silent.

Aarav stared at it, trying to understand Dr. Vikram Sethi's final words.

*The outbreak you survived was never supposed to happen in public.*

“What does that mean?” Meera whispered.

“Whatever happened at Phoenix Mall, Sethi knows about it,” Kabir replied. “And he knows our names.”

Imran peered through the narrow opening of the drainage culvert. Searchlights continued sweeping across the road, and armed guards were checking the bushes along the perimeter.

“We can't stay here,” he said. “The drainage channel leads beneath the administration building. If we're lucky, it'll take us inside.”

“And if we're not lucky?” Dev asked.

“Then we'll find out why they wanted us alive.”

Nobody laughed.

They moved deeper into the culvert, keeping close to the damp concrete walls. After several minutes, they reached a rusted maintenance ladder leading to a metal hatch.

Imran pushed against it.

Locked.

Rishi examined the hinges while Dev searched the surrounding wall. Together, they managed to force the hatch open just enough for Aarav to squeeze through.

He climbed into a dark utility room and helped the others up.

A sign on the wall read:

ADMINISTRATION BLOCK — RESTRICTED ACCESS.

They had made it inside.

But the building was not empty.

Voices drifted from the corridor beyond the door.

“Doctor, the recovery team has confirmed seven subjects near the perimeter.”

“Then why haven't they been brought to me?”

“They escaped through the drainage system.”

A brief silence followed.

“Find them,” the doctor replied. “And don't let the child come to any harm.”

Priya gripped Sana's arm.

“Sameer said they wanted Arjun because of his mother. What if they're planning to do something to him?”

“We won't let them,” Sana whispered.

Aarav motioned for everyone to stay quiet. He opened the utility-room door a few centimetres.

The corridor was empty.

At the far end, a staircase led to the upper floor. A security camera rotated slowly above it.

They waited until the camera turned away, then crossed the corridor and climbed the stairs.

At the top, they found a row of offices. Most were locked, but one door stood open.

Inside, computer screens displayed lists of names, photographs, and medical reports.

Aarav stepped closer.

There they were again.

His own photograph. Kabir's. Dev's. Rishi's. Meera's. Sana's.

Beside each picture was the same classification:

PHOENIX INCIDENT — PRIORITY WITNESS.

Below the photographs was another file.

SUBJECT: ARJUN.

Sana opened it.

The first page contained a photograph of Nandini, taken inside a hospital several weeks before the outbreak. She was standing beside a man in a white coat.

Priya leaned over the screen.

“That isn't her husband.”

“No,” Imran replied. “Look at his identification badge.”

The photograph was blurry, but the name was readable.

DR. VIKRAM SETHI.

Meera opened the next document.

It described an experimental medical programme intended to study a highly contagious infection. The project had been conducted in a restricted laboratory outside Mumbai.

The details were incomplete, but one line stood out.

UNAUTHORIZED EXPOSURE CONFIRMED. CONTAINMENT FAILURE POSSIBLE.

Dev read it twice.

“They knew there was a risk.”

“Maybe they were studying the infection before it spread,” Kabir said. “But this doesn't prove they deliberately started the outbreak.”

Aarav nodded.

“Then we need the original records. Not just these summaries.”

A sound came from the corridor.

Footsteps.

Imran switched off the monitors while Meera copied the files onto a small storage drive she found beside the computer. The others slipped into an adjoining records room just as the office door opened.

Two guards entered.

“Doctor Sethi wants the Phoenix files transferred to the control room,” one said.

“What about the witnesses?”

“They'll be questioned after the transfer.”

“And the child?”

The second guard lowered his voice.

“The doctor says the boy may be the key to understanding the first exposure.”

The men left.

Sana looked at Arjun, who was standing silently beside her.

“He's six years old,” she whispered. “What could he possibly know?”

Aarav remembered Nandini's final warning.

*Don't take him to the railway yard.*

She had known the place was dangerous. Perhaps she had known more than she had been able to tell them.

“We have to find Sethi,” Aarav said.

---

The control room was at the end of the upper corridor.

Unlike the other offices, its entrance had no visible lock. A keypad was built into the wall, and a camera watched anyone approaching.

Imran studied the door.

“We can't force this without alerting the whole building.”

Rishi pointed to a small service panel beside the keypad.

“Maybe we don't need to.”

He removed the panel cover and examined the wiring. He wasn't an expert, but after several tense minutes, the electronic lock clicked.

The door opened.

Inside, Dr. Vikram Sethi stood beside a large screen showing a map of Mumbai. Red markers covered the city, indicating outbreaks and evacuation zones.

He was older than Aarav had expected, with tired eyes and grey hair. His white coat was clean, but his hands trembled slightly as he turned around.

“You came,” he said.

Aarav stepped into the room.

“Who are we?”

Sethi studied the six friends one by one.

“You were witnesses to the first public incident. I needed to know what you saw.”

“You photographed us before the outbreak,” Sana said. “You followed us.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the man you saw at Phoenix Mall was carrying evidence stolen from my laboratory.”

Kabir stepped forward.

“Evidence of what?”

Sethi glanced at the map.

“An experimental treatment was being developed for a dangerous viral disease. The project was intended to help people, not harm them. But one of the test samples changed unexpectedly. It became far more contagious than our team had anticipated.”

“So you created the infection?” Dev demanded.

“We created the experimental agent,” Sethi answered. “We did not intend for it to spread. Someone violated containment procedures, and the infection escaped before we could understand what it had become.”

“Who?” Priya asked.

Sethi hesitated.

“I don't know who opened the restricted storage unit. I was told the sample had been secured. By the time I learned otherwise, the first infected people were already appearing in Mumbai.”

“And instead of warning everyone, you started collecting survivors,” Aarav said.

Sethi's face tightened.

“The authorities feared mass panic. They established quarantine facilities and ordered the recovery of anyone connected to the initial incident. They believed the witnesses might help identify how the infection had spread.”

“You could have told people the truth,” Meera said.

“I tried to report the containment failure. My superiors overruled me. After the outbreak spread, they took control of the recovery operation.”

“Then why are you still working for them?” Kabir asked.

“Because I thought I could save the people being brought here.”

Sethi looked toward the screen showing the crowded enclosures.

“I was wrong about what this facility had become.”

Aarav didn't trust him.

“You expect us to believe you're innocent?”

“No,” Sethi replied. “I expect you to examine the evidence and decide for yourselves.”

He opened a drawer and removed a thin folder.

“This contains the original incident report. It names the officials who ordered the records concealed and identifies the transport route used to move the infected sample before the Phoenix Mall incident.”

Aarav reached for it.

Sethi pulled it back.

“First, you must understand why Arjun is here.”

Sana immediately moved in front of the boy.

“Stay away from him.”

“I have no intention of harming him,” Sethi said. “His mother was a nurse at the medical facility where the first exposure occurred. She helped evacuate patients before the facility was sealed.”

Priya's eyes widened.

“Nandini worked there?”

“Yes. She witnessed the initial containment failure. She later took her son and disappeared. The recovery teams found her name in the witness records.”

Aarav understood now why Nandini had recognized the black case and why she had feared the railway yard.

“But why Arjun?” he asked.

“Because his mother carried a medical record that the authorities believed had been copied onto a small data card. They thought she had hidden it with her son.”

“Did she?” Sana asked.

Sethi looked at Arjun.

“I don't know. But if that record still exists, it could prove exactly how the infection escaped.”

Arjun reached into his backpack.

“I have something.”

Everyone turned toward him.

With trembling fingers, he pulled out a small, scratched plastic card attached to a faded key ring.

“Mum told me to keep it safe,” he said. “She said not to give it to the men with the white bands.”

Sethi stared at the card.

For the first time, the doctor looked genuinely frightened.

“Where did you get that?”

“From my mum.”

Sethi took a slow breath.

“That is the original incident record.”

Before Aarav could ask another question, the control-room door slammed shut.

A red light flashed above it.

The loudspeakers crackled.

“Dr. Sethi, step away from the witnesses.”

Sethi's face went pale.

A new voice filled the room.

“You have been relieved of your duties, Doctor. Security is on its way.”

Sethi turned to Aarav.

“They've discovered the card.”

“Who is that?” Kabir demanded.

Sethi looked at the sealed door.

“The man who ordered the original records destroyed.”

A heavy impact shook the door.

Then the voice spoke again.

“Hand over the child and the data card. The remaining witnesses are no longer required.”

Aarav tightened his grip on the folder.

The truth was finally within reach.

But the people responsible for hiding it were standing just outside the door.

And this time, they weren't coming to take the group somewhere safe.

They were coming to erase every witness.
