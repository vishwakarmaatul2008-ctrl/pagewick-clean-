# Chapter 5 — The Mountain That Opened

At dawn, the binding spell shattered. A collective shriek of grief tore through Hamelin as parents broke from their homes and flooded the streets.

Led by Mayor Hunke, the frantic townspeople tracked the tiny, bloody footprints into the ancient pine forest at the base of Koppen Hill.

There, at the base of a massive wall of grey granite, the footprints stopped before a jagged, towering tear in the mountain—a cavern that smelled of ancient iron, stagnant river water, and crushed bone.

Mayor Hunke and the men rushed inside with torches, screaming their children's names. Deep within the earth, scattered along the damp stone floor, they found dropped wooden toys, torn hair ribbons, and tiny shoes stained with blood.

Suddenly, a sound like a thunderclap shook the mountain. The ground vibrated violently as the massive stone walls of the cavern began to slide shut.

The men scrambled back toward the light in blind panic. Mayor Hunke dragged himself out onto the grass just as the two granite faces slammed together with a deafening BOOM.

When the dust settled, the opening was gone. The rock face was smooth, solid, and unbroken.

Sitting on a rock nearby was small Johann—the seven-year-old boy with a crippled leg who had been too slow to keep up with the procession.

Hunke fell to his knees, clutching the boy's shoulders. "Where are they?! Where did he take my daughter?"

Johann stared at the blank stone wall, his eyes hollow.

"The tune changed inside the mountain, Mayor," Johann whispered, his voice trembling. "He did not take them to a land of joy. He took them down to the subterranean river... to break their bones and turn them into things that crawl in the dark."

Johann looked directly into Hunke's eyes, his breath cold as river mist.

"He said the debt of Hamelin will be paid through generations. He said when the town dies, you will be the last one left to hear them tapping under your floorboards."
