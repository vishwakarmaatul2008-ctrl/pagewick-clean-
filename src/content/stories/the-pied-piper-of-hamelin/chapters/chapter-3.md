# Chapter 3 — The Rejection of the Bargain

By noon the following day, the mood in Hamelin had mutated from terror to collective, arrogant relief.

Inside the Town Council chamber, Mayor Hunke sat behind a table stacked with leather bags of silver and copper. Surrounding him were not just councilors, but the town's wealthiest merchants, guild masters, and prominent citizens.

"One thousand gold guilders," Hunke spat, running a thumb over his signet ring. "It is an absurdity. Are we to hand over the wealth of three generations to a homeless vagrant in a stitched rags?"

"He saved the town, Mayor," whispered Guildmaster Klaus nervously.

"He used black sorcery!" shouted a grain merchant from the back. "I saw him walk on the river! It is devilry! Is it lawful under Holy Church law to pay a sorcerer?"

"He is a fraud and a beggar!" shouted a butcher. "He probably brought the rats here himself to extort us!"

A collective roar of agreement echoed through the hall. The entire town leadership—backed by the shouts of the citizens gathered outside the open windows—decided together that the contract was void.

As dusk fell and the sun dipped below the horizon, the heavy doors of the hall swung open.

The Piper entered. The oil lamps flickered violently, their flames turning a faint, unnatural green. He stood in the doorway, a tall, terrifying silhouette. His deep hood cast his face in total darkness, revealing only the long, sharp ridge of his unnatural nose and the faint glint of pale teeth under the shadow.

"The river is full," the Piper whispered, his voice echoing off the stone walls. "The teeth are gone from your walls. Give me my gold."

Mayor Hunke stood up, backed by the sneering councilors. He tossed a single leather pouch containing fifty copper coins onto the table.

"Take this copper, you freak, and get out!" Hunke bellowed. "You are a vagrant, a fraud, and a practitioner of dark magic! The people of Hamelin owe you nothing! If you are not past the city gates by nightfall, our guards will drag you to the square and burn you as a witch!"

The crowd inside and outside the hall erupted into jeers and insults. They mocked his tall, unnaturally thin frame, pointed at the long, bizarre silhouette of his hood, and threw stale bread and stones at his feet.

The Piper did not flinch. He did not yell. He slowly lowered his head until the tip of his long nose pointed directly at Mayor Hunke.

"You have decided together that the debt is worthless," the Piper rasped softly, his voice cutting through the roaring crowd like a cold blade. "Very well. You will pay together."

He turned and melted into the gathering twilight. As he reached the city gates, the townspeople threw mud at his dark cloak and slammed the heavy iron-reinforced doors behind him, laughing and celebrating their victory over the mysterious rat-catcher.
