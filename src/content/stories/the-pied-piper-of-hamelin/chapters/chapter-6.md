# Chapter 6 — The Cursed City

Hamelin did not recover. The rejection of the debt became a terminal curse upon the land.

The town decayed from the inside out. Fields withered, trade routes rerouted to avoid the cursed valley, and no child was ever born in Hamelin again. The merchants who had laughed at the Piper went bankrupt as their goods rotted in empty warehouses.

For Mayor Hunke, his punishment was exquisitely personal.

He did not die. While the rest of the citizens gradually fled or perished in despair, Hunke was trapped in his decaying manor house. Every night, precisely at midnight, the temperature in his room would drop.

He would hear the faint, echoing sound of a bone flute playing outside his window. When he looked down into the unlit street, he would see the tall, thin silhouette of the Piper standing beneath the broken street lamp, his hood pulled low, his long nose pointed up at Hunke's window, silently watching.

And from deep beneath his oak floorboards—from the dark earth where the foundation met the bedrock—came the constant, wet, rhythmic tapping of dozens of tiny, malformed hands, scraping against the stone, waiting for the Mayor to come down into the dark.

Hamelin became a ghost town—a monument to a broken promise, where the dark collected what was owed, down to the very last grain of flesh.
