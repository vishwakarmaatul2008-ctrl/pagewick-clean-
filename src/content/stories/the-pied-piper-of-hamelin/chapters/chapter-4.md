# Chapter 4 — The Midnight Procession

Night fell over Hamelin, but the atmosphere did not feel safe. A suffocating dread settled over every house.

From that night onward, the Piper became a recurring shadow at the edge of their world.

Guardians on the watchtowers reported seeing a tall, impossibly thin silhouette standing beneath the lonely iron street lamps outside the walls, staring silently up at the battlements. Woodcutters spoke of hearing a faint, melancholy notes of a bone flute drifting through the dark forest at midnight.

Then came the night of the collection.

At 3:00 AM, the temperature inside every home plummeted. Candles extinguished themselves in unison.

The pipe spoke again from the dark pine woods of Koppen Hill. This time, it was a sound of agonizing, terrifying sweetness—a crystal-clear melody that drifted through the shuttered windows.

The adults woke first, but they were paralyzed. A dark, binding spell held every grown man, woman, and merchant frozen in their beds. Mayor Hunke lay awake, his eyes bulging in terror, unable to lift a finger or utter a scream as his nine-year-old daughter, Clara, rose from her bed with pitch-black, dilated eyes and walked silently out the door.

Across Hamelin, every parent watched from their beds as one hundred and thirty children stepped barefoot into the freezing night air.

The children marched four abreast, their bare soles slapping against the cold cobblestones. At the head of the procession walked the Piper, dancing backward into the dark, his face completely buried beneath his deep hood, his long nose casting a sharp shadow under the pale moonlight as his fingers flew over the bone flute.

The entire town council, the merchants, and the guards were forced to watch in silent, agonizing paralysis as their children marched past the open city gates, following the dark shadow into the black woods.
