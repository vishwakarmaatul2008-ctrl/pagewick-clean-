# Chapter 9 — The Conductor

Arjun stared at the message.

NEXT TRAIN --- 12:13.

His father's metal badge felt cold in his hand.

He looked at the clock.

12:12 AM.

One minute.

Then his phone rang.

The screen showed:

UNKNOWN NUMBER

Arjun answered.

For several seconds, there was only static.

Then a man's voice.

His father's voice.

"Arjun."

He stopped breathing.

"Dad?"

"You shouldn't have picked up."

"Where are you?"

A long silence.

"Where I have always been."

The line went dead.

Arjun immediately called Meera.

No answer.

He called Rohan.

Nothing.

Then Joseph.

The phone rang once.

Twice.

Joseph answered.

"I got the message too."

Arjun's stomach tightened.

"What message?"

Joseph was silent.

Then he said:

"Your father's name."

Arjun looked at the badge.

"Did you know he was the conductor?"

"I knew he disappeared."

"That's not what I asked."

Another silence.

"Yes."

Arjun closed his eyes.

"You knew."

"I suspected."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because I didn't know whether your father was trying to save you..."

Joseph's voice became quieter.

"...or bring you back."

The clock changed.

12:13 AM.

Every light in Arjun's apartment went out.

Then his phone screen went black.

A train horn sounded outside.

Arjun slowly walked toward the window.

There were no railway tracks anywhere near his apartment.

But outside---

a train was passing through the street.

Silent.

Black.

Impossible.

And inside the last carriage stood a man in an old railway uniform.

His father.

Rajiv Mehra.

Arjun ran downstairs.

---

The street was empty.

The train had stopped at the intersection.

There were no tracks.

Yet it stood there as naturally as if it had always belonged.

The doors opened.

Rajiv stepped down.

He looked older than the last time Arjun had seen him in the station.

His hair was almost completely grey.

His face was tired.

But his eyes were the same.

Arjun couldn't speak.

Rajiv looked at him.

"I knew you'd come."

"Why did you leave us?"

Rajiv looked away.

"I didn't."

"You disappeared."

"I was taken."

"By the station?"

Rajiv nodded.

"But not as a passenger."

He looked at the train.

"I became the conductor."

Arjun's voice shook.

"Why?"

"Because someone had to remember."

Rajiv explained that after the 2006 incident, the station could no longer take the five children directly.

Their memories had become locked away.

But the station still needed a conductor.

Someone who remembered the entire night.

Someone who could guide the train when it returned.

"So you stayed?"

"I had no choice."

"There's always a choice."

Rajiv looked at him.

"That's what I believed too."

He touched the badge on Arjun's hand.

"This badge isn't a key."

"Then what is it?"

"A promise."

Arjun frowned.

"Whose promise?"

Rajiv's eyes moved toward the train.

"Yours."

Arjun froze.

"My promise?"

"Before you forgot, you made one."

Rajiv's voice became softer.

"You promised you'd come back for your sister."

Arjun remembered.

A flash.

A little boy standing on the platform.

Holding Nisha's hand.

Crying.

Telling her:

"I'll come back."

Then the memory disappeared.

Arjun looked at his father.

"I did come back."

"Yes."

"Then why is the train still here?"

Rajiv's expression changed.

"Because Nisha wasn't the only one who stayed."

Arjun stared at him.

"What do you mean?"

Rajiv pointed toward the train.

"Look inside."

Arjun stepped closer.

Through the window, he saw five empty seats.

Then a sixth.

Someone was sitting there.

A little girl.

Red scarf.

Anika's younger self.

Arjun stepped backward.

"But Anika left."

Rajiv nodded.

"Her body did."

"Then what's that?"

"A memory."

The child looked directly at Arjun.

She wasn't crying.

She was smiling.

Rajiv whispered:

"The station doesn't release memories."

"It only releases people."

Arjun suddenly understood.

The station had let Anika go.

But it had kept the memory of the girl who stayed behind.

And that memory was still inside the train.

Rajiv continued:

"That's why the train returned."

"To get her?"

"No."

Rajiv looked at Arjun.

"To finish what began twenty years ago."

The train doors opened.

Inside, the lights turned on.

The child wearing the red scarf stood.

She pointed toward Arjun.

Then toward the empty seat beside her.

Rajiv shook his head.

"Don't get on."

Arjun stared at his father.

"You brought me here."

"I came to warn you."

"About what?"

Rajiv looked terrified for the first time.

"The conductor is not the one who controls the train."

Arjun's blood ran cold.

"Then who does?"

Rajiv looked toward the darkness inside the carriage.

Something moved.

A figure slowly stepped forward.

Tall.

Thin.

Wearing an old railway uniform.

But its face was completely hidden beneath the shadow of its cap.

Rajiv whispered:

"The first passenger."

The figure stopped beside the little girl.

Then it slowly raised its head.

Arjun saw its face.

And recognized it.

It was him.

Not the eight-year-old Arjun.

Not the adult Arjun.

Something in between.

The version of him that had disappeared twenty years ago.

The figure smiled.

And the train announcement echoed through the empty street:

"Five passengers aboard."

Rajiv grabbed Arjun's shoulder.

"Run."

The train doors began closing.

But before they shut completely, the other four passengers appeared inside.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

All asleep.

All sitting in their original seats.

The train horn sounded.

Rajiv pushed Arjun backward.

"Whatever happens..."

He looked at his son.

"...do not let the train reach Northfield again."

The doors slammed shut.

The train began moving.

Arjun watched helplessly as his father remained inside.

Then Rajiv looked through the window.

His lips formed three words.

"Remember your promise."

The train vanished.

Arjun stood alone in the street.

His phone turned back on.

One new message appeared.

12:13 AM --- NORTHFIELD STATION

FINAL SERVICE DEPARTS IN 24 HOURS.

Then another message appeared.

From his father's number.

"Bring the five of you."

Arjun stared at the screen.

A final line appeared.

"This time, don't forget who the fifth passenger is."
