# Chapter 6 — The Station's True Rule

Arjun opened his eyes.

He was lying on the platform.

Rain fell around him.

But the station was different.

The walls were new.

The benches were clean.

The lights worked.

A clock above the ticket office showed:

12:13 AM.

Arjun slowly stood.

"Meera?"

No answer.

"Rohan?"

Nothing.

"Joseph?"

Silence.

Then he heard a child crying.

He turned.

A little boy was sitting alone on a bench.

Eight years old.

Dark hair.

A small scar above his eyebrow.

Arjun stared at him.

It was himself.

Twenty years ago.

The boy looked up.

"You came back."

Arjun couldn't speak.

The child pointed toward the tracks.

"Your mother is there."

Arjun followed his finger.

A woman stood across the platform.

His mother.

She looked exactly as he remembered her.

Young.

Tired.

Worried.

Arjun stepped toward her.

"Mom?"

She looked at him.

Tears filled her eyes.

"I've been trying to get you back."

Arjun stopped.

"Back from where?"

She looked at the train tracks.

"From the station."

A train passed without making a sound.

His mother continued.

"Northfield isn't a place."

Arjun frowned.

"It's a door."

The train disappeared.

"And every twenty years, it opens."

Arjun remembered the five children.

"Why us?"

His mother looked at him.

"Because the station doesn't choose people."

She whispered:

"It chooses memories."

---

Arjun looked toward his younger self.

The boy was crying.

"What happened that night?"

His mother closed her eyes.

"I knew the train would come."

"How?"

"Because your father had already seen it."

Arjun froze.

"My father?"

She nodded.

"He tried to destroy the station's records. He thought if nobody remembered Northfield, the train couldn't return."

"What happened to him?"

"He disappeared."

Arjun's breathing became unsteady.

"Was he taken?"

His mother shook her head.

"No."

She pointed toward the tracks.

"He became the conductor."

A bell rang somewhere in the distance.

Ding.

Arjun looked at the empty track.

His mother grabbed his hand.

"The conductor isn't the one who drives the train."

"Then what is he?"

She looked terrified.

"The conductor is the person who remembers everything."

Arjun suddenly understood.

Joseph.

The old man had remembered.

He had returned.

And he had been looking for himself.

"Joseph..."

His mother nodded.

"He was the last conductor."

Arjun stared at her.

"Then why did he bring us here?"

"Because he thought he could finally end it."

She looked toward the clock.

12:14 AM.

"Your friends aren't really passengers."

Arjun looked at her.

"They're anchors."

"What does that mean?"

"The station needs five memories to open."

She pointed toward the empty seats.

"Four memories belong to the children who escaped."

Then she looked at Arjun.

"And the fifth belongs to the person who was left behind."

"Anika."

His mother nodded.

"Anika was never the fifth passenger."

She whispered:

"She was the lock."

---

A train horn suddenly echoed through the station.

Arjun turned.

The old train was approaching.

But something was different.

There was a person standing inside the driver's cabin.

An old man in a railway uniform.

Arjun could barely see his face through the rain.

Then the train passed beneath the platform light.

Arjun saw him.

His father.

Older.

Pale.

Still wearing the same watch Arjun remembered from childhood.

Arjun ran toward the train.

"Dad!"

The train stopped.

The doors opened.

His father stepped down.

He looked at Arjun.

But there was no happiness in his eyes.

Only exhaustion.

"You shouldn't have come back."

Arjun stared at him.

"You made us forget."

His father shook his head.

"No."

He pointed toward the station.

"The station did."

"Then why did you let it happen?"

His father looked toward the other platform.

Meera, Rohan, Joseph and Anika were standing there.

Alive.

Confused.

His father said:

"Because forgetting was the only way to keep you alive."

Arjun's voice broke.

"And now?"

His father looked at the clock.

12:19 AM.

"We don't have much time."

The station lights began flickering.

His father stepped closer.

"There is only one true rule."

"What rule?"

His father whispered:

"The station can only take what you willingly give it."

Arjun stared at him.

"Then why did it take Anika?"

"It didn't."

His father looked at Anika.

"She gave herself."

Anika's eyes widened.

Her memories were returning.

The night of 2006.

Her mother.

The train.

The four children.

And the promise she had made.

Her mother had told her:

"If the station asks for one, give it a memory---not a life."

Anika had misunderstood.

She had given it everything.

Her childhood.

Her family.

Her identity.

Her memories of the others.

And because she had forgotten herself...

the station kept her.

Arjun looked at the train.

"So how do we stop it?"

His father looked at him.

"You don't."

The train doors opened.

"You make it choose something it cannot keep."

Arjun frowned.

"What?"

His father smiled sadly.

"A memory that belongs to everyone."

The clock jumped.

12:20 AM.

Five voices began whispering through the station.

Arjun.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

All speaking the same words.

"We remember."

The station began shaking.

Cracks spread across the walls.

His father stepped backward.

"Arjun."

"What?"

"You have one chance."

The train began moving.

"Give it the memory of the night."

Arjun stared at him.

"If I do that, I'll forget everything."

His father nodded.

"Yes."

"And you?"

His father smiled.

"I've been waiting twenty years for someone to remember me."

The train accelerated.

Arjun looked at his father.

Then at Anika.

Then at the other three.

He understood.

The station didn't need five people.

It needed five memories.

And if they all remembered together---

the station couldn't take one without taking all five.

Arjun closed his eyes.

And said:

"We remember Northfield."

The station screamed.

The clock shattered.

12:21 AM.

And somewhere beneath the tracks---

something ancient finally woke up.
