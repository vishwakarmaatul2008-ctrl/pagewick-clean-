# Chapter 3 — The Fifth Passenger

The footsteps continued.

Clang.

Silence.

Clang.

Silence.

They were coming from beneath the station.

Rohan stepped backward.

"There's something under us."

Joseph tightened his grip on his walking stick.

"There wasn't a basement here."

Meera looked at the floor.

The old wooden boards trembled slightly.

Then the station lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

And suddenly every light turned on.

For the first time, Northfield Station was completely visible.

Arjun looked around.

The walls were covered with old posters.

Most were faded beyond recognition.

But one poster caught his attention.

A missing-person notice.

He walked closer.

There were five photographs.

Five names.

Five people.

And underneath them:

MISSING --- JUNE 14, 2006

Arjun's breathing stopped.

He knew one of the faces.

It was him.

"No..."

Meera came beside him.

"What is it?"

Arjun pointed at the photograph.

The picture showed a young boy.

Maybe eight years old.

Dark hair.

Same eyes.

Same small scar above his eyebrow.

Underneath it was a name.

ARJUN MEHRA --- AGE 8

Meera stared at him.

"That's impossible."

"I know."

Rohan looked at the other photographs.

His expression slowly changed.

"Guys..."

He pointed.

The second photograph was a young girl.

MEERA SHARMA --- AGE 9

Meera stepped backward.

"No."

Rohan's hand began shaking.

The third photograph.

ROHAN MALHOTRA --- AGE 12

Joseph looked at the fourth.

His face became completely empty.

JOSEPH D'SOUZA --- AGE 31

Then everyone looked at Anika.

Her photograph was missing.

There were only four.

Anika whispered:

"Why isn't mine there?"

Nobody answered.

Then Joseph said something that made everyone's blood run cold.

"Because you weren't there."

Anika stared at him.

"What?"

Joseph pointed toward the poster.

"Five people disappeared that night."

He pointed to the four photographs.

"Four of them are standing here."

Then he looked at Anika.

"You're the fifth."

The station became silent.

Anika slowly shook her head.

"No."

She backed away.

"I've never been here."

Joseph's voice remained calm.

"Neither had I."

Arjun looked at him.

"What are you saying?"

Joseph turned toward the dark tunnel entrance at the end of the station.

"I didn't come here looking for someone."

He looked at Arjun.

"I came looking for myself."

A loud CLANG came from beneath them.

The floor cracked.

Anika screamed.

Something struck the wooden boards from underneath.

Once.

Twice.

Then the floor burst open.

A black hole appeared beneath the station.

Cold air rushed upward.

And from the darkness below came a voice.

A child's voice.

"Arjun?"

Arjun froze.

The voice came again.

"Arjun, it's me."

He knew that voice.

He hadn't heard it in twenty years.

His sister.

Nisha.

She had disappeared when he was eight.

He took a step toward the hole.

Meera grabbed his arm.

"Don't."

Arjun stared into the darkness.

"Nisha?"

The voice answered immediately.

"Help me."

Arjun pulled away.

"She's alive."

Joseph grabbed him.

"No."

"But that's her voice!"

Joseph shook his head.

"The station remembers voices."

Rohan stared into the hole.

"Then whose voice is it?"

Joseph looked at him.

"The people who never came back."

A hand suddenly appeared from the darkness.

Small.

Pale.

Reaching upward.

Arjun stumbled backward.

The hand disappeared.

Then another appeared.

Then another.

Five pale hands slowly climbed over the edge of the broken floor.

Meera screamed.

"RUN!"

They sprinted toward the station exit.

But before they could reach it---

The doors slammed shut.

12:15 AM.

The station clock had moved again.

Joseph looked at it.

"We have ten minutes."

Rohan pulled at the doors.

"They're locked!"

Anika suddenly whispered:

"Wait."

Everyone turned.

She was staring at the red scarf around her neck.

There was something written on it.

Words that hadn't been there before.

She pulled it off.

On the inside, written in faded black ink:

I AM THE FIFTH.

Anika dropped it.

"No..."

Arjun stared at her.

"Who are you?"

Anika looked at him.

For the first time, her expression wasn't frightened.

It was sad.

"I don't know."

Then she began crying.

"I don't remember my childhood."

Nobody moved.

"I don't remember my parents. I don't remember where I was born. I don't even remember when I got this scarf."

She looked toward the broken floor.

"But every night, I dream about this station."

Joseph slowly approached her.

"What do you dream?"

Anika wiped her tears.

"Five children."

She looked at Arjun.

"Four of them are crying."

Then she looked at herself.

"And one of them is smiling."

Arjun felt his stomach drop.

"Which one?"

Anika whispered:

"Me."

The lights went out.

Complete darkness.

Then a voice spoke directly beside Arjun's ear.

A child's voice.

"You left me here."

Arjun turned.

Nobody was standing beside him.

But something cold touched his hand.

He looked down.

A small child's hand was holding his.

He couldn't see the child.

Then the station lights came back on.

And there were now five wet footprints leading from the broken floor...

straight toward them.

The footprints stopped at Anika.

She looked down.

Her bare feet were covered in water.

She hadn't been near the hole.

Arjun looked at the station clock.

12:16 AM.

Eight minutes remained.

And beneath the station, something began to laugh.

Not one voice.

Five voices.

Children.

All laughing together.
