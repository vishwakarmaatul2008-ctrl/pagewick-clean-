# Chapter 1 — 12:13

The last train was supposed to leave at 11:58 PM.

Arjun knew that because he had checked the timetable three times.

He also knew that he had missed it.

The station was almost empty when he reached the platform, breathing heavily, his backpack hanging from one shoulder.

The digital clock above Platform 4 read:

12:07 AM

"Perfect," he muttered.

He dropped onto a metal bench.

His phone showed 18% battery.

No missed calls.

No messages.

Just the faint sound of rain hitting the station roof.

Arjun looked down the tracks.

Nothing.

The station had officially closed five minutes ago.

A security guard at the far end was pulling down the shutters of the ticket counter. One by one, the lights along the platform were being switched off.

Arjun stood.

"Excuse me!"

The guard looked over.

"Is there another train?"

The guard shook his head.

"Not tonight."

"What about the old platform?"

The guard stopped.

Arjun pointed toward the darkness beyond Platform 4.

There was another platform behind a locked iron gate.

It looked abandoned.

The sign above it had almost completely faded.

PLATFORM 6

The guard's expression changed.

"Don't go there."

Arjun laughed.

"I'm just asking."

"Then take a taxi."

"I don't have enough money."

The guard stared at him.

Then he said something that Arjun couldn't hear.

"What?"

The guard looked away.

"Go home."

Arjun frowned.

Before he could ask anything else, the lights went out.

The entire station fell silent.

---

For the next five minutes, Arjun waited alone.

12:09.

12:10.

12:11.

Then the station clock flickered.

12:12

Arjun checked his phone.

12:12.

He looked toward Platform 6.

The locked gate was still there.

Then he heard a train.

Not the sound of a normal train approaching.

It sounded distant.

Very distant.

As though it were travelling through a tunnel buried beneath the city.

The sound grew louder.

Arjun stood.

The clock changed.

12:13 AM

A headlight appeared beyond Platform 6.

The iron gate was suddenly open.

Arjun stared.

The train rolled into the abandoned platform without making a single sound.

No screeching brakes.

No announcement.

No vibration beneath his feet.

It simply arrived.

Five dark carriages.

Old-fashioned windows.

No railway logo.

No destination displayed.

Then the doors opened.

Arjun didn't move.

Someone stepped out.

A woman in her twenties.

She looked around the empty platform.

"Is this Central Station?"

Arjun nodded.

"I think so."

The woman frowned.

"I don't remember coming here."

A man stepped out behind her.

He was perhaps thirty-five, wearing a rain-soaked office shirt and carrying a black laptop bag.

He looked at Arjun.

"Where's the driver?"

"I don't know."

Another passenger appeared.

An elderly man with a walking stick.

He looked at the train and whispered,

"No..."

A fourth person stepped down.

A young woman wearing a red scarf.

She immediately began crying.

Arjun stared at her.

"Are you okay?"

She shook her head.

"I shouldn't be here."

Then the doors began closing.

Arjun looked at the four strangers.

"Wait."

The woman in the red scarf suddenly grabbed his wrist.

"Don't let the doors close."

"Why?"

Her eyes widened.

"Because we weren't supposed to board."

The doors continued closing.

Arjun pulled her forward.

The five of them stumbled inside together.

The doors shut.

12:13 AM.

The train began moving.

---

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Arjun looked around the carriage.

Old brown seats.

Yellow lights.

Dust-covered windows.

No advertisements.

No emergency instructions.

No CCTV cameras.

Nothing modern.

The woman who had asked about Central Station sat across from him.

"My name is Meera."

The office worker placed his laptop bag on the seat.

"Rohan."

The elderly man tightened his grip around his walking stick.

"Joseph."

The woman with the red scarf wiped her tears.

"Anika."

Four names.

Four strangers.

Arjun hesitated.

"Arjun."

Meera looked at him.

"Why were you at the station?"

"I missed my train."

Rohan frowned.

"So did I."

Joseph looked toward the window.

"I wasn't travelling."

Everyone looked at him.

"I was looking for someone."

"Who?" Anika asked.

Joseph didn't answer.

Meera crossed her arms.

"I don't understand something."

She pointed toward the ceiling.

"There was no train on the timetable at 12:13."

Rohan checked his phone.

"No signal."

Anika checked hers.

"Mine says 12:13."

Arjun pulled out his phone.

12:13.

Joseph looked at the old clock inside the carriage.

Its second hand wasn't moving.

12:13.

Rohan laughed nervously.

"Maybe the clocks are broken."

Then the train announcement came.

A woman's voice.

Calm.

Emotionless.

"Five passengers aboard."

Everyone went silent.

Meera counted.

"One."

She pointed at Rohan.

"Two."

Joseph.

"Three."

Anika.

"Four."

She pointed at Arjun.

"Five."

Arjun stared at her.

"Yes."

Five people.

Exactly.

Then the announcement repeated.

"Five passengers aboard."

The train entered a tunnel.

The lights flickered.

For a moment, the windows became completely black.

Then Arjun noticed something.

There was a reflection in the glass.

He could see the five of them sitting inside the carriage.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

And himself.

Five.

He relaxed.

Then the train passed a bright light outside.

The reflection changed.

Arjun froze.

There were six people sitting in the carriage.

The sixth person was seated directly behind him.

A woman.

Her face was hidden by wet black hair.

Arjun slowly turned.

The seat behind him was empty.

He looked back at the window.

Six reflections.

He stared.

Nobody else seemed to notice.

Then the woman in the reflection slowly raised her head.

Arjun's blood went cold.

She was looking directly at him.

Her lips moved.

No sound came through.

But Arjun understood the words.

Don't let them count again.

The tunnel ended.

The reflection returned to five.

Arjun stood suddenly.

"What the hell is this?"

Everyone stared at him.

"What?" Rohan asked.

Arjun pointed at the window.

"There was someone---"

The train announcement interrupted him.

"Next station."

A pause.

Then the woman's voice continued.

"Twenty years ago."

The carriage went completely silent.

Joseph dropped his walking stick.

It hit the floor.

Clack.

His face had gone pale.

"No..."

Arjun looked at him.

"You know this train?"

Joseph stared at the dark window.

"I know what happened twenty years ago."

"What happened?"

Joseph slowly looked at the other four passengers.

Then at Arjun.

His voice trembled.

"Five people disappeared."

The train began slowing down.

Ahead, through the darkness, a station sign appeared.

Old.

Broken.

Almost impossible to read.

But Arjun could make out the name.

NORTHFIELD

Joseph whispered:

"That was the last station they ever saw."

The doors began opening.

And somewhere on the empty platform outside---

someone was waiting for them.
