# Chapter 7 — When the Station Woke

The scream came from beneath the tracks.

It wasn't the sound of a person.

It was deeper.

Older.

The entire station shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling as cracks spread across the walls.

Arjun opened his eyes.

His father was gone.

The train was gone.

Even the rain had stopped.

For a moment, there was complete silence.

Then---

12:22 AM.

The clock above the station exploded.

Meera covered her head.

Rohan stumbled backward.

Joseph dropped his walking stick.

Anika stood completely still.

"What's happening?" Rohan shouted.

Joseph looked toward the tracks.

"The station is awake."

A deep rumbling came from below them.

The floor began to split.

Not randomly.

The cracks formed a perfect circle around the five of them.

Arjun looked down.

Something was written between the cracks.

Five names.

ARJUN.

MEERA.

ROHAN.

JOSEPH.

ANIKA.

Then a sixth name slowly appeared.

Arjun's blood ran cold.

NISHA.

His sister.

"No."

He stepped toward it.

"That's impossible."

Anika grabbed his arm.

"Don't touch it."

Arjun stared at the name.

"My sister disappeared twenty years ago."

Joseph picked up his walking stick.

"And the station remembers her."

"She's alive?"

Joseph didn't answer.

Instead, he looked toward the tunnel.

A faint light appeared in the darkness.

Then came footsteps.

Someone was walking toward them.

Slowly.

A woman emerged from the tunnel.

Arjun stopped breathing.

She was about twenty-eight years old.

Dark hair.

Same eyes as his.

Same small birthmark near her left cheek.

Arjun whispered:

"Nisha."

The woman smiled.

"You finally came back."

Meera stepped forward.

"Who are you?"

The woman ignored her.

She looked only at Arjun.

"I waited twenty years."

Arjun couldn't move.

"You were eight."

"I know."

"You disappeared."

"I know."

"How are you still alive?"

Nisha's smile faded.

"I wasn't."

The lights went out.

---

When they came back on, Nisha was standing much closer.

Her clothes were soaked.

Water dripped from her hair onto the floor.

Arjun looked at her carefully.

"You said you weren't alive."

Nisha nodded.

"Not anymore."

Rohan whispered:

"Then what are you?"

Nisha looked at him.

"A memory."

The station trembled again.

Joseph suddenly understood.

"The station didn't keep the people."

He looked at Nisha.

"It kept what was left of them."

Nisha nodded.

"Exactly."

She looked toward the five names.

"Every person who entered Northfield left something behind."

Arjun stared at the floor.

"Then why did the station bring us back?"

Nisha looked at Anika.

"Because she opened the door."

Anika shook her head.

"I didn't."

"You did twenty years ago."

Anika's face changed.

The memory returned.

She remembered standing on the platform.

She remembered her mother.

And she remembered what she had whispered before stepping away from the train.

"I'll come back."

Nisha continued:

"The station has been waiting for her promise to be completed."

Anika whispered:

"What happens if I don't?"

Nisha looked toward the tunnel.

"It opens."

A low sound rolled beneath the station.

Like something breathing.

The tracks began glowing faintly.

Rohan looked at Joseph.

"What exactly is underneath us?"

Joseph stared into the tunnel.

"The reason Northfield was built."

---

The five of them moved toward the edge of the platform.

Below the tracks was no longer a tunnel.

There was a huge underground chamber.

Stone walls.

Hundreds of railway clocks.

Thousands of names.

And in the center---

a black train stood completely still.

It had no windows.

No doors.

No headlights.

Only one thing was visible.

A brass plate on its front.

NORTHFIELD --- FINAL SERVICE

Meera whispered:

"That's not a train."

Joseph shook his head.

"No."

"It's a grave."

The black train suddenly moved.

Not forward.

Backward.

It disappeared into the darkness beneath the station.

Then every clock in the chamber began ticking.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

All at different speeds.

Arjun looked at Nisha.

"What does it want?"

Nisha answered quietly:

"Five memories."

"But we already gave it ours."

"No."

She looked at him.

"You remembered them."

A pause.

"You didn't give them."

Arjun finally understood the difference.

The station could only take what they willingly surrendered.

And they hadn't surrendered anything.

They had simply remembered.

Nisha pointed toward the tunnel.

"That's why it's angry."

The station shook violently.

The five names on the floor began disappearing.

One by one.

ARJUN.

Gone.

MEERA.

Gone.

ROHAN.

Gone.

JOSEPH.

Gone.

Only one remained.

ANIKA.

The station became completely silent.

Anika looked down.

"No."

Nisha stepped toward her.

"You made a promise."

Anika backed away.

"I was a child."

"The station doesn't care."

Arjun stepped between them.

"Then take my memory."

Nisha shook her head.

"It won't work."

"Why?"

"Because you don't understand the rule."

She looked at all five of them.

"The station doesn't want a memory."

Her eyes moved toward the darkness.

"It wants a choice."

The lights flickered.

The black train appeared again.

This time, it was directly behind them.

Its doors opened.

Inside was complete darkness.

Then five voices spoke together.

"One stays."

Anika began crying.

Arjun looked at the train.

Then at his friends.

He finally understood.

The station had never wanted to kill them.

It wanted them to choose who would remain behind.

Just like they had been forced to choose twenty years ago.

And this time---

they would have to choose willingly.

The clock above them began ticking again.

12:23 AM.

The train doors remained open.

Nisha whispered:

"You have one minute."

Then she disappeared.

And the five passengers stood before the train...

knowing that someone had to stay.
