# Chapter 4 — The Five Voices

The laughter stopped.

All at once.

Arjun could still feel the cold around his hand.

But the child's hand was gone.

He looked at Anika.

She was standing perfectly still.

The wet footprints around her feet slowly disappeared into the floorboards.

Rohan whispered,

"What happened?"

Anika didn't answer.

She was staring at something behind them.

Arjun turned.

At the far end of the station stood five children.

They were about eight to twelve years old.

Their clothes were old-fashioned, dusty and completely soaked.

None of them moved.

None of them blinked.

And all five were staring at Arjun.

Meera whispered,

"Those are the children from the poster."

Joseph nodded slowly.

"Yes."

Arjun's eyes moved from one child to another.

Then he recognized himself.

The smallest boy.

Same dark hair.

Same scar above his eyebrow.

The child looked exactly as Arjun remembered himself from old photographs.

Except for one thing.

The boy was crying.

Arjun stepped forward.

"Who are you?"

The child looked at him.

"You know."

Arjun shook his head.

"I don't."

The boy pointed toward Anika.

"Ask her."

Everyone looked at Anika.

She was trembling.

"I don't know anything."

The child smiled.

"You always say that."

Anika suddenly grabbed her head.

A sharp cry escaped her.

"I remember..."

She dropped to her knees.

Images flashed through her mind.

A railway platform.

Rain.

Five children running through the station.

A train arriving at 12:13 AM.

A little girl holding a red scarf.

A boy shouting.

A door opening beneath the platform.

Then---

Darkness.

Anika screamed.

"I remember!"

Arjun rushed toward her.

"What do you remember?"

She looked up at him.

Her eyes were filled with tears.

"I wasn't one of the passengers."

She pointed at the five children.

"I was one of them."

Silence.

Joseph slowly sat down on an old bench.

"Then the fifth passenger..."

Anika nodded.

"Was never supposed to be on the train."

The station clock moved.

12:17 AM.

Seven minutes.

The five children began walking toward them.

Step.

Step.

Step.

Arjun couldn't move.

His younger self stopped directly in front of him.

The child looked up.

"You promised."

Arjun swallowed.

"Promised what?"

"You promised you'd come back."

"I don't remember."

The child's expression became sad.

"You forgot because she made you."

Arjun looked toward Anika.

"Who?"

Before she could answer, every window in the station shattered at once.

Glass exploded across the floor.

The children disappeared.

The lights went out.

And the old telephone began ringing.

Ding.

Ding.

Ding.

Nobody touched it.

The receiver lifted by itself.

A voice came through.

A woman's voice.

"Five passengers."

A pause.

"Five memories."

Another pause.

"One must stay."

The call ended.

Rohan looked terrified.

"What does that mean?"

Joseph stood.

"It means the station wants one of us."

Arjun looked at the clock.

12:18 AM.

Six minutes.

Suddenly, the station doors opened.

Outside was not the abandoned platform.

It was a completely different station.

Bright lights.

Crowds of people.

New trains.

Cars passing beyond the station.

It looked like Northfield twenty years ago.

Meera stared.

"That's impossible."

Joseph whispered:

"No."

He looked at the scene outside.

"That's June 14, 2006."

Arjun stepped toward the doorway.

A train stood outside.

Its doors were open.

Five children were walking toward it.

Arjun recognized them.

The same five children.

His younger self.

Meera's younger self.

Rohan's younger self.

Joseph, twenty years younger.

And Anika.

The memory was playing again.

But this time, Arjun noticed something he hadn't remembered.

There was another person standing on the platform.

An adult woman.

She was holding a red scarf.

Anika stared at her.

"That's my mother."

Arjun looked at her.

"You remember your mother?"

Anika nodded.

"She brought us here."

"Why?"

Anika's face went pale.

"To save us."

The station shook.

The memory outside suddenly stopped.

Everything froze.

The five children stood motionless.

The woman turned her head.

And looked directly at Arjun.

Even though she was part of a memory.

She could see him.

Her lips moved.

Arjun couldn't hear her.

Then she pointed toward Anika.

Anika stepped backward.

"No."

The woman pointed again.

This time, Arjun understood.

Take her back.

The doors slammed shut.

The memory vanished.

The abandoned station returned.

12:19 AM.

Five minutes.

Anika was crying.

"She didn't save us."

Arjun looked at her.

"What did she do?"

Anika slowly raised her eyes.

"She chose me."

A deep rumbling sound came from beneath the floor.

The broken boards began opening again.

This time, there was no darkness beneath them.

There was a railway track.

And on that track---

A train was approaching.

Its headlights appeared in the tunnel.

Joseph whispered:

"The last train."

Rohan looked at the clock.

"Five minutes."

The train horn sounded.

12:20 AM.

Anika looked at Arjun.

"Now I remember why I was smiling."

The train came closer.

She whispered:

"I wasn't happy."

"I was the one who pushed them."

Arjun stared at her.

"What?"

Anika looked toward the approaching train.

"I pushed the other four onto the train."

Her voice broke.

"And then I stayed behind."

The train emerged from the tunnel.

Its doors opened.

Inside were four empty seats.

And one seat occupied by a child wearing a red scarf.

The child slowly lifted her head.

It was Anika.

At eight years old.

She smiled at the adult Anika.

Then she whispered:

"You promised you'd take my place."

Anika stepped toward the train.

Arjun grabbed her wrist.

"No."

She looked back at him.

"If I don't go..."

The train lights flickered.

The four empty seats suddenly filled.

With the ghosts of the children.

All four stared at Arjun.

Then his younger self pointed at him.

"Not her."

Arjun froze.

The child smiled.

"Him."

The clock changed.

12:21 AM.

Four minutes remained.

And the train doors began closing.
