# Chapter 2 — The Station That Remembered

The train slowed.

Not normally.

There was no screech of brakes, no rocking of the carriages, no sound of metal grinding against metal.

It simply became slower.

As if something outside was holding it back.

Arjun looked through the window.

The darkness outside had changed.

There were no buildings anymore.

No roads.

No lights.

Only trees.

Tall, thin trees standing close together, their branches bending toward the tracks.

Then the train passed a rusted sign.

NORTHFIELD

Arjun's stomach tightened.

The station ahead looked abandoned.

The platform was cracked and covered in weeds. Old lamps stood along its edge, but none of them worked.

Yet someone was standing beneath one.

A man.

He was completely still.

The train continued moving toward him.

"Is that the station?" Meera asked.

Nobody answered.

Rohan closed his laptop.

"I thought Northfield was demolished."

Joseph stared at the platform.

"It was."

Anika suddenly stood up.

"We shouldn't get off."

Her voice was quiet.

Arjun looked at her.

"Why?"

She didn't answer.

The train finally stopped.

12:13 AM.

The doors opened.

Cold air entered the carriage.

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Then the man on the platform raised his head.

Arjun could finally see his face.

He was wearing an old railway uniform.

His clothes were covered in dust.

And around his neck hung a metal identification badge.

He walked toward the train.

Slowly.

Step.

Step.

Step.

He stopped directly in front of their carriage.

Then he looked inside.

At all five passengers.

His expression changed.

Not fear.

Recognition.

"You came back," he whispered.

Joseph gripped his walking stick.

"I've never been here before."

The railway worker smiled.

"Not you."

His eyes moved to Meera.

Then Rohan.

Then Anika.

Finally, they stopped on Arjun.

"None of you remember."

Arjun felt a chill run down his spine.

"Remember what?"

The man stepped closer.

"You were told there would be five."

Anika's face went pale.

"We are five."

The man looked behind them.

At the empty seats.

Then he whispered:

"No."

Everyone turned.

There was a sixth seat.

Empty.

But the seat was wet.

As if someone had been sitting there in the rain.

A single drop of water rolled down the window beside it.

Then another.

Drip.

Drip.

Drip.

Arjun stared at the empty seat.

The same cold feeling from the reflection returned.

Someone had been there.

Someone was still there.

The railway worker stepped backward.

"Get off."

Joseph shook his head.

"Why?"

The worker's expression became serious.

"Because this train doesn't stop here for passengers."

"Then why did it stop?" Rohan asked.

The man didn't answer.

Instead, he looked at the clock hanging above the platform.

It showed 12:13.

Then he looked at them.

"The station is only open for thirteen minutes."

Meera frowned.

"What happens after that?"

The man whispered:

"It leaves again."

"And where does it go?"

The railway worker looked directly at Arjun.

"Back to the night you died."

Silence.

Arjun's heart stopped.

"What?"

The man didn't repeat himself.

He turned and began walking away.

"Wait!" Arjun shouted.

The man stopped.

Without turning around, he said:

"You have twelve minutes."

Then he disappeared into the darkness.

---

Nobody spoke.

Finally, Rohan stood.

"I'm getting off."

Anika grabbed his arm.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because he said we have twelve minutes."

Rohan pulled his arm away.

"Exactly. We should find out what's happening."

Meera looked toward the open doors.

"I agree."

Joseph remained seated.

Arjun looked at him.

"You knew something."

Joseph slowly raised his eyes.

"I knew the name."

"What name?"

"Northfield."

He swallowed.

"Twenty years ago, five people disappeared from this station."

Arjun remembered the announcement.

Five passengers aboard.

His voice became quieter.

"And they were never found?"

Joseph shook his head.

"Not until tonight."

Arjun frowned.

"What do you mean?"

Joseph pointed toward the platform.

"They're waiting for us."

Meera stepped down first.

Her shoes touched the cracked platform.

Nothing happened.

Rohan followed.

Then Anika.

Joseph carefully climbed down.

Arjun was the last.

The moment his foot touched the platform---

The train doors slammed shut.

"Wait!"

Arjun spun around.

The train began moving.

"Open the doors!"

He ran alongside it.

The windows passed him one by one.

Meera's face.

Rohan's face.

Joseph.

Anika.

Then the last carriage.

Arjun stopped.

Something was wrong.

There had been five of them.

But now, through the final window, he could see six people sitting inside.

Five were facing forward.

The sixth was sitting beside the window.

A woman.

Long black hair.

Head lowered.

Red scarf.

Arjun stared.

Anika was standing beside him.

She wasn't wearing a red scarf.

The woman inside slowly lifted her head.

Arjun couldn't see her face.

Because she was looking directly at him.

Then she raised one finger to her lips.

Don't speak.

The train vanished into the darkness.

Arjun stood frozen.

Meera came closer.

"Arjun?"

He didn't answer.

"What's wrong?"

He looked at Anika.

"Where did you get that scarf?"

She stared at him.

"What scarf?"

Arjun pointed.

Anika looked down.

She was wearing a red scarf.

Her face went completely white.

"I wasn't wearing this before."

Nobody moved.

Then Joseph whispered:

"Don't touch it."

A distant sound came from somewhere inside the abandoned station.

Ding.

Like an old telephone.

Then again.

Ding.

Rohan looked around.

"Where's that coming from?"

Joseph pointed toward the station building.

"The ticket office."

They walked toward it.

The building was covered in vines.

Most of the windows were broken.

Above the entrance was an old sign:

NORTHFIELD STATION --- EST. 1897

Inside, dust covered everything.

There were old benches.

A ticket counter.

A dead clock.

And a telephone sitting on the counter.

It was ringing.

Ding.

Ding.

Ding.

Meera stared at it.

"Should we answer?"

Joseph immediately said:

"No."

The phone stopped.

Everyone relaxed.

Then it rang again.

But this time---

It wasn't coming from the counter.

It was coming from Anika's pocket.

She slowly pulled out her phone.

The screen was black.

Yet the phone was ringing.

She answered.

"Hello?"

Nothing.

Then a voice whispered through the speaker.

A woman's voice.

Terrified.

"There's only four of you."

Anika's eyes widened.

The voice continued.

"One of you isn't real."

The call ended.

Nobody spoke.

Arjun looked at the other four.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

Five passengers.

One of them wasn't real.

Then the station clock suddenly started moving.

The hands jumped forward.

12:14.

Joseph looked toward the entrance.

"We have eleven minutes."

A loud metallic sound echoed from somewhere beneath the station.

CLANG.

Then another.

CLANG.

Something was coming.

From underground.

And this time---

It sounded like footsteps.
