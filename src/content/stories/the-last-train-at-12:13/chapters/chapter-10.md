# Chapter 10 — 12:13

The next morning, Arjun called everyone.

Meera answered first.

Then Rohan.

Then Anika.

Joseph was the last.

None of them asked why.

They already knew.

At exactly 11:30 PM, the five of them stood outside Northfield Station.

The station had been abandoned for years.

But tonight, every light was working.

The clock above the entrance showed:

12:12 AM.

Anika looked at Arjun.

"Are you sure about this?"

"No."

She almost smiled.

"Good."

Arjun looked at the others.

"Whatever happens, nobody goes anywhere alone."

Joseph nodded.

"And nobody gets on the train."

A bell rang.

Ding.

The clock changed.

12:13 AM.

The station doors opened.

A train waited on Platform 6.

Its lights were on.

Its doors were open.

And someone was standing inside.

Rajiv.

Arjun's father.

He looked through the doorway.

"Come inside."

Joseph immediately shook his head.

"No."

Rajiv looked at him.

"You know what happens if you refuse."

Joseph stepped forward.

"I know."

"Then you know there is no other way."

Arjun looked at his father.

"What happens if we get on?"

Rajiv didn't answer.

Instead, he pointed toward the seats.

Five seats.

Four were occupied.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

All four were asleep.

Arjun looked at them.

"Then who's the fifth?"

Rajiv looked directly at him.

"You."

Arjun felt the cold return.

"But I'm standing here."

Rajiv nodded.

"That's the problem."

A figure appeared behind Arjun.

His younger self.

Eight years old.

The boy whispered:

"You promised you'd come back."

Arjun turned.

The boy was holding Nisha's hand.

Then Arjun finally remembered everything.

The night in 2006.

The train.

The five children.

Anika's mother.

His sister.

And the truth.

There had never been five passengers.

There had been six.

Arjun.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

And Nisha.

Nisha had been the first passenger.

She had boarded the train willingly to save the others.

But before the train left, young Arjun had followed her.

He had stepped onto the train.

His mother had pulled him back.

The station took his memory instead.

Nisha remained.

And twenty years later, the station had been trying to recreate that missing sixth passenger.

That's why it kept saying:

Five passengers aboard.

It wasn't counting the people.

It was counting the memories.

Arjun looked at his father.

"You knew."

Rajiv nodded.

"I tried to keep you away."

"And Nisha?"

Rajiv looked down.

"She was the first conductor."

Arjun's eyes widened.

"Then why did you become the conductor?"

"Because she couldn't remember who she was anymore."

Rajiv's voice broke.

"I took her place."

The train began to shake.

The sleeping passengers opened their eyes.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

All four looked at Arjun.

Anika whispered:

"We remember."

The station lights turned red.

The train doors began closing.

Rajiv shouted:

"Arjun! You have to choose!"

Arjun looked at his father.

"Choose what?"

"Your memory..."

Rajiv pointed toward the train.

"...or your life."

Arjun understood.

The station wanted him to surrender the memory of Nisha.

If he did, the train would disappear.

But Nisha would disappear with it.

If he refused---

the train would remain.

And eventually return for all of them.

Arjun closed his eyes.

He remembered his sister laughing.

He remembered holding her hand.

He remembered promising to come back.

Then he opened his eyes.

"I choose neither."

Rajiv stared.

"What?"

Arjun stepped toward the train.

"I won't give it my memory."

He looked at the other four.

"And I won't leave anyone behind."

Meera understood.

She stood.

"We choose together."

Rohan stood beside her.

"So do I."

Joseph lifted his walking stick.

"After twenty years..."

He smiled.

"...I'm tired of running."

Anika took Arjun's hand.

"I'm staying with you."

The train screamed.

The station shook.

Rajiv looked at them.

Then, slowly, he smiled.

"That's the answer."

The station suddenly went silent.

The clock stopped.

12:13 AM.

The train doors opened.

Nisha stepped out.

She looked exactly as Arjun remembered her.

Eight years old.

She walked toward him.

"You remembered."

Arjun knelt.

"I'm sorry."

Nisha shook her head.

"You came back."

She touched his face.

"That's all that mattered."

Behind her, the train began disappearing.

Rajiv's body started fading too.

Arjun stood.

"Dad!"

Rajiv smiled.

"You don't need a conductor anymore."

He looked toward Nisha.

"Take care of your brother."

Nisha nodded.

Rajiv disappeared.

The train vanished.

The station became dark.

Then the first light of morning appeared beyond the trees.

For the first time in twenty years---

Northfield Station was completely empty.

No train.

No voices.

No footsteps.

Only five people standing on the platform.

Arjun.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

Anika.

And one memory that finally felt like a goodbye.

---

One Year Later

Northfield Station was demolished.

A small memorial was built where the platform had once stood.

Five names were carved into it.

But there was something unusual.

Under the five names was a sixth.

NISHA MEHRA

Arjun visited the memorial every year.

He never forgot her.

He never forgot the station.

And he never forgot the rule:

The station can only take what you willingly give it.

One night, exactly one year after Northfield closed, Arjun stood beside the memorial.

His phone suddenly vibrated.

He looked down.

No number.

No message.

Just the time.

12:13 AM.

Arjun stared at the screen.

Then another notification appeared.

A single sentence:

"The train has arrived."

He slowly looked toward the old railway tracks.

There was nothing there.

No train.

No lights.

Nothing.

Then---

from somewhere far beyond the darkness---

came the sound of a distant railway horn.

Arjun smiled.

Because this time, he wasn't afraid.

He knew the truth.

Northfield was gone.

The train was gone.

And Nisha was finally free.

Arjun turned away.

Behind him, the old station clock---removed during demolition---ticked once.

12:14 AM.

And stopped.

THE END
