# Chapter 8 — The Choice

The train doors remained open.

Beyond them was nothing.

No seats.

No windows.

No lights.

Only darkness.

The clock above the platform showed:

12:23 AM.

One minute.

Anika stood closest to the train.

Arjun moved beside her.

"Nobody is staying."

Joseph looked at him.

"That's not how this works."

"Then we'll change how it works."

Rohan stared into the darkness.

"How?"

Arjun didn't know.

But he knew one thing.

Twenty years ago, they had been children.

They had been frightened.

They had forgotten.

And the station had used that fear against them.

This time, they remembered.

Meera stepped forward.

"We choose together."

The station trembled.

The lights flickered.

A voice came from inside the train.

"One stays."

Meera raised her voice.

"No."

The voice repeated:

"One stays."

Rohan shook his head.

"We're not choosing anyone."

The darkness inside the train seemed to move.

Then five figures appeared inside.

Their younger selves.

Five children.

Arjun's younger self stood at the front.

He looked at the adults.

"You already chose."

Arjun stepped closer.

"When?"

The child pointed toward Anika.

"When she stayed."

Anika lowered her head.

"I remember."

She stepped toward the train.

"I made the choice twenty years ago."

Arjun grabbed her hand.

"Then we'll make a different one tonight."

Anika looked at him.

"What if it needs someone?"

Arjun smiled faintly.

"Then it can take all of us."

Joseph suddenly laughed.

Everyone looked at him.

"What?"

He shook his head.

"That's it."

"What?"

"The station has always made us believe there were only two choices."

He pointed toward the train.

"Stay or leave."

His eyes moved toward the five children.

"But there is another choice."

Meera understood.

"Refuse."

Joseph nodded.

"The station has no power unless we choose what it offers."

The train began shaking.

The voice returned.

"One stays."

Arjun shouted:

"No one stays."

The clock changed.

12:24 AM.

The station screamed.

The train doors began closing.

Anika suddenly pulled away from Arjun.

"Wait!"

She reached into her pocket.

She still had the red scarf.

The same scarf from twenty years ago.

She looked at it for a long moment.

Then she smiled.

"I know what I gave it."

Arjun frowned.

"What?"

"My memory."

She held the scarf tightly.

"The station took my memories because I was afraid."

She looked at the train.

"So I'll give it something else."

"What?"

Anika stepped onto the train.

Arjun's heart dropped.

"Anika!"

She turned around.

"I'm not staying."

She placed the red scarf on the floor.

"I'm giving it back what belongs to it."

The train became completely silent.

The scarf slowly disappeared into the darkness.

Then---

nothing happened.

Anika stepped back onto the platform.

The station clock stopped.

12:24 AM.

Rohan stared.

"Did it work?"

Joseph looked at the tunnel.

"No."

The station began cracking.

"But it broke the rule."

A deep sound came from beneath them.

The underground chamber appeared again.

Hundreds of clocks stopped simultaneously.

Then every name carved into the walls began disappearing.

One after another.

The station was losing its memories.

Nisha appeared at the end of the platform.

She was smiling.

Arjun ran toward her.

"Nisha!"

She looked at him.

"You remembered me."

Arjun nodded.

"I'll never forget you again."

Nisha smiled.

"That's why I can leave."

Arjun stopped.

"What?"

She pointed toward the tracks.

The black train was waiting.

Its doors were open.

But this time, there was light inside.

Warm morning light.

Nisha stepped toward it.

Arjun's eyes filled with tears.

"Are you coming back?"

She shook her head.

"No."

"Then where are you going?"

"Home."

She smiled.

"For the first time."

She stepped onto the train.

The doors closed.

The train began moving.

Slowly.

Then faster.

And finally---

it disappeared.

The station became silent.

The cracks closed.

The underground chamber vanished.

The old walls faded.

The abandoned platform became ordinary again.

The clock above the station changed.

12:25 AM.

The first light of dawn appeared beyond the trees.

Meera looked around.

"Is it over?"

Joseph picked up his walking stick.

"I think so."

Rohan looked at Arjun.

"Think?"

Joseph smiled.

"Nothing involving Northfield is ever certain."

Arjun looked at Anika.

She was staring at the red scarf.

It was gone.

For the first time, she wasn't wearing it.

"How do you feel?" Arjun asked.

Anika thought for a moment.

"Like I finally woke up."

They walked toward the station exit.

Behind them, the old railway clock suddenly moved backward.

12:24.

Then forward.

12:25.

Nobody noticed.

Except Arjun.

He turned around.

The station was empty.

Completely empty.

But on the platform wall, someone had written four words:

THE LAST TRAIN RETURNS.

Arjun stared at them.

Then the words slowly disappeared.

Epilogue

Three months later, Northfield Station was demolished.

The railway department claimed the structure was unsafe.

No one mentioned the old records.

No one mentioned the five missing people from 2006.

And no one remembered the train.

Except five people.

Arjun.

Meera.

Rohan.

Joseph.

And Anika.

They stayed in contact.

Sometimes they talked about that night.

Sometimes they didn't.

Life slowly became normal.

Until one evening, Arjun received a message from an unknown number.

There was no text.

Only a photograph.

Not an old photograph.

Not a memory.

A live picture.

It showed a railway platform.

A clock.

12:13 AM.

And five empty seats.

Arjun's phone displayed the message time.

12:13 AM.

Then another message arrived.

Four words.

"You forgot the conductor."

Arjun stared at the screen.

His father's watch was lying beside him.

The watch hadn't worked in twenty years.

Its hands suddenly began moving.

12:13.

12:14.

12:15.

Then stopped.

Arjun looked toward his bedroom window.

Across the street, beneath a broken streetlight, stood an old railway worker.

Watching him.

The man slowly raised his hand.

And pointed behind Arjun.

Arjun turned.

Nobody was there.

When he looked back---

the railway worker had disappeared.

Only one thing remained beneath the streetlight.

An old metal badge.

Arjun walked closer.

He picked it up.

His hands began shaking.

The name engraved on it was:

RAJIV MEHRA

His father's name.

On the back, four words were carved:

NEXT TRAIN --- 12:13.

Arjun looked at the clock.

12:12 AM.

One minute remained.

The End.
