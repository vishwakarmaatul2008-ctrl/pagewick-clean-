# Chapter 2: The Restricted Stacks

The archive wing smelled like every old building Nell had ever been in — dust, cold stone, and the particular mustiness of paper that had been breathing the same air for a century. Idris led her past the public reading room without slowing, through a door marked STAFF ONLY that he unlocked with a key on a chain around his neck, and into a narrow room lined floor to ceiling with ledgers.

"You have a key to the restricted archive," Nell said. "As a student."

"I have a key because Professor Calloway trusts me not to burn the place down." Idris pulled a thin, cloth-bound volume from a shelf marked 1930–1940, blowing a fine layer of dust off the top. "And because I asked him about the ninth bell two years ago, and he decided it was easier to let me look than to keep answering questions."

"Two years ago? Why were you asking two years ago?"

"Because my sister went to this school." Idris set the ledger down on the reading table and didn't look up. "She graduated the year before I started. She told me about the bell the summer before she left — said the older students used to talk about it like a ghost story. I didn't think about it again until I got here and started reading things I wasn't supposed to."

He opened the ledger to a page marked with a strip of faded ribbon. Nell leaned over his shoulder. The handwriting was cramped, formal, the ink gone the color of weak tea.

*October 14th. The ninth toll was heard by no fewer than eleven witnesses at half past ten in the evening. The Headmaster has instructed that the matter not be discussed with the younger students, and that the tower be inspected at first light.*

*October 23rd. Miss Sabine Holt, third form, has not been seen since retiring last night. Her room was found locked from within. A search of the grounds has yielded nothing. The Headmaster wishes it recorded that Miss Holt was known to be a troubled and solitary girl, prone to wandering, and that no foul play is suspected.*

"Nine days," Nell said quietly. "Bell to disappearance."

"Nine days, exactly, every time." Idris turned the page. "This isn't the only entry. There are five more, going back to when the school was founded in 1834. Always the same pattern. The bell rings once, out of sequence. Nine days pass. Someone vanishes. And every single time, the official record calls it a runaway, or an accident, or — my personal favorite — 'a nervous disposition.'"

"That's not a coincidence. That's a pattern someone's covering up."

"That's what I thought too." He finally looked at her, and for the first time since the courtyard, something like relief crossed his face — the relief of a person who has been carrying a secret alone and has just, cautiously, set part of it down. "I've been trying to figure out who gets chosen. There's no obvious connection between the six names I've found. Different years, different families, different everything. Scholarship students and legacy students both. Popular ones and ones nobody noticed."

"Then how do you stop it if you don't know the pattern?"

"I don't know that you can stop it." Idris closed the ledger gently, like it might bruise. "I know that every year it doesn't ring, nothing happens. And every year it does, someone disappears exactly nine days later, and the abbey makes very sure that the story dies before the next intake arrives to ask questions." He met her eyes. "Which means whatever's happening, the school knows more than they're telling. And I intend to find out what, before the ninth day."

"Why does it matter to you this much?" Nell asked. "It rang last night. Nobody knows who it's for yet. It could be anyone."

Idris was quiet for a long moment, his jaw tight in a way that made Nell wonder if she'd asked the wrong question.

"Because the year my sister graduated," he said finally, "the bell rang too. Nobody in her year went missing. She always said it felt like waiting for a verdict that never came — like something had reached for her and missed." He slid the ledger back onto its shelf with careful precision. "I want to know why it missed her. And I want to know who it isn't going to miss this time."
