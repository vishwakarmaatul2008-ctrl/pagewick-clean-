# Chapter 6: Eva

Calloway's face went the color of old paper when Nell said the name.

"Eva Marchetti," he said, sitting down heavily behind his desk, as though the weight of the name had physically pushed him into the chair. "You found that in the chapel."

"You know her," Idris said. It wasn't a question.

"I taught her." Calloway's hands, folded on the desk, weren't quite steady. "Four years ago. Brightest student I've had in three decades — the kind who makes you remember why you started teaching in the first place. The bell rang in her second week at Thorne, same as it's rung for your friend. And when nine days came and passed and nothing happened, I thought — foolishly, I see now — that the bargain had simply skipped a cycle."

"But it didn't skip," Nell said. "She went down there. Into the chapel."

"I didn't know that at the time. I've spent four years assuming she was simply one of the rare ones the bell marked and then let go." Calloway's voice cracked slightly on the last word. "She graduated. She wrote to me twice after leaving — ordinary letters, university gossip, nothing troubling. And then the letters stopped, and I told myself people simply drift, the way students do, and I didn't let myself think about it further because the alternative was unbearable."

"What's the alternative?" Idris asked, though his expression said he already knew.

"That she went down to that chapel and tried to bargain on someone else's behalf — the way the old book implies is possible — and that whatever agreement she struck bought her four years instead of nine days, and that the bill has simply not come due until now." Calloway looked up, and for the first time since Nell had met him, he looked less like a teacher and more like an old man carrying something he'd never wanted to carry alone. "The ninth toll you heard in the chapel. I think that was Eva's marker calling in, not a new summons. I think whatever she bought with her bargain has just run out."

"Then she's still out there," Nell said. "We could find her. Ask her what she did — how she bargained—"

"If she bargained the way I suspect she did," Calloway said quietly, "then finding her won't help you, because whatever's coming for Priya was always coming for someone. Eva didn't break the arrangement. She rerouted it. Delayed it. Traded her own nine days for four years, and in doing so, likely traded someone else's certainty for uncertainty — passed the debt forward without ever actually cancelling it." He rubbed a hand over his face. "It's not a loophole, Miss Ashworth. It's a deferral. And deferred debts, in my experience, always collect interest."

Idris had gone very quiet, staring at the fresh scar in Calloway's map where E.V.A.'s name had been added to the chapel floor.

"So the only real choice," he said slowly, "is who pays, and when. Not whether."

"That has always been the shape of this bargain," Calloway said. "Edmund Thorne didn't build an escape hatch when he made his arrangement, Mr. Vane. He built a very long, very patient collection system, dressed up in enough ceremony that each generation could convince itself they'd found a clever way out." He closed his eyes briefly. "I am sorry. I had genuinely hoped, for four years, that Eva Marchetti had simply been the exception. I would have told you both something very different a week ago."

Nell looked between the two of them — the old man who'd spent four years quietly grieving a hope he'd never confirmed was false, and Idris, whose jaw had set in the same hard line she'd seen the first night in the courtyard.

"Then we don't defer it," she said. "We end it. Whatever's actually owed down there, we find out exactly what it is, and we finish what Eva started instead of just repeating it."

"That," Calloway said, "is considerably easier said than done. But it is, for what it's worth, the first thing anyone in ninety years has actually suggested doing."

Outside, the light was already fading toward evening. Two days remained until the ninth day arrived for Priya Okonkwo, and none of them, Nell realized, actually knew yet what it would take to make sure it didn't.
