# Chapter 7: The Ninth Day

Priya didn't remember walking to the tower.

She told Nell that afterward, in fragments, the way people describe dreams they're still half inside of — one moment she'd been in her room, the light going gold through the curtains as evening settled over the quad, and the next she was standing at the clocktower door with her hand already on the handle, no memory of the distance in between.

Nell found her because she'd been watching. She and Idris had taken turns for two days, trading shifts outside Priya's door under the thin excuse of studying together, waiting for the moment Calloway had warned them would come — the moment the pattern stopped being a story and became something happening in real time. When Priya's door opened at half past nine, silent, her expression distant and strangely peaceful, Nell was three steps behind her before she'd even finished pulling on her shoes.

"Priya." She caught up at the base of the tower stairs, gripping her arm. Priya turned, and her eyes, when they focused on Nell, took a full second longer than they should have.

"I have to go down," Priya said, her voice perfectly calm, which was somehow worse than if she'd been afraid. "It's time. I don't — I don't really want to, but it's time."

"It's not time. It's not anything. You're coming with me, right now—"

"Nell." Idris's voice, low and urgent, from the tower doorway. He'd beaten them there, torch already lit, and behind him the stairwell breathed cold air up into the night the same way it had the night they'd found the chapel. "It's already started. Listen."

Far below, faint but unmistakable, came the sound of the ninth bell — not from above, where the mechanism hung silent in its frame, but from underneath them, rising up through solid stone like something calling its debt home.

"We go down together," Nell said, keeping her grip on Priya's arm even as the other girl's steps toward the stairs grew more insistent, less her own. "Whatever's happening, you're not doing this alone, and you're not doing it as payment. Do you understand me? We're going to find another way."

The chapel, when they reached it, had changed. The empty basin at its center was no longer empty — a thin, dark liquid had risen in it since Nell's last visit, and the carved symbols along the walls seemed to pulse faintly in time with something Nell felt more than heard, a low pressure behind her sternum that built with every step deeper into the room.

Priya walked straight to the basin's edge, and this time, when Nell reached for her, her hand passed through empty air where Priya's arm should have been — not through nothing, exactly, but through resistance, like the space around her had thickened into something that didn't want to be interrupted.

"She's not really here," Idris said, his voice tight. "Not all the way. Look."

Priya's outline had gone faintly indistinct at the edges, the way a reflection blurs on disturbed water. She was looking down into the basin with the same distant calm, murmuring something Nell couldn't make out, and with every word, the pulse of pressure in the room built higher.

"Eva bargained," Nell said, thinking fast, grabbing at everything Calloway had told them. "She didn't refuse. She didn't fight it. She *changed the terms.* Priya — Priya, listen to me — you don't have to accept what it's offering. You can ask for something different."

"It doesn't want a different offer," Priya said, and her voice, when it came, had an odd double quality to it, as if two voices were speaking a half-second out of sync. "It wants what it's owed. That's all it's ever wanted."

"Then we give it something else owed," Idris said suddenly, stepping forward with the torn page from Calloway's book still clutched in his hand. "Nell — the seventh name. Eva didn't erase her debt. She rerouted it to someone unnamed, someone the arrangement hasn't actually claimed yet. If that's true, there's still an uncollected debt sitting out there from four years ago, separate from whatever it thinks it's owed tonight."

"You're saying we tell it to collect from someone who isn't here?"

"I'm saying the arrangement is old, and rigid, and rule-bound in exactly the way Calloway described," Idris said, already crouching to add words to the stone with the edge of the torn page, tracing what symbols he recognized with desperate, careful precision. "If Eva's debt is still technically open, and we can make the terms clear enough, it may take the older, larger claim over the newer one. It's not mercy. It's just math it can't refuse."

Priya's murmuring had grown louder, more insistent, and the basin's dark surface had begun to ripple without anything touching it.

"Do it," Nell said. "Whatever you're doing, do it now."
