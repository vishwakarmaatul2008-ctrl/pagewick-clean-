# Chapter 8: What the Bell Owes

The pressure in the room broke like a held breath finally released.

One moment the basin's surface was rippling, black and restless, and Priya's outline blurring further at the edges with every passing second — and the next, the moment Idris pressed the torn page flat against the stone floor and spoke Eva Marchetti's full name aloud into the dark, everything stopped at once. The pulse behind Nell's sternum vanished. The carved symbols along the walls stilled. And Priya, released so suddenly she staggered, fell forward into Nell's arms, solid and present and entirely, ordinarily herself.

"What—" Priya's voice cracked, disoriented, her eyes finally clear and afraid in the way they should have been afraid all along. "What just happened? Where are we?"

"You're safe," Nell said, holding on to her, hardly believing it herself yet. "You're okay."

Idris hadn't moved from where he knelt by the basin, watching the dark liquid inside it slowly, impossibly, begin to recede — not draining away, exactly, but simply becoming less, the way a debt looks smaller once someone else has agreed to carry it instead. He let out a breath that shook slightly on its way out.

"It took the older claim," he said. "Calloway was right. It's rigid. It doesn't care who owes it, only that the ledger balances." He looked up at Nell, something between relief and grief on his face. "Which means somewhere, right now, this is happening to Eva. Wherever she is. Whatever she's been doing for the last four years, thinking she'd gotten away clean."

"We didn't save her," Nell said quietly. "We just moved it back onto her."

"We didn't create the debt, Ashworth. She rerouted her own nine days onto someone she never met, four years ago, because she wanted to live. I understand why she did it. I might have done the same thing, in her place." Idris stood, carefully, still watching the basin. "But I'm not going to pretend that makes it fair. It just makes it the only thing we had time to do tonight, with someone's actual life on the floor in front of us."

They didn't speak much on the walk back up. Priya, still shaking, didn't ask the questions Nell knew were coming — not yet, not that night — and Nell was grateful for the silence, because she didn't yet know how she'd answer them.

---

Calloway was waiting in his office when they returned, as though he'd known, somehow, that this would be the night it resolved one way or another. He listened to the whole account without interrupting, his expression growing heavier with every detail, and when Idris finished, the old man sat for a long moment with his eyes closed.

"I will try to find her," he said finally. "Eva. If there's any way to warn her, to help her the way you helped Priya tonight — I owe her that much, for four years of not asking the right questions." He looked between the two of them, something like gratitude and grief mixed together in his weathered face. "You did something in that chapel that no one at this school has managed in nearly a century. I want you to understand that clearly, whatever guilt you're both currently carrying about how you did it."

"It's not over, is it," Nell said. It wasn't really a question. "The arrangement's still there. The chapel's still there. Next year, or the year after, it'll ring again."

"Yes," Calloway said, with the particular honesty of a man who had decided, finally, to stop protecting people from the truth. "It will. Edmund Thorne built something that doesn't end because three students had a very brave, very frightening night. But you've proven something important tonight — that the terms can be argued. That the ledger isn't as fixed as everyone before you assumed." He managed something almost like a smile. "That's not nothing, Miss Ashworth. In fact, I suspect it's the beginning of considerably more homework than either of you were counting on."

Outside, across the dark quad, the clocktower stood exactly as it always had — four pale faces, patient and unreadable, giving no sign that anything inside it had changed. But when the bells rang ten o'clock that night, Nell found herself counting anyway, the way she suspected she'd count for the rest of her time at Thorne Abbey, waiting to see whether eight would, one day, stop being enough.

**THE END**

*(Or, perhaps, the beginning — of whatever Nell, Idris, and Calloway decide to do about Eva Marchetti, and about a bargain two hundred years old that has never once, until tonight, been made to answer for itself.)*
