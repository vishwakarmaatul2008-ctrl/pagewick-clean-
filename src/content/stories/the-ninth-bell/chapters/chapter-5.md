# Chapter 5: What the Tower Remembers

They found the chapel door on the fifth night, exactly where Calloway's map said it would be — beneath the clocktower, past a maintenance passage that hadn't been on any official floor plan Nell had seen, behind a wall of stone that looked solid until Idris ran his fingers along a seam too straight to be natural.

"It's not locked," he said, surprised. "It's just... heavy."

Together they pushed it open. Cold air rolled out, carrying a smell like wet stone and something else beneath it, faint and metallic, that Nell didn't want to think too hard about. Beyond the door, a narrow stair spiraled down into dark that swallowed the light of Idris's torch after only a few steps.

"Priya's dream," Nell said quietly. "Standing at the bottom of stairs. Something coming down from the top."

"Then let's hope we're going the right direction." Idris started down, and after a breath, Nell followed.

The chapel at the bottom was smaller than she'd expected — a single round room, its walls carved with symbols that predated anything Christian, worn nearly smooth by time. In the center stood a stone basin, empty, and around its rim, cut into the floor in a spiral that matched the stairs above, was writing in a language Nell didn't recognize but that made something ancient and uneasy stir in the back of her skull anyway.

"Six names," Idris murmured, crouching by the basin's edge. He'd brought a torn page copied from Calloway's book, and he held it up to compare. "Sabine Holt. The others from the ledger. They're all here, carved into the stone, like a record." His finger stopped on a seventh line, the carving fresher than the rest, the edges sharper. "This one's new. Recent. Within the last few years."

"Whose name?"

Idris angled the torch closer, and his face went very still.

"It's not a full name," he said slowly. "Just three letters. E-V-A."

"That doesn't help us."

"No. But it tells us something." He sat back on his heels, staring at the carving like it might rearrange itself into an answer if he looked hard enough. "This is fresh. The bell hasn't rung in ninety-one years, according to the ledgers — but something added a name to this floor more recently than that. Which means either the ledgers are wrong, or the bell has rung since 1934 and nobody wrote it down."

"Or someone made sure it wasn't written down." Nell moved further into the chapel, her torch sweeping across symbols that seemed to shift at the edge of her vision, and stopped at a second basin she hadn't noticed at first, set into an alcove behind the first. Unlike the outer one, this basin wasn't empty. A thin layer of dark liquid pooled at its bottom, undisturbed, as if it had been sitting there for a very long time, waiting.

"Idris."

He crossed to her side, and for a moment neither of them spoke.

"Calloway said the bargain could be renegotiated," Idris said finally, "by someone the tower chose to listen to. I think this is where that happens. I think this room is the actual mechanism — not the bell, not the tower. This." He gestured at the basin, careful not to touch it. "And I think whatever E.V.A. was, they came down here to try, and either it didn't work, or it worked in a way that isn't written down anywhere Calloway's ever found."

Somewhere far above them, muffled by two centuries of stone, a bell began to toll.

Nell froze, counting instinctively, her heart climbing into her throat with every strike. Six. Seven. Eight.

The ninth came a full ten seconds later than it should have — later, and closer, as though it were no longer ringing from the tower above them at all, but from somewhere in the dark beyond the chapel's carved walls, somewhere that hadn't existed a moment before.

"It's not supposed to ring again," Idris said, his voice tight. "Not until the cycle resets. Not for another year."

"Then either the rules just changed," Nell said, backing toward the stairs, "or we already broke one by coming down here." She grabbed his sleeve, pulling him toward the exit. "Whatever it is, we need to get Priya, and we need to figure out what E.V.A. did — before the tower decides we're worth adding to that floor too."
