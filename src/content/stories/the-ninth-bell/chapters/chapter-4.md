# Chapter 4: The Founder's Bargain

Professor Calloway's office was the one room in the archive wing that felt lived-in — a coal fire, a cracked leather chair, stacks of papers arranged in an order only he understood. He'd taught history at Thorne Abbey for thirty years, and when Idris knocked and asked, without preamble, whether he knew anything about a bargain made when the school was founded, the old man didn't laugh, or ask why, or send them away. He simply got up, locked his office door, and reached for a book on the highest shelf that Nell hadn't noticed was there.

"I wondered how long it would take one of you to ask the right question," Calloway said, setting the book down. It was older than anything Nell had seen in the restricted stacks — bound in cracked leather, no title on the spine. "Most students who go digging stop at the ledgers. They find the pattern and lose their nerve before they find the reason."

"You knew," Nell said. "This whole time, you knew, and you let us keep looking."

"I let you keep looking because I couldn't tell you outright. There are — obligations, attached to this knowledge, that I am not free to break on my own initiative. But nothing stops me from letting you find your own way to the door." He opened the book to a page near the front, a hand-drawn map of the abbey grounds as they'd looked before the clocktower existed. "Thorne Abbey was built on the site of an older structure. A chapel, technically, though what it actually housed was considerably less holy. The founder, Edmund Thorne, made an arrangement with whatever was underneath it, in exchange for the land staying fertile, the buildings staying standing, and his family's fortune staying intact through two wars and a depression that ruined everyone around him."

"What kind of arrangement?" Idris asked, though something in his voice suggested he already suspected the answer.

"The kind that requires paying rent." Calloway turned the page, revealing a second drawing — the same grounds, but with the clocktower now sketched in, and a small, deliberate mark at its base. "One soul, every generation, taken quietly enough that no one asks too many questions. The bell isn't a warning system built to help you, Miss Ashworth. It's a summons. It rings when the arrangement comes due, and it tells whatever's owed that payment is nine days out."

"That's monstrous," Nell said. "You've known this for years and done *nothing*?"

"I have done what one man quietly reading old books is capable of doing, which is very little against an arrangement written into the stones of the building I work in." Calloway's voice was tired rather than defensive. "I've tried, twice, to find a way to break it. Both times, the trail went cold at the same place — the chapel beneath the tower, which has been sealed since before I was born, and which no key in this school will open."

"Then how do we stop it?" Idris pressed. "There has to be a way. Nobody builds a bargain without a loophole; every story like this has one."

"This isn't a story, Mr. Vane." But Calloway didn't look away from the map, and after a long moment, he tapped a faint line running from the chapel's sealed door to a second mark near the margin — words too small and faded for Nell to read from where she stood. "There's a name here. Half-erased, but a name. Whoever wrote this book believed the bargain could be renegotiated, once, by someone the tower itself chose to listen to. I've never been able to make out who that's meant to be, or how it happens."

"The bell chose Priya," Nell said slowly. "Does that mean it's already chosen who gets to bargain, too?"

Calloway closed the book, his expression grave in the firelight.

"I don't know," he admitted. "But if I were you, Miss Ashworth, I would spend considerably less time asking whether you're allowed to go looking, and considerably more time finding that door before the ninth day arrives and the question stops being academic."
