# Chapter 8: The Gala Mask

The Grand Palais ballroom in upper Manhattan was a sea of crystal chandeliers, tailored tuxedos, and silk gowns worth more than Lyra's family home.

Lyra stood near the towering arched entrance, her hand resting softly on Alexander's arm. She wore a deep emerald velvet gown—backless, fitted smoothly to her curves, and ending in a subtle sweep along the polished floor. Her waist-length dark hair was styled in soft waves over one shoulder, revealing her pale neck and the diamond drop earrings Alexander had fastened around her ears himself an hour ago.

Beside her, Alexander was imposing. At six-foot-three, his custom black tuxedo accentuating his broad chest and corded shoulders, he commanded the room without uttering a single word. His razor-sharp jawline was set, his ice-gray hunter eyes scanning the elite crowd with quiet, predatory indifference.

"You look stunning, little flower," Alexander rasped, his voice dropping low enough for only her to hear. His hand covered hers on his arm, his thumb lazily tracing circles against her bare skin.

"They are staring," Lyra whispered, keeping her posture poised despite the flutter in her chest. "Everyone in this room is trying to figure out who I am."

"Let them stare," Alexander replied, his tone laced with possessive pride. "They know you are with me. That is all they need to understand."

A high-profile real estate developer approached them, holding a glass of champagne. "Vance! I heard you finalized the downtown district acquisition. Rumor has it the final holdout was a stubborn printing press."

Lyra's breath hitched slightly, her fingers tightening on Alexander's sleeve.

Alexander didn't flinch. He drew Lyra subtly closer, his large hand moving to rest at the small of her exposed back, his palm burning warm against her bare skin.

"The press is no longer a factor, Harrison," Alexander said coldly, his gray eyes fixing on the developer with dangerous authority. "The owner's family and I reached a personal settlement. The matter is entirely closed."

The developer blinked, registering the sharp edge in Alexander's voice, and quickly backed away with a tight nod.

Alexander turned his head, his gaze dropping to Lyra's flushed face. "Step onto the balcony with me," he commanded softly.

He guided her away from the crowded ballroom and through a pair of tall French doors onto the secluded, rain-slicked terrace overlooking Central Park. The cold night air hit her skin, making her shiver, but Alexander stepped directly behind her, his massive frame blocking the wind as his arms wrapped around her waist, pulling her back against his chest.

"Alexander..." she breathed out, resting her head back against his shoulder.

"You handled them well," he whispered against her ear, his lips brushing the delicate curve of her neck. "Now take off the mask."

"What mask?"

"The archivist who thinks she doesn't belong here," Alexander growled low, his hands sliding up her ribcage, his touch sending a violent surge of electricity straight to her core. "You belong everywhere I stand."

Lyra turned in his arms, her dark eyes locking onto his piercing gray ones. The shy, hesitant girl who had entered his office a week ago was gone, replaced by a deep, undeniable pull she could no longer fight.

She reached up, her soft hands framing his sharp jawline, and pulled him down into a fierce, breathless kiss under the shadowed arch of the terrace.
