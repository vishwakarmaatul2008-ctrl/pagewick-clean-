# Chapter 1: The Sovereign's Debt

The seventy-fifth floor of Vance Tower did not smell like the rest of the city. Down on the streets of Manhattan, the air was thick with exhaust, rain, and the cheap grease of street carts. Up here, behind soundproofed glass and black marble, it smelled of cedarwood, cold rain, and the quiet, crushing weight of absolute wealth.

Lyra Mercer adjusted the strap of her canvas bag, her fingers tightening around the edge of a thick manila folder. Her knuckles were white.

"Mr. Vance does not tolerate lateness, Miss Mercer," the executive assistant murmured without looking up from her curved dual monitors. Her voice was flat, practiced, and clinical. "You have two minutes before his next call. Straight through those doors."

Lyra took a quiet, measured breath. Her long, dark hair shifted over the shoulder of her worn wool coat—a coat that had seen four winters too many, faded at the cuffs and thin at the elbows. She was out of place in this monolithic shrine of glass and steel, and she knew it. But her father's debt didn't care about aesthetics, and neither did the man sitting behind those doors.

She pushed open the heavy double oak doors, her flat shoes making no sound against the hand-woven rug.

The office was vast, lined with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the rain-slicked skyline. Standing by the glass was Alexander Vance.

At twenty-eight, he was already considered an untraceable force in international finance—a man who bought companies to dismantle them and owned real estate he would never visit. He stood at six-foot-three, broad-shouldered and dangerously broad in the chest, framed by a tailored charcoal suit that clung to the dense, corded muscle beneath. His silhouette alone commanded the room, but it was his face that made people freeze: sharp, angular jawline, dark hair cut with military precision, and a pair of piercing, ice-gray hunter eyes that looked right through skin and bone.

He didn't turn around immediately when she entered. He simply watched her reflection in the dark glass.

"You're three minutes late, Miss Mercer," Alexander rasped. His voice was deep, smooth, and laced with a cold, unyielding authority.

"The service elevator was held on floor forty-two, Mr. Vance," Lyra replied softly. Her voice was calm, carrying a natural, quiet dignity despite the nervous flutter in her chest. She stepped toward the desk, her pale, porcelain skin standing out sharply against the dark interior. "I have the signed land deeds from the lower district archivist."

Alexander slowly turned around. His gray eyes locked onto hers with lethal focus, tracking every micro-movement as she crossed the polished floor—the slight hesitation in her step, the shy dip of her chin, the way her long dark hair fell past her waist.

He didn't look at the folder she placed on the mahogany desk. Instead, he took two slow, deliberate strides forward. The atmosphere in the room instantly shifted, compressed by his immense presence.

"You bring them yourself because you don't trust courier services," he said, stopping just inches from her. Up close, the contrast between them was absurd—his towering, muscular frame entirely enveloping her delicate, five-foot-four figure. The scent of high-end cologne and crisp paper drifted off him. "Or because you wanted to make sure I saw your face today."

Lyra refused to step back, though her pulse hammered against her ribs. "I brought them because I was instructed to deliver them directly to you. Nothing more."

"Liar," Alexander murmured.

Before she could react, his long, calloused fingers reached out, gripping her chin gently but with absolute control, tilting her face upward into the light. His thumb brushed over the curve of her lower lip. His gaze darkened, shifting from cold calculation to something heavy, primitive, and completely untamed.

"You tremble every time I enter a room, Lyra," he whispered, stepping closer until her hips bumped against the edge of his mahogany desk. "Yet you look me in the eye every single time."

"I am not afraid of you," she breathed, her shy demeanor melting into a quiet, electric tension that filled the air between them.

"You should be."

Alexander's hand moved from her chin to the back of her neck, his broad palm cupping her head, entangling in her soft, thick hair. Without another word, he bent his head down and claimed her mouth.

The kiss was an absolute takeover. Deep, possessive, and fierce, obliterating every boundary of professionalism in a single second. Lyra gasped softly against his lips, and Alexander took immediate advantage, deepening the angle as his other arm wrapped around her waist, effortlessly lifting her onto the edge of the desk.

Her hands instinctively flew to his chest, her fingers clutching the fine wool of his lapels as a shiver ran down her spine. The chemistry was explosive—a sudden, violent heat that made her head spin.

Alexander pulled back just half an inch, his heavy chest heaving against hers, his gray eyes burning down into her flushed face. With one fluid motion, he shed his suit jacket, tossing it carelessly onto the floor. His large, warm hands reached for her silk blouse, his fingers skilled as he undid the top three buttons, exposing the soft, pale curve of her collarbone.

"Alexander..." she breathed out, her breath catching in her throat as his mouth pressed against the sensitive skin right below her ear.

"You thought this was about land deeds," he growled against her skin, his hands trailing down to the zipper of her skirt as he unbuckled his leather belt with the other hand. "You have no idea why your name has been on my desk for six months, little flower."
