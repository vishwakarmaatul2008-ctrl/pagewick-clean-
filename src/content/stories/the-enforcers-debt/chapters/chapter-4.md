# Chapter 4 — The Crimson Shield

The grand ballroom of the Grand Regency Hotel was a sea of tailored tuxedos, silk gowns, and concealed weapons. Crystal chandeliers cast a warm golden light across five hundred of the city's most formidable power brokers—corrupt officials, shipping magnates, and high-ranking syndicate figures standing in quiet clusters while bodyguards posted against the perimeter walls watched the room with flat, unblinking eyes.

At the top of the grand marble staircase, Aria paused.

She wore a floor-length crimson silk dress with a high collar and a subtle side slit, its sharp tailored lines giving her a silhouette of disciplined composure. Her dark hair was pinned up in a clean twist, exposing the pale line of her throat and the string of black diamonds resting against her collarbone. Her dark hazel eyes scanned the crowd below, taking in the exit routes, the placement of security detail, and the subtle shifts in posture among the guests.

Julian stood beside her. In his black tuxedo, his tall, broad-shouldered frame commanded space effortlessly, his sharp features and dark hair giving him a quiet, lethal presence among the elite crowd. He didn't look like an enforcer making an entrance; he looked like a man inspecting an asset line.

"Remember the rules, Aria," Julian said, his baritone voice low enough that it carried only to her. "You don't leave the main floor without my security detail. You don't speak to Marco Valenti if he isolates you. And most importantly..."

He turned his head slightly, his slate-gray eyes meeting hers.

"...you let them see that you stand here by choice."

"I don't need to fake composure, Julian," Aria replied, her voice steady. "I've handled violent patients in trauma bays while their gang members argued outside the door. These men are just wearing better suits."

Julian's jaw tightened slightly, a fleeting spark of dark amusement passing through his eyes. He offered his arm. Aria slid her hand into the crook of his elbow, feeling the dense, unyielding muscle beneath the fabric, and together they descended the marble steps.

The quiet hum of conversation dipped the moment their feet touched the main floor. Dozens of eyes turned toward them—calculating, skeptical, and sharp.

"Ah, the managing director himself," a coarse voice called out.

An older man with slicked-back silver hair and a jagged scar running from his lip to his jawline stepped out of the crowd, a glass of scotch in hand. Two massive men in dark suits stepped up behind him.

Marco Valenti.

"And this must be the Sterling girl," Valenti said, his small, dark eyes sweeping over Aria with a heavy, patronizing look. "We were surprised to hear Arthur's thirty-two-crore disaster was settled overnight. I didn't realize the Cassian Syndicate was running a rescue fund for failing businessmen."

Aria felt the muscle in Julian's arm tense beneath her touch, but before he could speak, she stepped slightly forward, positioning herself on equal footing with him.

"My father's logistics firm suffered a deliberate supply chain breach, Mr. Valenti," Aria said, her voice clear and carrying effortlessly across the immediate circle. "Not a financial failure. And if you checked the manifests, you'd know the Cassians didn't absorb a loss—they secured exclusive rights to the northern routes."

A quiet ripple passed through the surrounding guests. Valenti's smile tightened, his grip on his glass stiffening.

"Sharp tongue," Valenti muttered, turning his gaze back to Julian. "Careful, Cassian. Weak foundations ruin strong structures. If Arthur Sterling's debt leaks into your council numbers, your own lieutenants might start asking questions about your leadership."

Julian took one slow, deliberate step forward, his tall frame imposing a cold, overwhelming shadow over the older man. He didn't reach for a weapon or drop his calm demeanor; he simply tilted his head, his slate-gray eyes locking onto Valenti with flat, absolute finality.

"The council numbers are balanced, Marco," Julian said softly, his deep voice carrying a razor-thin edge that silenced the immediate quadrant of the ballroom. "And if anyone in this room wants to test the stability of my logistics network, they can start by asking what happened to the three Valenti scows that tried to cross my perimeter last night."

Valenti's color drained slightly. He opened his mouth to reply, but the cold certainty in Julian's posture stopped him cold. Valenti took a step back, raising his glass in a tense, silent concession before vanishing back into the crowd.

As the chatter slowly resumed around them, Julian looked down at Aria, assessing her quiet breathing and steady pulse.

"You took a risk speaking to Valenti directly," Julian murmured, his hand moving to rest lightly against the small of her back—a gesture of public alignment rather than restraint.

"He was trying to bait you into a public threat to make you look volatile in front of your council," Aria said quietly, her hazel eyes searching his face. "I gave him a business facts check instead. It cost him more ground."

Julian stared at her for a long moment, the guarded distance in his gray eyes softening by a fraction.

"You're far more dangerous than your father ever was, Aria Sterling," he said low.

"Then remember that," Aria replied, looking straight ahead as the syndicate council members began to approach them, "next time you try to dictate my terms."
