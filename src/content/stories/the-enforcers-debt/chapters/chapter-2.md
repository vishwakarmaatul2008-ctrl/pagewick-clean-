# Chapter 2 — Behind Fortified Walls

The tinted glass of the matte-black SUV rendered the city outside invisible, cutting through the heavy midnight storm as it crossed the river.

Aria sat pressed against the leather interior, her fingers resting lightly on her left wrist, monitoring her own pulse. It was eighty-four beats per minute—elevated, but controlled. Years of running triage in overcrowded emergency rooms had taught her how to compartmentalize fear into functional focus.

Beside her, Julian sat with his eyes closed, his back straight, resting his head against the headrest. The bright lights of passing highway lamps cast quick, rhythmic shadows over his face. He looked drained, his posture tense despite his quiet demeanor.

Aria's eyes traced the sharp outline of his side profile, noticing the faint blue tinge beneath his jawline and the subtle twitch of his facial muscles every time the vehicle hit a bump.

"Your left shoulder," Aria said quietly, her voice cutting through the hum of the custom engine. "You're guarding it."

Julian didn't open his eyes, but his breathing paused for a microsecond. "I'm fine."

"You're elevating your right arm to take the weight off your left deltoid," she observed, turning in her seat to look at him directly. "And you haven't taken a full deep breath since we left the Vance Tower. That's not fatigue, Cassian. That's a cracked rib or a deep contusion."

Julian opened his eyes slowly. His slate-gray gaze locked onto hers, unreadable and cool, but there was a flicker of quiet assessment behind it.

"You observe a lot for a hostage," he said softly.

"I'm a trauma nurse, not a hostage," Aria shot back. "And if my contract partner dies of an untreated internal bleed on night one, my father's thirty-two-crore debt defaults right back to Valenti. Let me see it."

Julian stared at her for three long seconds. Then, without a word, he unbuttoned his suit jacket, reached up, and pulled open the top three buttons of his white dress shirt.

Across his left ribcage was a massive, purple-and-black hematoma stretching from his sternum to his flank—a blunt-force injury less than forty-eight hours old.

Aria leaned in closer, her professional instincts taking over completely. She reached out, her cool fingertips pressing firmly against the edge of the bruised tissue. Julian's jaw clenched tight, a sharp intake of air escaping his teeth, but he didn't pull away.

"No crepitus in the bone," she murmured, assessing the tissue with practiced precision. "Ribs are intact, but you have severe soft-tissue trauma. You took a heavy impact—bulletproof vest against a high-caliber round?"

"Valenti's men tried to intercept our convoy two nights ago," Julian admitted, his voice low. "The vest caught the kinetic hit."

"And you've been walking around running syndicate briefings with a massive internal hematoma instead of icing it," Aria said, leaning back and fastening her seatbelt again as the vehicle slowed down. "You're arrogant."

"I'm busy," Julian replied dryly, buttoning his shirt back up with steady fingers. "There is a difference."

The SUV turned off the main highway, passing through a massive set of wrought-iron gates flanked by high stone walls and automated security turrets. They drove up a long, winding driveway flanked by tall oak trees before pulling up to a towering modern estate constructed of dark slate, reinforced glass, and black steel.

Two guards in dark suits immediately stepped forward to open the doors.

Julian stepped out first, adjusting his suit jacket before offering a hand back toward Aria. She looked at his palm, met his eyes, and stepped out onto the damp stone driveway under her own strength.

"Follow me," he said.

The interior of the estate was vast and quiet—polished concrete floors, dark gray walls, and minimal furniture, built more like a tactical command center than a family residence.

Julian led her up a cantilevered staircase to the second floor, stopping at a heavy oak door near the end of the corridor. He swiped an electronic keycard, and the lock disengaged with a crisp double-beep.

"This is your suite," Julian said, gesturing inside.

Aria stepped through the doorway. The room was expansive, featuring a king-sized bed wrapped in charcoal linens, a private balcony overlooking the estate grounds, and an adjoining glass-walled bathroom. On the bed sat her single black suitcase—retrieved from her apartment while she had been stuck in the boardroom.

"You had your men enter my apartment," Aria said, turning to face him near the doorframe.

"Your father provided the security code," Julian said smoothly. "Everything inside has been vetted by my team. Anything else you require, inform Marcus, the house manager."

Aria crossed her arms, standing her ground. "And where are your quarters?"

Julian pointed toward a dark connecting door at the far side of the room. "Through there. My study and private quarters connect to yours. The heavy bolt is on your side, Aria. I don't enter unless you open it, or unless there is a breach in estate security."

She looked at the door, then back at his quiet, guarded face. "You expect me to believe you bought a thirty-two-crore liability just to give me a fortified room?"

"I don't care what you believe tonight," Julian said, his baritone voice dropping into a measured register. "You're exhausted, your hands are cold, and your father is safe in his home. That is enough for tonight."

He reached into his pocket and set a silver keycard on the console table beside the door.

"The front doors lock automatically at midnight. The perimeter is monitored twenty-four hours a day." He paused, his gaze resting on her face for a long moment. "Get some sleep, Aria."

"Julian," she called out before he could turn away.

He stopped, his hand resting on the heavy doorframe. "Yes?"

"In the boardroom, you said you'd been waiting for three years," Aria said, her hazel eyes fixed on him. "What happened three years ago?"

Julian stood motionless in the corridor light. For a fraction of a second, the stoic mask slipped, revealing a deep, quiet gravity beneath his slate-gray eyes.

"Three years ago, you saved a young man's life in the rain outside St. Jude's," Julian said quietly. "He was bleeding out from a gunshot wound. You didn't ask his name, and you didn't report it to the police. You patched him up and left him in the vestibule."

Aria's eyes widened slightly as the memory rushed back—the freezing rain, the terrified boy clutching his side, her quick hands applying a pressure dressing.

"That was my younger brother, Leo," Julian said, his voice cutting cleanly through the quiet hallway. "And I've been keeping track of your family's debts ever since."

He stepped back and closed the door, leaving her standing alone in the quiet room.
