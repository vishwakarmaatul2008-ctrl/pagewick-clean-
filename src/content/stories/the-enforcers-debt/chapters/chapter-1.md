# Chapter 1 — The Ultimatum

The air inside the top-floor boardroom of the Vance Tower was thick with the scent of cheap whiskey and expensive cigars. Rain slammed relentlessly against the floor-to-ceiling glass, casting long, distorted shadows across the mahogany table.

At the head of the table sat Arthur Sterling. His head was buried in his hands, his knuckles white, his chest heaving with every ragged breath. Across from him sat three reams of financial ledgers—a physical record of a multi-crore collapse that had taken less than seventy-two hours to complete.

Standing near the floor-to-ceiling windows, her arms crossed tightly over her chest, was his daughter, Aria.

She wasn't looking at the numbers. She was looking at the door.

"He's not going to show up, Dad," Aria said, her voice quiet, steady, but edged with a blade. "Men like Julian Cassian don't come to negotiate. They send people to handle the trash."

"He will come," Arthur rasped, his voice breaking. "He promised me ten minutes. I just need ten minutes to explain the shipment betrayal. The inventory—"

"The inventory is at the bottom of the harbor," a voice cut through the darkness.

The heavy oak doors of the boardroom didn't slam open; they glided inward without a sound.

Two armed men in immaculate gray suits stepped in first, scanning the corners with practiced, cold efficiency. Then came Julian Cassian.

He was imposing—easily six-foot-three, with a broad, heavily muscular frame that strained against the dark fabric of his tailored charcoal suit. His dark hair was neatly styled, accentuating sharp, chiseled cheekbones, a shadow of stubble along a square jaw, and striking slate-gray eyes. He was dangerously, breathtakingly handsome, but every inch of his body radiated absolute authority.

Julian pulled out the chair directly across from Arthur and sat down. He didn't look at the ledgers. He didn't look at Arthur.

His eyes landed directly on Aria.

He locked gazes with her for three long, suffocating seconds before turning his head toward her father.

"Thirty-two crore, Arthur," Julian said, his voice a low, gravelly baritone that seemed to vibrate through the floorboards. "That is the exact figure your failed shipment cost my organization tonight. You don't have the liquid capital. You don't have the collateral. Your family's real estate assets wouldn't cover half of it even if I seized them by morning."

Arthur pushed himself up, hands trembling on the table. "Julian, please. Give me six months. My contacts in Singapore—"

"You don't have six months," Julian interrupted, his tone chillingly even. "You have six minutes before my men begin liquidating your family's properties."

Arthur collapsed back into his chair, the last drop of color draining from his face. He looked broken—a man stripped of his pride, his legacy, and his future in a single sentence.

Aria stepped away from the window. The heel of her boots clicked sharply against the hardwood as she walked directly to the edge of the table, placing herself between Julian and her father.

"Take me to court, Mr. Cassian," Aria said, her chin raised, refusing to let her voice shake despite the cold knot tightening in her stomach. "Or take the company. But stop playing games with a sick man."

Julian leaned back in his leather chair. A faint, unreadable shadow crossed his chiseled face as he looked up at her.

"Court is for civilized men, Miss Sterling. I am an enforcer," Julian said softly. He rested his elbows on the table, lacing his long fingers together. "And I don't play games. I collect."

"Then collect," she shot back. "Seize the buildings. Take the accounts."

"None of that interests me," Julian said.

He reached into his inner jacket pocket, pulled out a fountain pen, and slid a single, crisp piece of paper across the dark wood. It wasn't a bank transfer or a foreclosure notice.

It was a marriage certificate.

Arthur gasped, struggling to sit up. "What... what is this?"

"A full settlement," Julian said, his gray eyes never leaving Aria. "Sign this, and your thirty-two crore debt vanishes. The shipments are forgotten. The syndicate never speaks your family's name again. Your father keeps his home, his dignity, and his life."

Aria stared at the document, the black ink of the legal text blurring for a fraction of a second before her vision cleared. "You want a puppet bride to keep my father quiet."

"I want an alliance," Julian corrected calmly. "And I want a contract that guarantees absolute silence."

"Aria, no," Arthur choked out, reaching for her hand. "No, I won't let you do this—"

"Shut up, Dad," Aria whispered, her voice tight with unshed tears.

She picked up the heavy silver pen from the table. She looked at Julian—the towering, untouchable man who controlled the city's darkest corners. He didn't look victorious. He looked completely impassive, like a man executing a routine business transaction.

"If I sign this," Aria said, her hands remarkably steady as she hovered the pen over the line, "my family is untouched. Forever. No more surprise visits. No more debts."

"You have my word," Julian said.

Aria pressed the pen to the paper and signed her name in quick, sharp strokes. The moment the last letter was finished, Julian reached out and pulled the contract back toward his side of the table. He folded it once and slipped it into his jacket.

He stood up, his massive frame towering over the table as he buttoned his suit jacket with effortless grace.

"Pack one bag, Miss Sterling," Julian said, turning toward the door. "My car is downstairs. You live at the estate now."

Aria stood her ground, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. "I signed your paper, Cassian. But don't think for a second that you own me."

Julian paused at the threshold. He turned his head sideways, looking back at her over his shoulder. A dark, dangerous flicker passed through his gray eyes.

"I don't need to own you, Aria," he whispered, his voice cutting through the quiet room like shattered glass. "I've been waiting for you to walk into my office for three years. The debt was just the excuse."
