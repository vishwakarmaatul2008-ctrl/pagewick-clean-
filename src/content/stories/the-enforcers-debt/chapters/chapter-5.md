# Chapter 5 — Anatomy of Sabotage

The rain had returned by the time the armored SUV pulled into the estate garage at 2:00 AM.

The silence inside the vehicle was no longer the sharp, hostile standoff of their first night together. It was a heavy, watchful quiet—the tactical alignment of two people who had just navigated a minefield together and survived without a scratch.

Aria stepped out onto the polished concrete floor, her bare feet cool against the stone as she carried her heels in one hand. The floor-length crimson dress rustled softly against her legs. She walked upstairs to her suite, her gaze sweeping over the estate's secondary security points—noticing that Julian had quietly doubled the interior patrols following their encounter at the Regency.

She pushed open the connecting door.

Julian was standing near the floor-to-ceiling windows of his private study, his dark tuxedo jacket tossed across the back of an armchair. His white dress shirt was unbuttoned at the collar, the sleeves rolled up past his forearms, exposing the dark, intricate syndicate ink that etched down to his wrists. He held a tumbler of black coffee, watching the rain hit the glass.

"You should be sleeping," Julian said without turning around, his baritone voice quiet in the dim room.

"I tried," Aria replied, leaning against the doorframe, her posture straight. "Your interior guards are cycling shifts every forty-five minutes instead of every two hours. You're anticipating an escalation."

Julian took a slow sip of his coffee and turned to face her. The dim lighting picked up the tired tension in his jaw and the quiet intensity of his slate-gray eyes.

"Valenti lost standing at the gala," Julian said flatly. "A man like Marco doesn't absorb a public embarrassment without trying to claw back leverage. He won't hit your father directly now—he knows I've covered the Sterling properties with my own men. He'll look for an opening here."

Aria walked into the room, stopping at the edge of his walnut desk. "Which brings us back to your internal problem. Valenti wouldn't risk an intrusion on a fortified estate unless he had information on your shift rotations."

Julian set his glass down on the blotter, his gaze fixing on her with a quiet, analytical focus. "You notice a lot for someone who has been here less than forty-eight hours."

"I spent five years in trauma units, Cassian," Aria said calmly, her hazel eyes holding his. "You learn to spot systemic failures before the patient crashes. Your perimeter isn't weak, but your security detail is compartmentalized to a fault. Someone in your mid-level command is feeding Valenti structural updates."

Julian looked at her for a long, unreadable moment. He didn't dismiss her, nor did he offer a defensive denial. Instead, he reached into his inner jacket pocket, pulled out a thin leather folder, and set it on the desk between them.

"That's the security manifest for the eastern dock facility," Julian said softly. "The shift logs from the night your father's shipment went down."

Aria reached out, opening the file. Her eyes scanned the names, times, and medical clearance sign-offs with practiced speed. "These aren't just shipping logs. These are casualty reports from the guard detail that was stationed at the pier."

"Four men were hospitalized with neurotoxic exposure," Julian said, his voice dropping into a dangerous register. "The official report claimed a chemical leak from a broken container. But the symptoms don't match standard industrial agents."

Aria's fingers stopped on a line of hand-written vitals. Her eyes narrowed. "Organophosphate poisoning. Rapid pupil constriction, severe bradycardia, respiratory distress... Julian, this wasn't an industrial accident. Someone laced their rations to neutralize the pier guards before Valenti's scows ever docked."

Julian stepped closer, his tall, broad frame moving with quiet precision until he stood just across the corner of the desk from her.

"That means the sabotage of your father's shipment wasn't an outside job," Julian whispered, his gray eyes locking onto hers with cold clarity. "It was coordinated from inside my own logistics command."

"And my father was just the convenient scapegoat," Aria realized, the last pieces of the puzzle falling into place. "Valenti and your insider used his debt to cover the real theft."

"Which is why I needed you here," Julian said low, his voice steady. "Not as a hostage. Not as leverage. I needed someone outside the syndicate structure who could read those medical manifests without leaking the analysis back to the council."

Aria looked up at him, her pulse steady despite the weight of what he was admitting. The tension between them remained sharp, but the nature of it had shifted—the cold distrust replacing itself with a high-stakes, strategic partnership.

Before she could speak, a sharp, rhythmic tone chimed from the terminal behind his desk. A single red alert light began pulsing softly along the edge of the monitor.

Julian didn't flinch. He glanced at the screen, his posture instantly locking back into command authority.

"The perimeter sensor on the south wall just went dark," Julian said, his baritone voice dropping into a lethal whisper. "Valenti's insider just made their move."
