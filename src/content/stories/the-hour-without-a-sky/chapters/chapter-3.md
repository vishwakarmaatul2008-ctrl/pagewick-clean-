# Chapter 3 — The Eighth Floor

Mira couldn't move.

The elevator doors remained open.

Beyond them was the impossible hallway.

The eighth floor.

She had lived in the building for almost three years.

There were seven floors.

Seven.

She had climbed every staircase. She had walked every corridor. There was no eighth floor.

Yet the hallway was there.

And the woman at the end was waiting.

"Mira."

The voice sounded closer this time.

Mira tightened her grip around the pendant.

The silver symbol on it was still warm.

"Who are you?" she asked.

The woman didn't answer.

Instead, she stepped beneath the faint light hanging above the hallway.

Mira finally saw her face.

She looked about fifty years old.

Long silver-black hair.

Pale skin.

Dark eyes that seemed strangely familiar.

And around her neck was the same silver symbol.

Mira took a step backward.

The woman smiled.

"You shouldn't have brought that."

Mira looked down at her pendant.

"What is this?"

The woman looked toward the elevator.

"Who told you to come here?"

"No one."

"Then why did you come?"

"I didn't."

The woman's expression changed.

For the first time, she looked afraid.

"You don't remember."

Mira shook her head.

"Remember what?"

The woman took one slow step forward.

"That is why the barrier had to be built."

A cold wind suddenly moved through the hallway.

Mira felt it against her face.

But the elevator behind her was still.

No wind.

No sound.

Only the woman standing ahead.

"What barrier?" Mira asked.

The woman looked toward the ceiling.

"The one that keeps this city safe."

Mira swallowed.

"And what is outside?"

The woman's eyes returned to hers.

"Things that remember."

Mira didn't understand.

Then her phone vibrated.

She looked down.

A new message.

DON'T BELIEVE HER.

Mira looked up.

The woman was staring directly at her phone.

"You received another message."

It wasn't a question.

Mira stepped farther into the elevator.

"Who is sending them?"

The woman didn't answer.

Instead, she whispered:

"How many messages have you received?"

"Four."

The woman closed her eyes.

"Then you still have time."

"For what?"

The woman opened her eyes.

"For the fifth message."

Mira's phone vibrated.

Once.

The woman went completely pale.

Mira looked down.

There was no notification.

No message.

Nothing.

Then the elevator doors began closing.

The woman suddenly ran toward her.

"Mira!"

Mira screamed and pressed the button repeatedly.

The doors closed just before the woman's hand reached them.

The elevator dropped.

Not down one floor.

Not two.

It fell.

Mira slammed against the wall as the numbers on the display disappeared.

The elevator became completely dark.

Then a voice whispered beside her ear.

Not from her phone.

Not from outside.

From inside the elevator.

"You were never supposed to remember."

Mira stopped breathing.

The lights returned.

The display showed:

7.

The doors opened.

She stumbled out.

The familiar seventh-floor corridor was exactly where it should have been.

Her apartment door stood twenty steps away.

Mira ran to it.

She pulled out her keys.

Her hands were shaking so badly that she dropped them.

She bent down.

And froze.

There was something lying beside her keys.

A photograph.

Old.

Yellowed.

Mira picked it up.

It showed a group of people standing in front of her apartment building.

At first, she didn't recognize anyone.

Then she saw herself.

A little girl.

Maybe six years old.

Standing beside an older woman.

The same silver pendant hung around the child's neck.

Mira stared at the photograph.

Her stomach tightened.

She had never seen it before.

On the back, someone had written a date.

17 June 2014.

And underneath it:

THE FIRST NIGHT THE SKY DISAPPEARED.

Mira's phone vibrated.

She didn't want to look.

But she did.

A single message appeared.

FIFTH MESSAGE:

Then another line appeared beneath it.

LOOK OUTSIDE.

Mira slowly raised her head.

Her apartment window was directly across from her.

The curtains were open.

She could see her bedroom.

And someone was standing inside.

Watching her.

Mira knew that room.

She knew that apartment.

She was looking at her own bedroom.

But she hadn't gone inside yet.

The figure in the window lifted one hand.

And waved.

Mira's phone screen went black.

Then, for the first time that night, the city screamed.
