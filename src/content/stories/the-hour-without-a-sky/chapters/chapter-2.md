# Chapter 2 — The Woman Under the Streetlight

Mira ran.

She didn't look back.

Her shoes struck the pavement again and again as she hurried through the empty street, her breath coming in short, frightened bursts.

She could hear her own heartbeat.

Nothing else.

The message on her phone kept replaying in her mind.

IT HAS ALREADY FOUND YOU.

Who had sent it?

And what did it mean?

Mira turned the corner.

Her apartment building was only two streets away now.

She could see the familiar blue sign above the entrance.

Relief washed over her.

She slowed down.

Then stopped.

The entrance doors were open.

Mira frowned.

She always locked them at night.

She looked at her phone.

12:07 AM.

Seven minutes.

That's all that had passed since the clocks started working again.

Yet the city still felt wrong.

Mira stepped inside.

The lobby lights were on.

The security desk was empty.

"Mr. Rao?"

No answer.

She walked toward the elevator.

The doors opened immediately.

Mira hesitated.

Then stepped inside.

She pressed the button for the seventh floor.

The doors closed.

The elevator began to rise.

1.

2.

3.

Mira watched the numbers carefully.

Then the elevator stopped.

Between floors.

The lights went out.

Mira froze.

"No."

She pressed the seventh-floor button again.

Nothing happened.

She pressed the emergency button.

Nothing.

Then her phone screen lit up.

Another message.

DON'T LOOK UP.

Mira slowly raised her eyes.

There was a faint sound above her.

A scratching noise.

Something was moving across the ceiling of the elevator.

Slowly.

Carefully.

As if it were searching for a way inside.

Mira covered her mouth to stop herself from screaming.

The scratching stopped.

Silence.

Then---

Knock.

Mira's blood turned cold.

The sound had come from above.

Knock.

She stepped backward.

There was barely enough room.

Knock.

Her phone vibrated again.

Mira looked down.

This time, there was no warning.

There was a photograph.

She opened it.

Her hands began shaking.

The photograph showed the exact elevator she was standing in.

The same metal walls.

The same emergency panel.

The same tiny crack in the mirror.

But the photograph had been taken from above.

From the ceiling.

And Mira could see herself standing inside.

She stared at the image.

Then noticed something.

There was someone standing behind her.

Mira slowly looked into the elevator mirror.

Nothing.

She turned around.

Nothing.

Her phone vibrated.

A new message appeared.

MIRA.

She stared at her own name.

Another message appeared.

YOU'RE LOOKING FOR THE WRONG DOOR.

The elevator suddenly moved.

Mira grabbed the railing.

The numbers began changing.

4.

5.

6.

Then---

8.

Mira's eyes widened.

There was no eighth floor.

Everyone in the building knew that.

The elevator doors opened.

Mira stood completely still.

Beyond the doors was a hallway.

Dark.

Long.

And completely unfamiliar.

The walls were covered with old paintings.

At the very end stood a wooden door.

A silver symbol had been carved into it.

Mira recognized the symbol.

She had seen it once before.

On the pendant her grandmother had given her when she was a child.

Mira reached for the pendant beneath her shirt.

It was warm.

Far too warm.

Then a voice came from the hallway.

A woman's voice.

Soft.

Almost familiar.

"Mira."

Mira couldn't breathe.

The voice spoke again.

"You finally came back."

The elevator lights flickered.

And somewhere behind Mira, inside the elevator, something whispered:

"Don't let her see you."
