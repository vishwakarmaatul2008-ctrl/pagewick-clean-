# Chapter 6 — The Old Railway Station

Mira ran.

She didn't know where the woman was taking her.

She didn't care.

The voice of the child remained behind them, echoing through the stairwell.

"Why did you leave me here?"

Mira's chest burned as they reached the ground floor.

The woman pushed open an emergency exit.

Cold night air rushed over them.

They stepped into an alley behind the building.

Mira bent forward, trying to catch her breath.

"Wait."

The woman kept walking.

"Mira."

She stopped.

"What?"

"I need answers."

The woman looked at her.

"You'll have them at the station."

"You keep saying that."

"Because that's where everything began."

Mira looked at the sky.

The silver crack was still there.

Fainter now.

But growing.

"How much time?"

The woman checked an old pocket watch.

"Twenty-six minutes."

They started walking.

The city looked different.

Buildings stood exactly where they always had, but something about them felt wrong.

Every window was dark.

Every street was empty.

And above them, the sky seemed closer.

After several minutes, they reached an abandoned railway station.

Mira stopped.

She had passed this place countless times.

It had been closed for years.

The gates were covered with rust.

The station clock above the entrance had stopped at 12:00.

The woman unlocked the gate.

Mira stared at her.

"How do you have a key?"

"I've been waiting for tonight."

They entered.

Dust covered the old platform.

The tracks disappeared into darkness.

At the far end stood a small room with a wooden door.

The woman opened it.

Inside were hundreds of photographs.

Mira stepped inside.

Every wall was covered.

Families.

Children.

Buildings.

The sky.

And always the same date.

17 June.

Mira picked up one photograph.

A little girl stood in front of the station.

It was her.

Six years old.

But this time she wasn't alone.

Standing beside her was another little girl.

Same age.

Same dark eyes.

Same silver pendant.

Mira's heart stopped.

"Who is she?"

The woman closed her eyes.

"That's what your mother was trying to protect you from."

Mira looked at the photograph again.

The two girls were holding hands.

"She's me."

"No."

The woman pointed toward the second child.

"She's what the barrier created when it took you."

Mira looked up.

"What does that mean?"

The woman spoke quietly.

"When you disappeared for three days, the city couldn't decide whether you belonged inside or outside the barrier."

Mira stared at her.

"So it made another version of me?"

"Yes."

A deep rumble shook the station.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

The woman looked at her watch.

12:34 AM.

"Sixteen minutes."

Mira looked toward the sky through the broken station roof.

The silver crack was spreading.

"What happens when the hour ends?"

The woman didn't answer.

Mira already knew the answer would be terrible.

Instead, she asked:

"Where is my mother?"

The woman looked at the photograph.

"She never left the station."

Mira's eyes widened.

"What?"

The woman pointed toward the tracks.

A faint light appeared in the darkness.

Someone was walking toward them.

Mira couldn't breathe.

The figure stepped closer.

A woman.

Long dark hair.

A familiar face.

Mira's mother.

But she had died when Mira was nine.

"Mum?"

Her mother stopped.

Tears filled her eyes.

"Mira."

Mira took a step forward.

The woman beside her grabbed her arm.

"Don't touch her."

Mira turned angrily.

"Why?"

Her mother smiled sadly.

"Because I'm not your mother anymore."

The station clock suddenly began moving.

12:35.

The silver crack in the sky widened.

And somewhere behind Mira, a little girl began laughing.

Her mother looked past her.

"She's coming."

Mira turned.

At the end of the platform stood the six-year-old version of herself.

Holding the silver pendant.

Smiling.

And behind the child stood hundreds of shadowy figures.

The woman whispered:

"The barrier didn't keep them out."

Mira looked at the figures.

"Then what did it keep inside?"

The woman looked at her.

"You."

The station lights went out.

And the little girl whispered:

"Welcome home, Mira."
