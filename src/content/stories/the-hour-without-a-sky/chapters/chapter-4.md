# Chapter 4 — The Photograph

The scream lasted only a few seconds.

Then the city became silent again.

Mira stood frozen in the seventh-floor corridor, staring at the figure inside her bedroom.

The figure hadn't moved.

Neither had she.

For several seconds, they simply watched each other through the glass.

Then the bedroom light turned off.

Mira's heart slammed against her ribs.

She looked at her apartment door.

It was still locked.

She hadn't entered.

So how could someone be inside?

Her phone remained black in her hand.

Mira tried pressing the power button.

Nothing.

"Come on..."

She pressed it again.

The screen finally turned on.

12:19 AM.

Twenty minutes.

Only twenty minutes since midnight.

It felt like hours.

Mira looked at the photograph again.

The little girl in the picture was unmistakably her.

She had the same eyes.

The same small scar above her left eyebrow.

And the same pendant.

But there was something she hadn't noticed before.

Behind the little girl stood the woman from the eighth floor.

Mira's breath caught.

She looked closer.

The photograph had been taken outside her apartment building.

The woman was standing several metres behind her grandmother.

Watching.

Waiting.

Mira turned the photograph over.

There was another sentence beneath the date.

SHE WAS THE FIRST ONE TO FIND YOU.

Mira's phone vibrated.

She nearly dropped it.

A message appeared.

DO NOT ENTER YOUR APARTMENT.

Mira stared at it.

Then another message appeared.

THE PERSON INSIDE IS NOT YOU.

Her eyes moved toward the apartment.

The curtains were still closed.

She could no longer see anything.

Mira backed away from the door.

"Who are you?" she whispered.

The phone vibrated again.

ASK YOUR GRANDMOTHER.

Mira's stomach tightened.

Her grandmother had died eight years ago.

There was no one left to ask.

At least, that's what she had always believed.

Suddenly, something moved behind her.

Mira turned.

The hallway was empty.

But at the far end, the elevator doors were open.

Inside the elevator stood the silver-haired woman.

She didn't look angry.

She looked tired.

"Your grandmother lied to you," she said.

Mira couldn't speak.

The woman stepped out.

"About the night you disappeared."

Mira's fingers tightened around the photograph.

"I never disappeared."

The woman's expression softened.

"You were six years old."

Mira shook her head.

"I remember being six."

"No," the woman whispered. "You remember what they allowed you to remember."

A cold sensation spread through Mira's body.
