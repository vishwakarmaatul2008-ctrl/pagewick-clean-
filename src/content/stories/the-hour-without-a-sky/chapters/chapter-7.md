# Chapter 7 — The Girl Who Stayed

Mira couldn't move.

The little girl stood at the end of the platform, holding the silver pendant between her fingers.

She looked exactly like Mira had looked at six years old.

Same dark hair.

Same eyes.

Same small scar above her eyebrow.

But there was something wrong with her smile.

It wasn't the smile of a child.

It was the smile of someone who had been waiting for a very long time.

Mira's mother stepped forward.

"Don't be afraid."

Mira looked at her.

"You said I'm not supposed to touch you."

Her mother nodded.

"Because I'm part of the barrier now."

Mira stared at her.

"What did you do?"

Her mother looked toward the little girl.

"I made a deal."

The woman beside Mira whispered:

"Your mother tried to bring you back."

Mira looked at her mother.

"Back from where?"

"The other side."

The station trembled.

The shadowy figures behind the little girl moved closer.

Mira's mother continued.

"When the barrier disappeared eleven years ago, you walked through it."

Mira shook her head.

"I don't remember."

"I know."

Her mother's eyes filled with tears.

"I asked the city to give you back."

"And it did?"

"No."

The little girl smiled.

"It gave you back to me."

Mira looked at the child.

"What does that mean?"

Her mother whispered:

"The girl who came back wasn't the same girl who left."

Silence filled the station.

Mira looked at the little girl again.

The child raised the pendant.

"You left me."

Mira felt tears forming in her eyes.

"I didn't know you existed."

"You knew."

The little girl's voice changed.

For a moment, it sounded like Mira's own voice.

Then the station clock moved.

12:47.

The silver crack above the city had almost completely closed.

"Thirteen minutes," the woman said.

Mira looked at her mother.

"What happens when the hour ends?"

Her mother looked terrified.

"Everything that crossed the barrier tonight will remain here."

Mira looked at the shadows.

"And if they remain?"

"They will find everyone who carries the memory of the other side."

Mira understood.

"Me."

Her mother nodded.

"And you."

Mira stared at her.

"Why me?"

"Because you were the first."

The little girl suddenly began walking toward them.

The woman stepped in front of Mira.

"Stay behind me."

But Mira moved around her.

"No."

The woman grabbed her arm.

"Mira!"

Mira looked at the child.

The girl stopped.

For the first time, she looked frightened.

Mira slowly held out her hand.

"I didn't leave you."

The girl stared at her.

"You did."

"I was six."

"You promised."

Mira frowned.

"Promised what?"

The girl lifted the pendant.

"To come back."

Mira's memories suddenly cracked open.

A dark room.

A silver door.

Her mother's hand.

A little girl crying.

And her own six-year-old voice whispering:

"I'll come back for you."

Mira gasped.

She stumbled backward.

Her mother covered her mouth.

"You remembered."

The station shook violently.

The shadowy figures began moving.

The woman shouted:

"The barrier is closing!"

Mira looked at the little girl.

The child was crying now.

"You promised," she whispered.

Mira looked at the silver pendant.

Then she understood.

The barrier wasn't protecting the city from the girl.

It was protecting the girl from the city.

Mira stepped forward.

"I'm sorry."

The girl looked up.

Mira held out her hand.

"But I'm here now."

The child slowly reached for her.

Their fingers touched.

The entire station went silent.

The clock stopped.

The shadows disappeared.

And the silver crack in the sky vanished.

Mira blinked.

The little girl was gone.

So was her mother.

Only the pendant remained in Mira's hand.

Then her phone vibrated.

One message appeared.

THE BARRIER IS CLOSED.

Mira exhaled.

Then another message appeared.

BUT YOU OPENED SOMETHING ELSE.

Mira looked toward the station entrance.

Someone was standing there.

A tall figure.

Watching her.

It slowly raised its hand.

And behind it, the city lights began going out---

one street at a time.
