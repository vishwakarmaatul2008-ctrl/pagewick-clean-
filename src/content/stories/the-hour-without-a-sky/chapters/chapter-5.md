# Chapter 5 — The Empty Apartment

Mira couldn't move.

The woman's words echoed through the corridor.

"Your apartment has been empty for eleven years."

Mira looked at the door.

Her apartment.

The place where she had lived for almost three years.

The place where she had cooked, slept, cried, laughed, and spent countless ordinary evenings.

Empty?

"That isn't possible," Mira whispered.

The woman didn't answer.

Instead, she stared at the door as though she were looking at something much more dangerous than an empty room.

Mira stepped closer.

The three knocks came again.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Then a voice came from behind the door.

"Mira?"

Her own voice.

Mira's blood ran cold.

The woman grabbed her arm.

"Don't answer."

"But that's my voice."

"I know."

The voice came again.

"Mira, please open the door."

It sounded exactly like her.

Same tone.

Same breathing.

Even the slight hesitation she had whenever she was frightened.

Mira backed away.

"How?"

The woman shook her head.

"It copies people."

"What does?"

The woman looked toward the ceiling.

"The things beyond the barrier."

Another knock.

This time, the door handle moved.

Mira's heart raced.

The woman pulled her toward the staircase.

"We have to go."

Mira resisted.

"No. I need answers."

"You'll get them."

"When?"

"When we reach the old station."

Mira stared at her.

"Why the station?"

"Because that's where the barrier was created."

The lights flickered back on.

For half a second, Mira saw the entire corridor.

There was nobody behind them.

But when the lights stabilized, the woman was staring at the wall.

Mira followed her gaze.

A thin crack had appeared across the plaster.

It wasn't there before.

The crack slowly widened.

Something dark moved inside it.

The woman immediately pulled Mira down the stairs.

"Don't look at it."

They ran.

One floor.

Two.

Three.

Mira's breathing became heavier.

"What happened eleven years ago?" she asked between breaths.

The woman didn't stop.

"You were brought to this building when you were six."

"By my mother?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"She thought she could hide you."

"From what?"

The woman finally stopped on the fourth-floor landing.

She turned toward Mira.

"From the city itself."

Mira stared at her.

"That makes no sense."

"It will."

The woman reached into her coat and pulled out an old photograph.

She handed it to Mira.

It showed the same building.

But it looked different.

Older.

There were people standing outside.

Dozens of them.

Every person was looking toward the sky.

Mira turned the photograph over.

A date was written on the back.

17 June 2014.

Her hands trembled.

"What happened that night?"

The woman looked toward the window.

"The barrier disappeared for one hour."

Mira remembered the silver crack in the sky.

"The same thing that happened tonight."

"Yes."

"And that's when I disappeared?"

The woman nodded.

"For exactly three days."

Mira's face went pale.

"Three days?"

"You were found here after the barrier returned."

"Where?"

The woman pointed upward.

"On the eighth floor."

Mira looked toward the ceiling.

"There is no eighth floor."

"Not anymore."

A sound came from above them.

Footsteps.

Slow.

One step at a time.

The woman whispered:

"It's here."

Mira's phone vibrated.

She looked down.

There was one new message.

YOU HAVE 31 MINUTES.

Then another.

COME TO THE STATION.

Mira looked at the woman.

"You said we should go there."

The woman nodded.

"Yes."

Mira looked back at the message.

"But who sent this?"

The woman didn't answer.

Mira's phone vibrated again.

This time there was no text.

Only a photograph.

Mira opened it.

Her hands went numb.

The photograph showed the old railway station.

And standing beneath the station clock was a little girl.

Six years old.

Wearing Mira's silver pendant.

Mira stared at the child.

Then she noticed the person standing beside her.

Her mother.

Alive.

And looking directly at the camera.

At the bottom of the photograph, someone had written:

SHE DIDN'T BRING YOU HERE TO HIDE YOU.

Mira looked at the woman.

"What did my mother do?"

The woman's face became pale.

Before she could answer, the footsteps above them stopped.

Silence.

Then a child's voice floated down the staircase.

"Mira?"

Mira froze.

The voice sounded exactly like the little girl in the photograph.

Then it whispered:

"Why did you leave me here?"

The woman grabbed Mira's hand.

"Run."

And together, they disappeared into the darkness below.
