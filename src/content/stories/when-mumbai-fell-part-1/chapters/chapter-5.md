# Chapter 5: No Way Out

The staircase ended at a basement landing.

For several seconds, the six friends stood still, listening.

Nothing followed them through the door.

Aarav slowly released the breath he'd been holding.

"Everyone okay?"

"No," Meera said. "But I'm still here."

Kabir checked his arms again. Rishi had a small scrape on one hand from the stairwell railing, but no bite marks. Dev was breathing hard enough that he could barely speak.

Sana examined the next door.

A sign read PARKING — B2.

"Maybe there's an exit through the parking area."

"Maybe," Aarav said.

"Your optimism is inspiring," Meera muttered.

They pushed through.

The basement parking area was dim and strangely quiet compared with the floors above. Emergency lights cast a yellow glow over rows of parked cars. Somewhere in the distance, a car alarm was blaring continuously.

A few people were running between vehicles.

One man was shouting into his phone. A woman tried to open the door of a car whose owner was nowhere in sight. Near a concrete pillar, a person lay motionless on the ground.

The friends stayed close to the wall.

"Don't touch anything," Sana whispered. "And don't assume anyone lying down is safe to approach."

Rishi glanced at her. "You think they're all infected?"

"I think we don't know enough to take chances."

They reached a small maintenance office. The door stood partly open.

Inside, another mall employee was hiding beneath a desk.

He looked younger than the guard they'd seen earlier. His face was wet with sweat, and he clutched a phone in one hand.

"Please," he whispered. "Please help me."

Aarav stopped at the doorway.

"Are you hurt?"

The employee shook his head too quickly.

"No. I fell. I'm just scared."

Sana noticed his trouser leg was stained with blood.

"What's that?"

He looked down.

For a moment, he didn't answer.

Then he began to cry.

"Someone bit me. I didn't know what was happening. I thought it was just a fight."

Meera covered her mouth.

The employee held up his phone.

"My mother keeps calling. I can't tell her. I can't—"

His fingers tightened around the device.

"Please don't leave me alone."

Aarav felt sick.

He wanted to help. He wanted to believe there was something they could do.

But the employee's breathing had become irregular, and his hands were shaking.

"Stay there," Aarav said gently. "We'll try to get someone to help you."

The employee nodded.

Then his phone slipped from his hand.

His whole body stiffened.

He made a choking sound and fell sideways.

Sana backed toward the door.

"Everyone out. Now."

The employee lifted his head.

The expression on his face had changed. His eyes fixed on the nearest person, and he launched himself from beneath the desk.

Kabir slammed the door shut between them.

Something struck it from the other side.

Once.

Twice.

Then the employee began clawing at the door.

Meera flinched.

They stood outside, staring at the door.

Rishi whispered, "His mother was calling him."

Nobody answered.

They moved on.

At the end of the parking area, a corridor led toward a loading zone. They found a security booth overlooking the service road, where a guard was trying to use a radio.

He looked relieved to see other people.

"Get inside," he said. "Quickly."

The friends crowded into the small booth.

"What's happening?" Aarav asked.

The guard looked exhausted.

"I don't know the whole picture. We've had reports from different parts of the city. Violent attacks. People biting people. Ambulances can't get through some roads."

"Are they infected?" Sana asked.

The guard rubbed his face.

"Some people seem to become violent after being bitten. But I don't know why. Don't trust rumours. Don't assume anyone is safe just because they're standing and talking."

Aarav thought of the two employees.

"How do we get outside?"

The guard pointed to a corridor on the map.

"There's a lower delivery route. It should connect to a loading shutter. But I lost contact with the staff down there."

"Should we try it?" Dev asked.

The guard glanced at the monitors.

On one screen, several people were trying to force their way through an exit. On another, a security worker was running down an empty corridor with something following him.

"If you stay here, you might get trapped," the guard said. "If you go, keep away from the public areas. I can't escort you."

A loud bang sounded outside the booth.

The guard turned toward the door.

"Go. Now."

They followed the marked passage.

The lower delivery corridor was narrow and smelled of dust, oil, and damp concrete. A half-open shutter stood at the far end, with daylight visible beneath it.

Aarav approached cautiously and looked through the gap.

The service road outside appeared almost normal for a moment.

A parked delivery van. A discarded cardboard box. A plastic bag moving in the wind.

Then he heard a scream.

A man ran across the road, followed by two figures moving with a strange, stumbling urgency. The man tripped, scrambled up, and disappeared behind a parked car.

The figures followed him.

Aarav stepped back.

"We can't go out that way."

Behind them came a metallic scraping sound.

They turned.

An infected delivery worker was crawling beneath the shutter at the opposite end of the corridor. His fingers scraped the concrete as he dragged himself forward.

More hands appeared in the gap behind him.

"RUN!" Sana shouted.

The friends sprinted back toward the stairwell. Kabir and Aarav pulled the door open, and everyone rushed inside.

The infected worker's hand caught the bottom of the door just before it slammed shut.

Kabir kicked it free.

They ran down another flight of stairs.

At the next landing, they found a door marked LOWER DELIVERY ACCESS. It opened onto another corridor, ending at a partially raised shutter.

For a moment, nobody moved.

There was daylight beyond it.

Real daylight.

The sound of traffic drifted inside.

Aarav cautiously approached the opening.

The street outside was full of abandoned cars. A motorcycle lay on its side. Smoke rose from somewhere beyond the buildings, and distant sirens wailed without stopping.

A few people ran past the entrance.

One of them was screaming for his brother.

The city had not become silent.

It had become unpredictable.

Rishi's phone began ringing.

He stared at the screen.

"My brother."

He answered immediately.

"Hello? Where are you?"

His brother's voice came through, rushed and frightened.

"Rishi, where the hell are you? I've been calling you!"

"I'm near the mall. We're outside, almost. What's happening?"

"I don't know! People are attacking each other near the hospital. There's something happening in other areas too. The roads are blocked. Hospitals are overwhelmed. They're telling people to stay indoors."

"Are you safe?"

"I'm home. The building gates are closed. Don't come here if the roads are dangerous. Find somewhere secure and stay there."

Rishi looked at the others.

"We'll figure it out."

"Don't do anything stupid," his brother said.

Rishi almost laughed.

"Bit late for that."

The call broke up.

He lowered his phone.

"My brother's safe at home. He says the attacks aren't just here."

Sana looked toward the road.

"We need three things. Water, somewhere secure, and a way to contact our families."

Aarav took out his phone and called his mother.

The call failed.

He tried again.

Nothing.

His thumb hovered over the screen.

He hadn't messaged her when the screaming began. He hadn't told her where he was. He had assumed there would be time.

Now he couldn't even tell whether she had received his calls.

Meera noticed his expression.

"Try again in a few minutes," she said softly.

He nodded, though it did little to ease the fear growing inside him.

They stepped onto the service road.

Across the street stood a small commercial building with metal shutters over its entrance. A security guard was standing near the doorway, watching the road.

"Please!" Rishi called. "We just need somewhere safe. We won't cause trouble."

The guard looked at their faces, then past them.

His expression changed.

Several people were moving toward the road. One stumbled into a parked car and immediately began clawing at its window.

The guard pulled the shutter down.

"Wait!" Dev shouted.

The metal slammed against the ground.

They were left outside.

Meera stared at the closed entrance.

"Great. The building has officially rejected our application."

"Can you stop?" Kabir snapped.

She turned toward him.

"What do you want me to do? Start screaming?"

"Maybe stop making jokes for five seconds!"

"Maybe stop acting like you're the only one who's scared!"

They stared at each other.

Aarav stepped between them.

"Enough. We need to move."

"Move where?" Dev asked.

Nobody answered.

Behind them, the city continued to unravel.

Ahead of them, the road stretched between shuttered shops and abandoned vehicles.

They had escaped the mall.

They had reached the street.

And they still had nowhere to go.
