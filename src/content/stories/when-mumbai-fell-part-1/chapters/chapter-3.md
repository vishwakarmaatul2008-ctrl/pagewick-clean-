# Chapter 3: The First Bite

For several seconds, the six friends stood frozen.

The human mind had a strange way of rejecting things it wasn't prepared to understand. The woman below them was covered in blood, attacking strangers, and moving with a terrifying lack of hesitation.

But the brain still tried to find an ordinary explanation.

A fight.

Drugs.

A medical emergency.

Anything except what they had joked about the previous night.

Then a man crashed into their table, sending drinks and food across the floor.

"RUN!"

The word broke the spell.

People surged toward the nearest exits. Chairs scraped against the tiles. A little boy cried for his mother. Someone shouted that there was an attack on the lower floor, while someone else screamed that the exits were blocked.

"Don't follow everyone!" Aarav shouted.

Meera grabbed his sleeve. "Then where do we go?"

"Somewhere away from the crowd!"

"Brilliant. The entire mall is a crowd!"

He didn't have an answer.

They moved away from the food court, keeping close to the wall instead of pushing into the main flow of people. Aarav tried to count his friends as they moved.

Meera.

Kabir.

Dev.

Sana.

Rishi.

Six.

Still six.

They reached a side passage near a row of closed service doors. The noise from the food court followed them, mixed with the sound of alarms beginning to ring.

A woman sat against the wall ahead of them.

Her hand was pressed to her shoulder, and blood seeped between her fingers.

"Please," she said. "Help me."

Rishi immediately stepped forward.

"Hey, are you okay?"

Aarav caught his arm.

"Wait."

"She's hurt!"

"I know. But we don't know what happened."

The woman looked up. Her face was pale, her eyes unfocused.

"Please. I need help."

Rishi crouched slightly, keeping a few feet between them.

"Can you stand? We'll call someone."

The woman suddenly lunged.

Her mouth snapped shut inches from Rishi's hand.

He stumbled backward with a shout.

"WHAT THE HELL?"

The woman scrambled toward him, making a wet, choking sound.

Meera grabbed Rishi's jacket and yanked him away.

"Move!"

They ran.

Behind them, the woman struck the wall and turned toward another person entering the passage.

Nobody stopped to see what happened next.

Aarav's phone vibrated repeatedly in his pocket.

He pulled it out while running.

Notifications flooded the screen.

*Multiple violent incidents reported across Mumbai.*

*Police urge residents to avoid affected areas.*

*Hospitals report an emergency surge.*

Then a message appeared from an unknown group chat.

*Anyone near the mall? People are attacking each other. This is not normal.*

"How many?" Sana asked.

"What?"

"How many incidents?"

"I don't know."

"Then stop looking at your phone and run!"

They reached the main escalator area.

It was a disaster.

People pushed toward the lower level, while others tried to climb upward. Several had fallen, and the people behind them couldn't stop in time. A man was shouting for someone to help his wife. Security guards were trying to force a path through the crush.

Near the bottom, the bloodied figures were still moving through the crowd.

Aarav saw one grab a shopper by the shoulder.

The shopper screamed.

Others pushed forward without even looking back.

The crowd itself had become dangerous. Even someone who never came near the infected could be crushed beneath the people trying to escape.

"Not that way!" Aarav shouted.

Kabir looked at the escalators. "So what, we stay here?"

"No!"

Sana pointed toward a marked fire exit.

"There!"

They ran to it.

Kabir grabbed the handle and pulled.

It didn't move.

He tried again.

"Locked."

"You're joking," Dev said.

Kabir shook the door violently. "Does this look like I'm joking?"

Aarav pressed his shoulder against it. Nothing.

The alarm continued ringing overhead.

Behind them, the sound of footsteps and screaming grew closer.

"Find another route," Sana said.

They turned into a narrow corridor beside a restaurant. The public area ended at a swinging kitchen door.

Meera hesitated. "We're going through the kitchen?"

"Unless you'd prefer the escalators," Aarav said.

They pushed through.

Staff members were abandoning their stations. A cook stood beside an overturned trolley, staring at his phone. Another employee was trying to drag a fallen colleague toward the back of the kitchen.

"Don't go out there!" the cook shouted as the friends passed. "Stay inside!"

Then a crash came from the restaurant entrance.

The cook turned.

A security guard stumbled through the doorway, clutching his arm. Blood ran down his sleeve.

"Help me," he gasped.

One of the employees approached him.

The guard suddenly seized the employee and bit into his shoulder.

Everyone screamed.

The employee fell, and the guard dropped onto him, snapping at his face.

Kabir stood motionless.

"Kabir!" Aarav shouted.

The guard turned toward them.

Aarav snatched a metal serving tray from a counter and hurled it across the kitchen. It struck a steel shelf with a deafening crash.

The guard's head snapped toward the noise.

"RUN!"

They burst through another door and entered a service stairwell.

The door slammed behind them.

For a few seconds, they could hear only their own breathing.

Dev bent over, hands on his knees.

"I'm officially withdrawing every joke I've ever made."

"Put it in writing," Meera said, although her voice shook.

Kabir looked at his arm, turning it over as if expecting to find teeth marks.

"Did he touch me?"

"No," Sana said. "You moved away."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

He nodded, but the confidence he'd displayed the previous night was gone.

Aarav looked up the stairwell.

A sign indicated the roof access was several floors above them.

"Up," he said.

They climbed.

At the next landing, Sana tried the door.

Locked.

Aarav pulled at the handle himself.

Nothing.

From below came a heavy thud against the stairwell door.

Then another.

Someone—or something—was trying to get in.

Meera looked down the stairs.

"Tell me that's a security guard."

Nobody answered.

A third impact shook the door.

The handle began to move.

Aarav stared at the locked roof door, then at his friends.

For the first time since the outbreak began, he had absolutely no idea what to do.
