# Chapter 1: Bro, What If Zombies Were Real?

**11:47 PM — Group Chat: The Unemployed Legends**

**Aarav:** Guys.

**Meera:** No.

**Aarav:** I haven't even said anything.

**Meera:** You said "guys" at midnight. That's already a warning.

**Dev:** Bro's about to send us a 47-minute documentary about how pigeons are government spies.

**Aarav:** Worse.

A video link appeared in the chat.

**Aarav:** 10 Signs a Zombie Outbreak Could Happen Tomorrow.

**Kabir:** 💀💀💀

**Rishi:** If zombies come tomorrow, I'm dying first. I have zero interest in cardio.

**Sana:** That's because you consider walking to the kitchen exercise.

**Rishi:** And I stand by that lifestyle.

Aarav sent another message.

**Aarav:** Laugh all you want. An outbreak isn't completely impossible. Some infections can affect the brain and behaviour. We already have diseases that can cause aggression and confusion.

**Meera:** There's a difference between a person being sick and someone eating your face, Professor.

**Aarav:** Obviously. I'm talking about the possibility of a disease causing extreme behavioural changes.

**Dev:** Imagine getting bitten and your last words are, "Guys, don't forget to like and subscribe."

**Kabir:** If zombies happen, I'm surviving. Easy.

**Sana:** You cried when a cockroach flew near you.

**Kabir:** It attacked without warning. That's different.

**Meera:** The cockroach had better survival instincts than you.

The chat filled with laughing emojis.

Aarav smiled at his phone, but he wasn't entirely joking.

He had watched enough documentaries and survival videos to know that disasters rarely unfolded the way people imagined. The danger wasn't always the thing everyone feared. Sometimes it was the crowd running toward a locked exit, the person hiding an injury, or the road everyone assumed would be safe.

He typed again.

**Aarav:** Seriously, though. If something like that happened in Mumbai, we'd be finished.

**Rishi:** Speak for yourself. I'll simply move to Switzerland.

**Meera:** You'll need a passport first.

**Rishi:** Minor administrative issue.

**Aarav:** Think about it. Crowded local trains. Traffic everywhere. Hospitals packed. Shopping malls full of people. If everyone panics at once, even normal roads become dangerous.

**Sana:** He's not wrong about the crowd part.

**Dev:** Okay, survival experts. What's the plan?

**Aarav:** First, don't blindly follow a crowd. Second, keep your phone charged. Third, share your location with people you trust. Fourth, if communications are working, agree on a meeting point. And don't approach someone acting violently just because you want to help.

**Meera:** That's a lot of preparation for a disease that doesn't exist like that.

**Aarav:** Preparation costs nothing.

**Kabir:** Rule five: find a weapon.

**Sana:** Rule six: don't let Kabir choose the weapon.

**Rishi:** Rule seven: if I die, delete my search history.

**Dev:** Rule eight: if Dev dies, upload his unfinished memes.

**Meera:** Rule nine: Aarav is not allowed to give a TED Talk while we're being eaten.

**Aarav:** You people would die in the first ten minutes.

**Kabir:** Bro, you would die explaining the science behind the bite.

Aarav rolled his eyes.

The conversation slowly drifted toward college gossip, food, and Rishi's ongoing inability to arrive anywhere on time.

Then Rishi sent a voice note.

"Tomorrow. Phoenix Mall. Two p.m. Food, shopping, and absolutely no zombie discussions. I'm serious."

**Meera:** You're the one who brought it up yesterday.

**Rishi:** I am a changed man.

**Sana:** You said that last week before arriving forty minutes late.

**Rishi:** Growth takes time.

**Dev:** I'll bring my power bank. If we get trapped, at least I can play games.

**Kabir:** I'll bring my survival instincts.

**Meera:** Can you borrow some?

Aarav laughed and put his phone down.

The room was quiet. Outside, the city continued as it always did—distant horns, the occasional motorcycle, and the hum of traffic that never seemed to disappear completely.

He imagined a crowded mall. An alarm going off. People screaming. Every exit blocked by people trying to escape at the same time.

He imagined the lights going out.

Then he shook his head.

It was ridiculous.

Tomorrow would be normal. They would eat too much, make stupid jokes, and complain about the prices of everything.

The video had been entertainment. Nothing more.

Still, before sleeping, Aarav checked his phone battery and plugged it into the charger.

Just in case.

He didn't know that, less than twenty-four hours later, he would wish he had prepared for far more than a hypothetical conversation.
