# Chapter 4: The Escalators Are Blocked

"Okay," Aarav said, trying to keep his voice steady. "We need another way out."

"Excellent," Meera replied. "I was worried we were going to live here."

The stairwell door below them rattled again.

Sana looked at the roof access door.

"If this is locked, we can't keep going up."

"Then we go down," Kabir said.

"Down toward the infected?" Dev asked.

"Up toward a locked door, then?"

Nobody had a better idea.

They hurried back down one flight and pushed through a side door into a service corridor. The corridor smelled of cleaning chemicals and warm electrical equipment. Pipes ran along the ceiling, and fluorescent lights flickered overhead.

A sign pointed toward the restaurant and loading area.

They moved quickly, listening for footsteps behind them.

At the end of the corridor, a narrow window overlooked the mall's central atrium.

Aarav looked through it.

The main escalators were almost completely blocked. People were pushing against one another, trying to move both up and down. Several had fallen, and the people behind them couldn't stop in time. The infected moved through the confusion, attacking anyone they could reach.

A security guard tried to direct people toward another exit, but nobody seemed to hear him.

A woman fell near the escalator.

The people behind her tripped over her.

Aarav pulled away from the window.

"We can't go that way."

"Nobody was suggesting it," Sana said quietly.

They reached a small office door. It was unlocked.

Inside, an employee sat under a desk, shaking. He wore a mall uniform, and his hands were pressed tightly against his stomach.

"Please," he whispered. "Don't leave me."

Meera crouched near the doorway.

"Are you hurt?"

The man nodded. Tears ran down his face.

"I got bitten. One of them attacked me near the service lift. I thought I could hide here."

He looked up at them.

"I have a daughter. She's only six. I was supposed to pick her up today."

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Rishi swallowed. "We can try calling someone. Maybe an ambulance—"

"No." The employee shook his head violently. "Don't come close."

He pulled his sleeve back.

The bite was deep and ugly. The skin around it had darkened, and his hand trembled uncontrollably.

Aarav remembered the videos from earlier.

"How long ago?" he asked.

"Ten minutes. Maybe fifteen."

The employee squeezed his eyes shut.

"Please. If you see my daughter, tell her I—"

His body jerked.

He made a choking sound and lunged toward Meera.

She screamed and stumbled backward.

Kabir slammed the office door shut between them.

Something struck it from the other side.

Meera stared at the door, breathing hard.

"He was just asking for help."

"I know," Aarav said.

Nobody spoke for a moment.

They had been joking about zombies less than a day ago. Now they were leaving frightened, injured people behind because they didn't know who might turn next.

They continued down the corridor.

A few minutes later, they found a security booth near a staff-only passage. The door was open, and a radio crackled on the desk.

An older security guard stood beside a bank of monitors. Sweat darkened his uniform.

He looked at the six teenagers and shook his head.

"You shouldn't be here."

"We're trying to get out," Aarav said.

"Everyone is trying to get out."

The guard pointed toward the screens.

One camera showed the food court in chaos. Another showed a corridor crowded with people pushing against a shutter. A third showed an escalator with several motionless figures lying across the steps.

"Are the exits open?" Sana asked.

"Some. Not all. The main entrances are jammed, and people are pushing against the doors. If you go there, you might get crushed before you reach the street."

"Then where do we go?" Dev asked.

The guard hesitated before pointing at a map on the wall.

"There's a service staircase that leads toward the lower delivery corridor. Staff use it to move stock. It might take you to a loading exit."

"Might?" Meera repeated.

The guard gave her a tired look.

"I can't promise you anything."

A crash echoed somewhere beyond the booth.

The guard grabbed his radio.

"Control, this is security. I need an update on the lower delivery area."

Static answered.

He tried again.

Nothing.

Then a voice came through, broken by interference.

"—don't open the shutter—repeat, do not—"

The transmission cut off.

The guard stared at the radio.

"Go," he said. "Now."

They hurried toward the service staircase.

As they descended, the sounds of the mall became distant and muffled. The concrete walls were bare, the lights dimmer than those upstairs.

At the bottom, a heavy door opened into a delivery corridor.

Metal shutters lined one side. Plastic crates and hand trolleys cluttered the floor.

A delivery worker stood near the far end, facing the wall.

"Sir?" Rishi called cautiously.

The worker didn't respond.

His shirt was torn. One hand hung at an unnatural angle.

Aarav stopped.

"Don't go near him."

The worker slowly turned.

Blood covered his mouth.

Then he charged.

They ran toward the nearest stairwell door. Kabir shoved it open, and the group piled through.

Aarav slammed it shut behind them.

Something crashed against the other side.

The door shuddered in its frame.

Dev stared at it.

"Please tell me that door is stronger than my college attendance record."

Another impact made the metal vibrate.

Meera grabbed his arm.

"Save your jokes for when we're alive."

The six friends descended into the dark, unsure where the staircase would lead.

Above them, the infected continued pounding on the door.

Below them, the city waited.

And they were running out of places to hide.
