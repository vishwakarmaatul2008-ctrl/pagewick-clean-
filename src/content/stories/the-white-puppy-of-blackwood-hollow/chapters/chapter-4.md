# Chapter 4 — The Final Skin

The fog inside the Appalachian woods didn't drift—it hung frozen between the ancient hemlocks like wet, gray linen.

The moment the five children crossed the boundary fence, the ambient noise of the town died. No wind rustled the high canopy. No birds chirped. The ground beneath their boots felt soft and spongy, like walking over rotting flesh covered in moss.

"Aarav... we should turn back," Sam whispered, his voice trembling as he clutched his own elbows. The hypnotic trance that had pulled them over the fence was wearing off, replaced by raw, paralyzing terror.

"We can't," Aarav said, his hand locked around Maya's wrist so hard his knuckles were white. He looked behind them.

The boundary fence was gone. Where the park and the town should have been, there was only an endless wall of black pine trees swallowed by heavy gray mist. The path had erased itself.

"Aarav..." Maya whined softly, staring at the ground. "My feet feel heavy."

"Keep walking," Aarav ordered, his eyes darting frantically through the fog. "Stay together. Nobody let go of anyone's hand!"

They stumbled forward into a clearing—a circular break in the trees centered around an ancient, dry stone well. The air here was heavy with a stench so foul it made Chloe gag: wet fur, copper, and sweet, decaying earth.

Sitting on the edge of the stone well was the white puppy.

It sat perfectly upright, illuminated by a single shaft of weak, gray light cutting through the canopy.

"Look," Leo whispered, pointing a shaking finger. "It's... it's not moving."

The white puppy wasn't breathing. It sat frozen like a porcelain doll. Then, a sharp, wet ripping sound echoed through the silent clearing.

A jagged red tear split down the center of the puppy's back, right along its spine.

The white fur didn't bleed—it peeled back like old, waterlogged paper, folding outward to reveal the thick, glistening black mass pulsing underneath. Two long, jointed limbs—resembling the skeletal legs of a massive spider covered in matted human hair—pushed out from the split seam of the puppy's coat.

Maya let out a piercing scream. Sam and Chloe fell backward into the dirt, scrambling frantically on their hands and knees.

The white skin dropped away completely, sloughing off onto the stones like a discarded coat. What rose from the shell was a tall, hunched, monstrous chimera.

Its body was an unnatural, elongated canine frame, but its front limbs ended in pale, malformed human hands with long, yellowed nails. Its skin was a patchwork of coarse black fur and patches of gray, bruised human flesh stitched together seamlessly.

Slowly, the creature turned its head toward them.

Its skull was a narrow, bone-white canine shape, but draped over it were long, wet strands of pitch-black human hair. Set deep within the pale bone were two hollow, unblinking human eyes that locked directly onto Aarav.

Its jaw dropped—unhinging until the lower bone hung loosely against its neck—and it let out a sound.

It wasn't a roar. It was a chorus of voices speaking simultaneously in a wet, rasping whisper:

"Aarav... Maya... Leo... Sam... Chloe..."

It recited all five of their names, layering their mothers' voices, their own past screams, and the gentle laughter from the park into a terrifying, mechanical hum.

The Chimera dropped onto all four limbs, its elbows bending backward with sick, cracking pops as it crept toward them across the clearing. Its long human hair dragged through the wet leaves.

"Run!" Aarav screamed, shoving Maya behind him.

The five children scattered into the mist.

Aarav grabbed Maya by the hand and sprinted toward a dense cluster of pines. Behind them, the sharp, wet thud of four heavy limbs hammering the mud came fast and relentless.

Thud-thud-thud-thud.

"Aarav! Help!" Chloe's voice shrieked from fifty yards to their left.

Aarav didn't stop. He couldn't. He dragged Maya through the tearing briars, his heart pounding against his ribs like a trapped bird. Another scream cut through the woods—Sam's voice—abruptly cut short by a wet, heavy crunch.

"Don't look back, Maya! Don't look back!" Aarav sobbed, tears blurring his vision as he sprinted blindly into the gray void.

The fog grew so thick they couldn't see five feet ahead. Aarav's lungs burned like fire. Suddenly, his foot caught on a exposed tree root, and both he and Maya tumbled violently down a muddy slope, crashing into a shallow ravine.

Aarav hit the bottom hard, gasping for air, mud caking his face. He scrambled up immediately, reaching out in the dark mist.

"Maya! Maya, where are you?!"

A small figure stood five feet away in the fog, wearing a bright yellow raincoat.

Aarav let out a breath of pure relief and stepped forward. "Maya! Thank God—grab my hand, we have to keep—"

The figure in the yellow raincoat slowly turned around.

Tucked behind its left ear was a frayed pink hair ribbon. But as the fog parted, Aarav saw the face beneath the hood.

It wasn't Maya.

It was the Chimera. The long, bone-white canine face was stretched tight inside the yellow hood, its hollow human eyes staring down at him. From beneath the hem of the yellow raincoat, Maya's small, mud-stained boots hung limply, two inches off the ground, attached to the creature's lower joints.

It tilted its head ninety degrees to the side, its jaw swinging open.

And from deep within its rotting chest, it spoke in Maya's soft, innocent voice:

"Aarav... why didn't you keep me in your room?"
