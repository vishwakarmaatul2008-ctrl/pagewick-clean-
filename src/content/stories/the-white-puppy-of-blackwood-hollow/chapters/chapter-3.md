# Chapter 3 — The Gathering at the Park

The rain returned by late afternoon, turning the sky into a bruised shade of purple.

Aarav couldn't stay in his house. The heavy, suffocating atmosphere hadn't lifted even after he dumped the creature at the treeline. The hallway outside his bedroom still smelled of damp earth and decayed wood, and Bruno remained locked in the laundry room, shivering uncontrollably whenever someone walked past.

At 4:00 PM, Aarav met the rest of the group under the shelter of the park gazebo.

Maya, Leo, Sam, and Chloe were already there. Maya sat on a rusted bench, picking at the fraying edges of her raincoat. Her face was pale, and she kept staring down at her bare wrist where her pink hair ribbon used to be.

"Did you really get rid of it?" Leo asked, his voice low as he leaned against the wooden support pillar. "Your dad didn't let you keep it?"

"It wasn't a dog, Leo," Aarav said, his voice cold and flat. He looked around at the four of them, his knuckles white as he clenched his fists in his jacket pockets. "I'm serious. That thing... it talked last night. It used my mom's voice."

Sam scoffed, though he shifted uncomfortably on his feet. "Shut up, Aarav. Dogs can't talk. You were just scared because of the thunder."

"It knew Maya's name!" Aarav snapped, stepping directly up to Sam. "It took her hair ribbon before any of us even noticed it was gone. It stood up on its hind legs under my bed and its jaw dropped open like a broken hinge. My mom looked at it and almost passed out because the air in the room felt dead!"

Chloe drew her knees up to her chest on the bench. "I had a dream about it last night," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the wind rustling the nearby pines. "I dreamed the puppy came to my window. But when I opened the glass, its legs just kept stretching longer and longer until it was tall enough to look down at me from the second floor. It asked me if I wanted to play in the woods."

Silence fell over the gazebo. Nobody laughed. The atmosphere felt unnervingly tense, as if the forest across the fence was leaning in to listen to every word they said.

"We shouldn't have gone into the Miller house," Maya murmured quietly, clutching Aarav's sleeve. "The old people in town say the hollow belongs to things that don't have names. They say if you bring something out of those woods, it takes piece of you back with it."

"It's just a story to keep kids from wandering off," Leo said, though he wouldn't look toward the tree line.

Crack.

The sound of a snapping branch echoed from the overgrown brush across the rusty boundary fence.

All five children spun around.

Standing right at the edge of the dark, dense pine forest, framed by the rotting black wood of the old Miller house, was the white puppy.

It sat perfectly still in the tall, wet grass. Its pure white coat was completely dry despite the damp air. But as they stared at it, Aarav felt a cold spike of horror shoot down his spine.

Something was wrong with how it moved.

Slowly, the white puppy began walking toward the boundary fence. Its gait was stiff and jointless, like a puppet being pulled by invisible, clumsy strings. Its front left leg moved out of sync with its back legs, its shoulder collapsing inward with every step before snapping back into place with an audible thump.

It stopped ten feet from the gazebo, separated from them only by the broken wooden fence.

It didn't blink. It didn't sniff the air. Its coal-black eyes were fixed entirely on Maya.

"Maya..." Sam whispered, backing away slowly. "Don't look at it."

The white puppy turned its body toward the forest. But instead of running away, it tilted its head all the way back until its nose pointed straight up at the overcast sky.

It opened its mouth.

A sound didn't come out of its throat—it came from the forest behind it. A soft, clear, familiar laugh echoed through the trees. It was the exact sound of Maya giggling during their game of hide-and-seek three days ago.

Then, the puppy spoke. The voice drifted out from its narrow jaw, layered with three distinct tones—Aarav's mother, Chloe's soft whisper, and Maya's childhood laugh—all bleeding into one mechanical, chorus-like hum:

"Come back... we left the rest of you in the house..."

The white puppy turned and trotted into the deep, black woods, its white tail swaying in a bizarre, hypnotic rhythm.

Maya blinked, her eyes going wide and vacant. Without a word, she stepped off the gazebo platform and began walking toward the boundary fence, following the path the creature had taken into the dark hollow.

"Maya! Stop!" Aarav screamed, grabbing her arm.

"It has my ribbon, Aarav," Maya said, her voice completely devoid of emotion, staring straight into the pitch-black tree line. "It said if we come get it, we don't have to go home."

"It's trying to lure us in!" Aarav yelled, pulling her back. But as he looked around, he saw Leo, Sam, and Chloe staring at the dark treeline as if hypnotized. The forest seemed to pull at them, the silence between the tall pines heavy, suffocating, and absolute.

From deep within the dark Appalachian woods, the laughter rang out again—louder this time, echoing from multiple directions at once.

The trap was already set. And one by one, the five children stepped over the boundary fence into the trees.
