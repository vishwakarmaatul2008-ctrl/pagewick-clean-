# Chapter 1 — The White Puppy in the Rain

The rain in Blackwood Hollow didn't fall in drops; it came down in sheets of heavy, gray noise that swallowed the Appalachian hills whole.

It was 4:30 PM, but the storm had turned the afternoon sky into the bruised black of midnight. At the edge of the abandoned town park, five kids were huddled under the rusting metal roof of the old gazebo: nine-year-old Aarav, his younger sister Maya, and their neighbors Leo, Sam, and Chloe.

"We can't stay here," Leo shouted over the deafening roar of water hammering the metal roof. "The wind is blowing the rain straight in!"

"My dad said never to go past the boundary fence," Maya whimpered, clutching her yellow raincoat tightly around her chest.

Directly across the fence, half-buried in overgrown brambles and mountain fog, sat the old Miller house. Abandoned for over twenty years, its black wooden frame sagged like a rotting ribcage against the treeline. Local kids swore the land around it was cursed, but right now, it was the only roof with dry wooden floors.

"It's just wood and rusty nails," Aarav said, taking Maya by the hand. "We wait out the storm on the porch, then we run home. Come on."

The five children scrambled over the broken wooden fence, their boots squelching in the deep, black mud, and dashed onto the porch of the abandoned house. Aarav pushed against the warped front door. With a heavy, groan-like creak, it swung inward, revealing the dark pitch of the living room.

They stepped inside, the air instantly turning cold, thick with the smell of wet earth and decayed pine.

Then, they heard it.

It wasn't the wind. It was a faint, wet whimpering sound coming from the far corner of the rotting hallway.

"What is that?" Chloe whispered, stepping back toward the open doorway.

Aarav pulled out his small pocket flashlight and clicked it on. The narrow beam of light cut through the floating dust particles, illuminating the baseboard of the hallway.

Tucked into the shadow of a fallen closet door was a puppy.

It was tiny—small enough to fit inside a shoe box—with fur as pure and white as fresh winter snow. It sat completely still, shivering slightly, looking up into the flash of light with large, round black eyes.

"Oh my god," Maya gasped, breaking away from Aarav and dropping to her knees. "It's so sweet!"

"Wait, Maya, don't touch it!" Aarav warned, stepping forward.

But the puppy didn't move or snap. As Maya reached out, it gently pressed its small, wet pink nose against her palm. Strange, Aarav thought. The storm outside was drenched in mud and clay, yet this little creature's white coat was spotless, bone-dry, and clean.

"We can't leave it here," Leo said, kneeling down beside them. "It'll freeze when night comes."

"My parents will kill me if I bring a dog home," Sam muttered nervously, looking back at the dark woods outside.

"I'll take him," Aarav said instantly. He looked at the shivering white bundle. "Just for tonight. We'll hide him in my room until the storm passes, then figure out who he belongs to."

Aarav scooped the puppy into his arms. It felt strangely heavy for its size—dense, like a wet bag of sand—and it didn't make a single sound. It didn't bark. It didn't whine. It just rested its small head against Aarav's chest, its cold black eyes staring straight up at Aarav's throat.

By 8:00 PM, the storm had eased into a low, droning hum against the windowpanes of Aarav's family home. Aarav had successfully snuck the puppy upstairs inside his backpack, wrapped it in an old woolen blanket, and tucked it beneath the foot of his bed.

During dinner, the puppy hadn't made a sound. Aarav had slipped a piece of cooked chicken down into the blanket, but when he checked later, the meat was untouched. The puppy was simply sitting upright in the dark under the bed, staring at the wall.

At 2:14 AM, Aarav woke up.

The house was dead silent. The rain had stopped completely.

A slow, rhythmic sound was coming from the floor right next to his mattress.

Scritch. Scritch. Scritch.

It sounded like hard, sharp nails dragging slowly across the wooden floorboards.

"Puppy?" Aarav whispered into the dark room, his heart suddenly picking up speed. "Hey... quiet. You'll wake up Mom and Dad."

The scratching stopped.

Aarav reached over to his nightstand, grabbed his phone, and clicked the screen on. The bright glare illuminated the edge of his bedsheets.

He leaned over the side of the mattress and pointed the light underneath the bed frame.

The white puppy wasn't lying down.

It was standing on its hind legs in the narrow space between the floor and the bed springs. But its back legs weren't bent like a dog's—they were stretched long and rigid, its spine twisting at an impossible, sickening angle to keep its body upright.

Its front paws hung limply at its sides like hollow sleeves.

And as the phone light hit its face, the puppy slowly tilted its head all the way to its shoulder—a full ninety degrees. Its tiny jaw unhinged, dropping open far wider than its skull should allow, revealing a row of sharp, needle-like human teeth.

It didn't bark.

It leaned out from under the bed, its coal-black eyes reflecting no light at all, and spoke in a soft, clear, perfect imitation of Aarav's mother:

"Aarav... come down on the floor with me."
