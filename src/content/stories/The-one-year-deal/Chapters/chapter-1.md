# Chapter 1 – The Marriage Ultimatum

"It is every day, Grandma. I already told you---I don't want to get married."

Damien didn't even look up from the documents spread across his desk.

His grandmother sat opposite him, completely unimpressed.

"You're going to reach thirty soon. At least start dating a girl," she said. "Or I'll choose one for you myself."

Damien finally lifted his eyes from the paperwork.

"Grandma, I don't want to date or marry anyone right now. I need to focus on the business."

"Business, business, business." She shook her head. "It's been two years since you became CEO, dummy, and you still haven't even started dating."

Damien leaned back in his chair with a quiet sigh.

At twenty-seven, he had already become the CEO of the Moretti family's enormous business empire.

But becoming powerful had never been his goal.

Surviving had been.

The Moretti family was one of the most powerful families in the country, but behind the polished suits, luxury cars, and billion-dollar businesses was a family where nobody completely trusted anyone.

In the Moretti family, blood didn't always mean loyalty.

People could smile at each other during dinner and betray each other the next morning if money or power was involved.

Damien's father had once been the head of that family.

He had been a highly successful business tycoon with everything a man could want---a powerful position, a beautiful wife, and a young son he adored.

Damien had been fifteen when everything changed.

His parents died in a car accident.

And before the grief had even settled, relatives began appearing at the Moretti estate.

Not to comfort him.

Not to ask whether he was okay.

They came for his father's property.

His shares.

His money.

His inheritance.

Everyone wanted a piece of what the fifteen-year-old boy had been left with.

Everyone except his grandmother.

She had stood between Damien and the entire family.

She took custody of him, pushed the greedy relatives away, and made one decision that changed his life.

She would make Damien independent.

While she handled the business his father had left behind, she raised Damien herself.

She taught him how to understand business.

How to recognize betrayal.

How to protect himself.

How to make decisions without depending on anyone.

And eventually, she prepared him to take his father's place.

Two years ago, Damien became CEO.

Now, at twenty-seven, he was already one of the youngest business tycoons in the country.

But the boy his grandmother had raised had become extremely different from the teenager she once knew.

Damien was workaholic, cold, introverted, and frighteningly sharp-minded.

He could sit through ten hours of negotiations without showing the slightest sign of exhaustion.

He rarely trusted people.

He rarely spoke unnecessarily.

And despite countless women trying to get his attention, he had never dated a single woman in his entire life.

His grandmother was the only exception to his cold nature.

With everyone else, Damien kept his distance.

With her, he was patient.

He listened.

He argued.

And sometimes, when nobody else was around, he even smiled.

His grandmother was also the only person who could threaten him without consequences.

Damien had a striking appearance that only added to the intimidating presence surrounding him.

He stood around six-foot-two, with broad shoulders and a lean, athletic build. His thick black hair was usually styled neatly, although the longer he worked, the more a few strands fell naturally over his forehead.

His eyes were deep dark brown, almost black, and naturally intense.

A sharp jawline, defined cheekbones, straight nose, and a faint scar near his right eyebrow gave his handsome face an edge that made strangers hesitate before approaching him.

Today, he was dressed in a perfectly fitted charcoal suit with a white shirt, his sleeves slightly rolled up after another long day at the office.

He looked every bit like the young CEO everyone feared.

His grandmother, however, saw none of that.

To her, he was still the stubborn boy she had raised.

"Listen to me," she said.

Damien looked at her.

"If you don't get married and have children before you turn thirty, I'll fire you from the CEO position."

Damien's eyebrows lifted.

"You can't be serious."

"I am completely serious."

"Grandma---"

"Don't forget, I'm still the chairman of the company."

She leaned back confidently.

"And the board members are on my side---or at least most of them are."

Damien stared at her.

She stared back.

Neither moved.

Finally, Damien sighed.

"Fine."

His grandmother smiled.

"But I can't leave the business every day and go searching for a girlfriend."

"You don't have to."

Damien narrowed his eyes.

That tone worried him.

His grandmother's smile grew wider.

"I have a good idea."

"What idea?"

"We'll throw a party."

Damien immediately frowned.

"A party?"

"Yes. A very big one."

"I don't like where this is going."

"We'll invite the leaders of every major company we have connections with."

Damien slowly put down his pen.

"And their daughters," she added.

He stared at her.

"There will be plenty of girls for you to meet. Beautiful, educated, wealthy girls from powerful families."

"Grandma, I'm not choosing a wife from a business conference."

"Then don't call it a business conference."

She smiled.

"Call it a party."

Damien rubbed his forehead.

"And if I don't like anyone?"

"Then I'll choose someone for you."

He looked at her in disbelief.

"But what if I don't like your choice either?"

His grandmother calmly picked up her teacup.

"My choices are always right for you."

Damien gave her a long, tired look.

"And don't forget," she added with a smug smile, "I made you this capable."

Damien couldn't argue with that.

She had.

Every skill he possessed had been sharpened under her guidance.

Every decision he made had been influenced by the lessons she had taught him.

She had protected him when everyone else wanted his inheritance.

So even though he hated the idea...

Damien had never been able to refuse his grandmother.
