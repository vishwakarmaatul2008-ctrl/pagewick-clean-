# Chapter 2: Someone Behind the Door

"Please..."

The woman's voice was barely audible through the apartment door.

Meera stared at the thin line of blood spreading across the floor. Her fingers trembled around the strap of her backpack.

"She's alive," she whispered.

"Maybe," Aarav replied.

"What do you mean, maybe? We heard her!"

"We also saw what happened to that man outside."

Meera looked at him in disbelief.

"So we're just going to leave her here?"

Aarav didn't answer immediately.

He wanted to help. All of them did. But the memory of the infected employee at Phoenix Mall was still fresh in his mind. One moment, that man had looked frightened and confused. The next, he had attacked them without hesitation.

They had learned the hard way that a familiar voice didn't mean someone was safe.

The phone inside the handbag stopped ringing.

For a moment, the corridor became completely silent.

Then came three slow knocks from inside the apartment.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

"Please," the woman whispered again. "I know you're out there."

Kabir stepped closer to the door.

"Who are you?" he called.

"My name is Nandini. I live here with my husband and my little boy."

Aarav exchanged a glance with Dev.

"Are you hurt?" Aarav asked.

There was a pause.

"Yes."

"How?"

"I fell. Please, my son is inside. I can't get up."

Meera immediately moved toward the handle.

Aarav blocked her.

"Wait."

He looked through the small gap beneath the door. The blood was coming from somewhere deeper inside the apartment, but he couldn't see anyone's feet.

"Is your husband with you?" he asked.

Silence.

"Ma'am?"

"My husband..." Her voice broke. "He went outside yesterday. He hasn't come back."

"Is your son hurt?"

"No. He's hiding in the bedroom."

Something about her answer made Aarav uneasy.

He couldn't explain why.

"Can you come to the door?" he asked.

"I can't."

"Why not?"

"I told you. I'm hurt."

Behind them, Rishi shifted impatiently.

"Bro, we can't stand here all morning."

Sana shot him a warning look.

Aarav leaned closer to the door.

"Listen carefully. We're going to help you, but we need to know if you've been bitten. Have you been attacked by anyone?"

The woman didn't respond.

"Ma'am?"

"I haven't been bitten," she finally said.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes!"

Her sudden shout echoed through the corridor.

Everyone stepped back.

Something crashed inside the apartment.

Then a child began crying.

"Mumma?"

The woman's voice changed instantly.

"Shh, baby. Stay in the room."

Aarav tightened his grip on the iron pipe.

"What's happening in there?"

"Nothing. Please, just open the door."

Dev crouched beside the lock.

"It's an ordinary door lock. I might be able to open it if it's not bolted from inside."

"And if something attacks us?" Kabir asked.

"Then we run."

"Brilliant plan."

"Do you have a better one?"

Before Kabir could answer, a loud bang came from inside the apartment.

The child screamed.

"MU M MA!"

Meera flinched.

"We have to do something!"

Aarav closed his eyes for a second.

He remembered the people at the mall. The ones who had been screaming for help while everyone pushed past them. He remembered how quickly panic had turned strangers into enemies.

He didn't want to become someone who walked away from every person in danger.

But he couldn't risk six lives without thinking.

"Dev, check the lock," he said. "Kabir, stay behind me. Everyone else, keep your distance from the doorway."

Dev worked quickly with a small metal tool from his pocket. After several tense seconds, the lock clicked.

He looked up.

"It's open."

Aarav pushed the door inward.

The smell hit them first.

Blood.

Sweat.

And something rotten.

The living room was a mess. A chair lay overturned beside a shattered television. Broken plates covered the floor, and a dark trail of blood led toward the hallway.

A woman in a pale blue nightdress was sitting against the wall near the kitchen. Her left leg was twisted beneath her, and blood soaked the fabric around her ankle.

She looked exhausted.

Terrified.

Human.

"Please," she whispered. "My son."

Aarav entered cautiously, keeping the iron pipe raised.

"Where is he?"

"Bedroom. At the end."

Meera rushed toward the hallway, but Sana grabbed her arm.

"Together," Sana whispered.

They moved down the corridor.

The first bedroom door stood open.

Inside, a little boy of about six was hiding beneath a bed, clutching a stuffed dinosaur. His face was wet with tears.

When he saw them, he scrambled backward.

"Don't let him take me!"

Meera knelt beside the bed.

"Hey, hey. It's okay. We're not going to hurt you."

The boy shook his head violently.

"Papa is different!"

Everyone froze.

Aarav slowly turned toward the living room.

The woman was staring at them.

"What did he mean?" Aarav asked.

She began crying.

"Please don't leave us."

A heavy impact shook the wall behind them.

BANG!

The little boy screamed and crawled farther beneath the bed.

Another impact followed.

BANG!

Aarav moved toward the bedroom doorway.

The noise was coming from a second door across the hallway. It was closed, and something was throwing itself against it from the other side.

A low growl seeped through the wood.

Kabir appeared behind Aarav.

"Tell me that's not what I think it is."

The door shook again.

Aarav backed away.

"Everybody out. Now."

Meera reached for the boy.

"Come with us, sweetheart."

He refused to move.

"My papa is inside."

The next impact cracked the doorframe.

The woman shouted from the living room.

"Don't open that door!"

Aarav turned toward her.

"What's in there?"

She pressed both hands against her face.

"My husband."

Silence swallowed the corridor.

Then she spoke again, her voice breaking.

"He came home last night. He was bleeding, but he said he was fine. He said he only needed to sleep."

The door shook.

"He started screaming at around three in the morning. Then he tried to bite me."

The boy began sobbing beneath the bed.

"I locked him in the spare room," the woman continued. "I thought he would calm down. I thought the fever would pass."

A loud crack sounded from the door.

"He wasn't sick like a normal person," she whispered. "He kept saying he was hungry."

The handle began turning.

Aarav didn't wait.

"Take the boy. We're leaving!"

Meera reached under the bed and gently pulled the child toward her. Sana helped him stand, while Dev and Kabir rushed back toward the living room.

The woman tried to push herself upright but cried out in pain.

"My son!"

"We're taking him!" Meera promised.

"Please, don't leave me!"

Aarav hesitated.

He could see the fear in her eyes. She wasn't pretending. She was a mother watching strangers carry her child away while something terrible waited behind a door.

He couldn't simply abandon her.

"Dev, help me."

Together, they lifted the woman carefully. She screamed when they moved her injured leg, but she forced herself to keep quiet.

Behind them, the bedroom doorframe splintered.

A pale hand thrust through the broken wood.

The child began screaming again.

"RUN!" Aarav shouted.

They stumbled into the corridor.

Kabir slammed the apartment door behind them just as something burst through the spare-room door.

The crash echoed through the building.

For one terrible second, they heard footsteps rushing across the apartment.

Then the infected man struck the front door.

Once.

Twice.

The entire door shook in its frame.

Aarav and Dev half-carried the injured woman toward the staircase. Meera held the boy's hand so tightly that he nearly stumbled.

They reached the landing and descended as fast as they could manage.

When they returned to the courtyard, Rishi rushed toward them.

"What happened?"

"No time!" Aarav shouted. "Get the entrance ready!"

Kabir and Rishi shoved the wooden table back into place while Sana helped the woman sit against the wall. Meera crouched beside the boy, who had buried his face against her shoulder.

The infected man's pounding continued upstairs.

Then, slowly, it stopped.

Nobody moved.

They listened.

A faint dragging sound travelled across the ceiling above them.

One step.

Then another.

Then silence.

Rishi looked up.

"Please tell me he can't get down here."

Nobody answered.

The injured woman began shaking uncontrollably.

"My husband," she whispered.

Aarav knelt in front of her.

"What's your name?"

"Nandini."

"Okay, Nandini. We need to clean your wound. Do you know what bit you?"

She looked at him.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she began crying harder.

"It wasn't my husband."

Aarav felt his stomach tighten.

"What?"

Nandini looked toward the little boy.

"Yesterday, before my husband came home, I went downstairs to look for help. There was a man in the parking area. He looked injured, so I tried to help him."

Her voice trembled.

"He grabbed me."

Aarav stared at the blood around her ankle.

"Did he bite you?"

Nandini nodded.

Meera slowly pulled away from her.

Dev took a step backward.

"No," Sana whispered. "No, no, no."

Nandini reached for Aarav's hand.

"Please. I didn't know what was happening. I thought it was just a wound."

Aarav couldn't speak.

He had seen what happened to people who were bitten.

He had watched an infected employee change in front of his eyes at Phoenix Mall.

He remembered the way the man's expression had gone blank.

The way his body had jerked.

The way he had attacked without recognizing anyone.

Nandini looked down at her wound.

"How long does it take?"

Nobody answered.

"How long before I become like them?"

Her little boy looked up at her.

"Mumma?"

She forced a smile, but tears ran down her face.

"Come here, baby."

He wrapped his arms around her neck.

Aarav looked at his friends.

They had barely survived one night.

Now they had a frightened child, an injured woman, limited supplies, no reliable communication, and a question none of them wanted to answer.

Could they save her?

Or would helping her put all six of them in danger?

Outside the courtyard, the infected man at the gate had finally stopped moving.

For the first time that morning, the street was quiet.

Then a loudspeaker crackled somewhere beyond the buildings.

A distorted voice broke through the silence.

"Attention, residents of Mumbai. This is an emergency announcement. Remain indoors. Do not approach infected individuals. Emergency services are currently overwhelmed."

The message repeated.

Then the voice changed.

"Anyone who has been bitten must immediately isolate themselves from others."

Nandini shut her eyes.

Her little boy tightened his arms around her.

Aarav looked at the others.

Nobody needed to say what they were thinking.

The city wasn't coming to save them.

And the first difficult decision of their new life had just arrived.
