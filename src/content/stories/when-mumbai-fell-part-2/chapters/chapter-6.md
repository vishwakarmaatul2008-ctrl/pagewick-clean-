# Chapter 6: The Voice on the Radio

The horn sounded again.

Its deep, continuous note rolled across the rooftops before fading into the distance.

Aarav stood beside the window, watching the smoke rise beyond the railway line. He couldn't tell whether the sound came from a ship, an industrial alarm, or something else entirely.

But he knew one thing.

Mumbai was no longer a city they recognized.

Behind him, Nandini struggled to sit upright on the mattress. Meera immediately moved to help her, while Sana brought another bottle of water.

"Did you hear that?" Nandini whispered.

"Everyone heard it," Kabir replied.

"What do you think it means?"

Nobody answered.

Nandini lowered her eyes.

"Yesterday, my husband told me that everything would be fine. He said the news was exaggerating and that people were panicking for no reason."

She swallowed painfully.

"He was wrong."

Aarav turned away from the window.

"We need more information. That radio transmission mentioned emergency shelters. If we can find out where they are, we might have a chance."

Dev crossed his arms.

"And if the shelters are already overrun?"

"Then we'll find somewhere else."

"You're saying that like there are thousands of safe places left."

"I'm saying we can't sit here and wait for the infected to find us."

Dev looked away, frustrated.

Rishi sat on the floor, examining the scratches on his knees.

"Can we at least agree that we need a proper plan before someone gets dragged into another grocery shop?"

Kabir gave him a tired look.

"You fell over a packet of flour."

"And survived. You're welcome."

For a brief moment, the tension eased.

Then Nandini began coughing.

It started as a quiet cough, but quickly grew harsher. She pressed a hand against her mouth, trying to control it.

Meera knelt beside her.

"Nandini?"

"I'm okay," Nandini whispered.

But she wasn't.

Her skin was flushed, her breathing was uneven, and the bandage around her ankle had become damp. Sana carefully checked the dressing without disturbing the wound.

"We need proper medical help," she said.

Nandini shook her head.

"There won't be any."

"Don't say that."

"I heard the announcement. Hospitals are overwhelmed. The streets are full of those things. Even if you find a doctor, what can they do?"

"We don't know until we try."

Nandini looked at Arjun, who was sitting beside her with his stuffed dinosaur.

"I don't want him to watch me become one of them."

The room fell silent.

Aarav felt a familiar tightness in his chest. He wanted to believe there was still a way to save her, but hope without information was becoming dangerous.

"We'll keep looking," he said quietly. "That's all we can do right now."

Nandini nodded.

Then she reached for Arjun and pulled him close.

---

By late afternoon, the group had made their decision.

They would search the nearby streets for a working radio, useful supplies, and information about the shelters. They wouldn't travel far unless they had a clear destination.

Priya would stay with Nandini and Arjun.

Before leaving, Aarav gathered everyone near the apartment entrance.

"We stay together. Nobody enters a building without checking it first. If we see infected people, we avoid them. We don't fight unless we have no other choice."

Kabir adjusted the backpack on his shoulders.

"What if we find survivors?"

"We listen first. We don't automatically trust anyone, but we don't assume everyone is dangerous either."

Sana nodded.

"Good. Because we're going to need other people eventually."

Dev checked the hammer tucked into his bag.

"And if we find a shelter?"

"We find out whether it's actually safe before we go anywhere near it."

Rishi raised his hand.

"One more rule."

Aarav sighed.

"What?"

"If anyone finds a packet of biscuits, it belongs to the group. No secret snacks."

Meera stared at him.

"You're genuinely worried about snacks right now?"

"I'm worried about survival. Snacks are part of survival."

Kabir shook his head.

"Let's just go."

They stepped into the corridor.

Before Aarav closed the apartment door, Nandini called his name.

He turned.

"Please," she whispered. "If you find a doctor, tell them about me. Tell them I haven't changed."

Aarav nodded.

"I will."

He closed the door gently.

For several seconds, he stood in the corridor, listening to the silence on the other side.

Then he followed his friends downstairs.

---

The streets were more crowded than they had been that morning.

Not with traffic.

With people.

Some wandered aimlessly, carrying bags filled with whatever they had managed to salvage. Others stood outside locked shops, arguing over supplies. A few families moved together, their faces exhausted and their clothes stained with dust.

Everyone seemed to be searching for something.

Food.

Medicine.

Shelter.

Someone they had lost.

At the end of the street, a man had set up a small table beside a shuttered pharmacy. He was selling bottles of water and packets of biscuits at prices that made Kabir stop walking.

"Two hundred rupees for a bottle?"

The man shrugged.

"Supply and demand."

"People are dying!"

"And I still need to eat."

Aarav pulled Kabir away before the argument could escalate.

"Forget it."

Kabir stared at the man in disbelief.

"How can someone do that?"

"Fear changes people," Sana replied.

"That doesn't make it right."

"No," she said. "It doesn't."

They continued down the road.

At a nearby intersection, several abandoned buses blocked the traffic. A police vehicle sat sideways against a divider, its windshield shattered.

A loudspeaker had been attached to a pole, but it produced only occasional bursts of static.

Dev pointed toward a small municipal office.

"Maybe there's a radio inside."

The building's entrance was partly open.

They approached cautiously.

A broken chair lay in the doorway, and papers were scattered across the floor. Inside, several desks had been overturned. A computer monitor lay shattered near the reception counter.

Aarav raised the iron pipe.

"Stay close."

They searched the reception area first.

Nothing useful.

Then Dev discovered a narrow room behind the counter.

Inside sat an old radio set connected to a battery pack. A handwritten sheet of frequencies was taped to the wall.

"Found something."

Rishi hurried over.

"Please work."

Dev switched it on.

Static filled the room.

He adjusted the dial slowly, moving from one frequency to another.

A man's voice emerged.

"...emergency coordination channel. Any civilian groups receiving this transmission, acknowledge if possible."

Everyone froze.

Dev leaned toward the microphone.

"Hello? Can you hear us?"

Static.

He tried again.

"This is a group of six survivors in Mumbai. We need information about emergency shelters."

For several seconds, there was no response.

Then the voice returned.

"Unidentified group, repeat your location."

Aarav stepped closer.

"We're in a residential area near a railway line. We don't know the exact status of our neighbourhood."

"Can you provide a landmark?"

Aarav glanced at Dev.

"The municipal office near the old bus depot," Dev replied quietly.

Aarav repeated the landmark into the microphone.

The radio crackled.

"Copy. Are there any injured persons in your group?"

Aarav hesitated.

Nandini's face flashed through his mind.

"One injured civilian is sheltering with us. She has a bite wound and a fever."

The silence that followed felt different from the others.

Then the voice returned.

"Is the individual conscious?"

"Yes."

"Has she shown signs of aggression or confusion?"

"Not that we've seen."

Another pause.

"Listen carefully. Do not transport the injured person through a public shelter unless directed by medical personnel. Keep her separated from uninjured civilians where possible. Do not attempt to restrain or harm her unless there is an immediate threat."

Sana lowered her eyes.

At least someone was giving them instructions that made sense.

Aarav leaned toward the radio.

"Where can we find medical help?"

The answer came slowly.

"Several emergency collection points were established earlier today. Their status is changing rapidly. We cannot guarantee that any location remains secure."

"Can you give us one that's still operating?"

Static interrupted the transmission.

The voice returned, weaker this time.

"Repeat: avoid the eastern industrial zone. Civilian access is restricted. Do not approach military checkpoints unless instructed."

Aarav remembered Priya's brother.

He looked at the radio.

"Why is the eastern industrial zone restricted? Are people trapped there?"

The transmission broke apart.

"Unidentified group, remain—"

Static swallowed the rest.

Dev adjusted the frequency.

Nothing.

He tried another.

Nothing.

Then a different voice appeared.

A woman's voice.

Quiet.

Shaking.

"If anyone can hear this, please help us."

Everyone looked at one another.

The woman continued.

"We're trapped inside a warehouse near the eastern industrial zone. There are people here. Some are injured. We can't get out."

A burst of static interrupted her.

Then she spoke again.

"They're not letting anyone leave."

Aarav grabbed the microphone.

"Who isn't letting you leave? Are you talking about the infected?"

The answer came almost immediately.

"No."

The woman began crying.

"The soldiers."

The radio crackled violently.

"We thought they were here to rescue us. They took our phones, locked the gates, and told us to wait. But people are getting sick inside. They're separating the bitten from everyone else."

Aarav felt his stomach tighten.

"How many people are with you?"

"I don't know. Hundreds, maybe. Please, you have to tell someone."

A loud bang sounded through the transmission.

The woman gasped.

"They're coming back."

Her voice dropped to a whisper.

"If anyone finds this frequency, don't trust—"

The transmission ended.

The room became completely silent.

Rishi slowly lowered himself into a chair.

"Okay."

Nobody spoke.

He looked at Aarav.

"That was definitely not a normal rescue operation."

Dev stared at the radio.

"It could be a misunderstanding. We don't know who those soldiers are or what's happening inside that warehouse."

"But we know someone is asking for help," Meera said.

Kabir shook his head.

"And we know we can't save hundreds of people. We can barely protect ourselves."

Sana looked toward Aarav.

"What do we do?"

Aarav didn't answer immediately.

He had promised Nandini that he would look for medical help. He had promised Arjun that they would try to keep his mother safe.

Now they had heard about hundreds of people trapped somewhere in the eastern industrial zone.

And someone had specifically warned them not to trust the people who were supposed to be rescuing survivors.

Aarav looked at the radio.

"We find out whether that transmission is real."

Dev stared at him.

"You're not seriously thinking about going there?"

"I'm thinking about finding out what's happening before we make a decision."

"And if the woman was telling the truth?"

"Then we need to know why people are being held there."

Kabir exhaled sharply.

"We're going to get ourselves killed."

"Maybe," Aarav replied. "But we can't keep pretending that surviving one day at a time is enough."

Sana studied him.

"What about our families?"

Aarav looked down.

"We keep trying to contact them. But if there's something bigger happening in this city, we need to understand it."

For the first time since the outbreak began, they weren't just running from the infected.

They were beginning to suspect that the living might be hiding something too.

---

They returned to the apartment just before sunset.

Priya opened the door before they had even knocked twice.

"What happened?"

Aarav explained the radio transmissions.

When he mentioned the warehouse near the eastern industrial zone, Priya went completely still.

"My brother works there."

The others exchanged glances.

"Are you sure?" Sana asked.

Priya nodded.

"He told me his company had moved some of its equipment into a warehouse near that area last month. He was supposed to finish his shift yesterday morning."

Her voice began to shake.

"I thought he might have escaped before everything started."

Aarav looked at her carefully.

"The woman said there were hundreds of people inside. We don't know if your brother is one of them."

Priya wiped her eyes.

"But he could be."

"Yes."

She looked toward the window.

"Then I need to find him."

"No," Dev said immediately. "We don't even know whether that place exists where she said it did."

"I can't just leave him there!"

"And we can't walk into a restricted industrial zone without knowing what's waiting for us!"

Priya stared at him.

For a moment, Aarav thought she might argue.

Instead, she sank onto the nearest chair.

"I know."

Her voice was barely audible.

"I just can't lose him too."

Aarav understood that feeling more than he wanted to admit.

He sat beside her.

"We'll try to find out more. But we won't rush into something we don't understand."

Priya nodded.

Across the room, Nandini began coughing again.

Meera hurried to her side.

"Nandini?"

The older woman struggled to breathe for several seconds before the coughing subsided.

Her eyes were glassy.

Arjun stood beside the mattress, clutching his stuffed dinosaur.

"Mumma, are you okay?"

Nandini forced a weak smile.

"I'm okay, baby."

But when she looked toward Aarav, the smile disappeared.

"You found a doctor?"

"Not yet," he replied. "We found a radio. There may be emergency collection points, but their status keeps changing."

Nandini looked away.

"Then I don't have much time."

"Don't say that," Meera whispered.

Nandini reached for her hand.

"Listen to me. If I get worse, I want you to take Arjun somewhere safe."

"We will."

"And if I stop recognizing you..."

Her voice broke.

"Don't let him come near me."

Arjun began crying.

"No! Mumma, stop saying that!"

Nandini pulled him close.

For several seconds, she held him tightly, whispering that she loved him.

Aarav stood in the doorway, unable to look away.

He had spent his entire life believing that the worst thing that could happen to a person was losing everything they loved.

Now he was watching someone slowly prepare to lose herself.

And there was nothing he could do to stop it.

---

That night, the group gathered in the living room.

They had food for perhaps another day if they rationed it carefully. The water supply was running low, and the building offered no reliable protection if the infected discovered their location.

They had three possible directions.

Stay where they were and hope the situation improved.

Search for a functioning emergency collection point.

Or investigate the warehouse transmission and risk entering the restricted eastern industrial zone.

None of the options felt safe.

"We need to decide by morning," Aarav said.

Kabir rubbed his eyes.

"I vote for the emergency collection point. At least there's a chance of finding medical staff."

Dev nodded.

"Agreed. We shouldn't go near that warehouse until we have better information."

Sana looked at Priya.

"What about you?"

Priya was staring at the floor.

"I want to find my brother. But I won't ask you to walk into a place that might get all of you killed."

Rishi leaned forward.

"Then we look for the collection point first. Maybe someone there knows what's happening."

Meera nodded.

"And maybe they can help Nandini."

Aarav looked around the room.

For the first time, they were beginning to make decisions together instead of reacting to whatever happened next.

It wasn't a perfect plan.

But it was a start.

He reached for the radio they had brought back from the municipal office and switched it on.

Static filled the room.

He adjusted the dial to the frequency where the woman had spoken.

Nothing.

He tried again.

Still nothing.

Then, just as he was about to switch it off, a faint voice emerged.

"...if anyone is receiving this transmission..."

Aarav leaned closer.

The voice was male this time.

"...the warehouse is not a shelter. Repeat, it is not a shelter."

Everyone stopped moving.

The transmission crackled.

"Those people are not being rescued. They're being collected."

Aarav gripped the radio.

"Who is this? What do you mean, collected?"

The voice continued, almost drowned by static.

"If you're listening, stay away from the eastern industrial zone. They know people are trying to contact the outside."

A sharp sound interrupted him.

Then came a whisper.

"They're looking for the ones who got out."

The radio went silent.

Nobody spoke.

Aarav slowly looked at his friends.

Somewhere in Mumbai, hundreds of survivors might be trapped behind locked gates.

And someone was searching for people who had escaped.

The outbreak was no longer just about the infected.

There were secrets buried beneath the chaos.

And somehow, six ordinary friends had stumbled onto one of them.
