# Chapter 3: The Price of Helping

Nobody spoke after the announcement ended.

The loudspeaker crackled once more, but instead of another warning, there was only static. Then it went silent.

Aarav stared at Nandini's bandaged ankle.

The wound had already begun to swell.

"How long ago were you bitten?" he asked.

"Yesterday afternoon," Nandini whispered.

Meera looked at her in disbelief. "Yesterday? And you were still walking around?"

"I didn't know what it was. The man attacked me near the parking area. I thought he was drunk or on drugs." Nandini swallowed hard. "I cleaned the wound when I came upstairs. Later, my husband came home, and everything started happening so quickly."

"Do you have a fever?" Sana asked.

Nandini nodded.

"Since last night."

Dev looked away.

They all remembered the mall employee. The way he had struggled to breathe before suddenly turning violent. The way his body had changed within minutes.

Rishi rubbed his face.

"We can't keep her here."

The little boy lifted his head.

"Why?"

Rishi froze.

The child's eyes moved from one face to another, searching for an answer.

Meera knelt beside him.

"What's your name?"

"Arjun."

"Okay, Arjun. We're going to look after you, all right?"

"Will Mumma get better?"

Meera opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

Nandini reached for her son's hand.

"Come here, baby."

Arjun immediately climbed into her lap, careful of her injured leg. She wrapped both arms around him and pressed her cheek against his hair.

For a moment, the courtyard felt painfully ordinary. A mother holding her child. Friends standing nearby. The morning sunlight touching the walls.

It could have been any Sunday in Mumbai.

Except that somewhere outside, people were being hunted by the dead.

Aarav stood up.

"We need to make a plan."

"What plan?" Kabir asked. "We have almost no food, barely any water, and no idea how this infection works."

"First, we check whether the building has more supplies. Then we find a safer place."

"And what about her?" Rishi asked.

Aarav looked at Nandini.

"We keep her comfortable and make sure Arjun is safe. But we can't ignore the risk."

Sana folded her arms.

"She's a person, Aarav. Not a problem we can throw outside."

"I never said that."

"You're thinking it."

"I'm thinking about all of us!"

His voice echoed across the courtyard.

Everyone fell silent.

Aarav immediately regretted shouting. He hadn't slept, his phone was useless, and the thought of his mother being somewhere in the city without him was eating him alive.

He took a breath.

"Sorry. I'm just trying to keep everyone alive."

Sana's expression softened slightly.

"I know."

Dev picked up the first-aid kit.

"Let's clean her wound properly. We should also look for fever medicine and fresh bandages. We can decide what to do next after that."

"Don't give her random medicines," Meera said. "We don't even know what this infection is."

"Then we start with cleaning and dressing the wound. No guessing."

Aarav nodded.

"Fine. Nobody leaves the courtyard alone. Kabir, come with me. We'll check the ground-floor rooms and the staircase. Dev, stay here and help Sana with the supplies. Meera, stay with Arjun."

Rishi raised his hand.

"And me?"

"Keep watch at the entrance. If you hear anything, tell us immediately. Don't open the gate for anyone without warning us first."

Rishi glanced at the gate.

"Trust me, I'm not planning to open that thing."

---

The ground floor contained four apartments, a small storage room, and a narrow passage leading toward the building's parking area.

Aarav and Kabir checked the passage first.

The air smelled of damp concrete and petrol.

A scooter lay overturned near the wall. Several cardboard boxes had been scattered across the floor, their contents spilled everywhere.

Kabir pointed toward a half-open door.

"Storage room."

Aarav raised the iron pipe.

"Stay behind me."

They pushed the door open.

Inside, they found cleaning supplies, an old toolbox, two buckets, a stack of empty plastic containers, and a carton of bottled water.

Kabir stared at the carton.

"Tell me those are real."

Aarav picked up one bottle and checked the seal.

"Looks like it."

"Finally, some good news."

They carried the carton into the courtyard, along with a torch, several dust masks, a roll of tape, and a small hammer.

Dev immediately began counting the bottles.

"Twenty-four."

"How long will that last?" Meera asked.

"Depends on how many people we have and how long we're stuck here."

"Seven people," Sana said.

Dev glanced at Nandini and Arjun.

"Right. Seven."

Rishi looked at the water.

"We should ration it now. No wasting bottles because someone feels thirsty every ten minutes."

"Nobody is going to waste water," Meera replied.

"I'm just saying."

"Then stop saying it."

Rishi raised both hands.

"Okay. Everyone's angry today. Noted."

A faint smile appeared on Arjun's face.

It vanished almost immediately, but Meera noticed.

For the first time that morning, she felt as though they had managed to protect something.

Not the city.

Not even themselves.

Just one small moment of normality.

---

By late morning, the courtyard had become their temporary shelter.

They divided the remaining biscuits into small portions. Dev used the hammer and a wooden plank to reinforce the gate. Kabir checked the staircase while Sana helped Nandini change the dressing around her wound.

Aarav tried calling his mother again.

No service.

He sent another message.

*Mom, please reply. I'm safe for now. Where are you?*

He watched the message remain unsent.

Then he tried his sisters.

Nothing.

His throat tightened.

He could still remember leaving home before everything collapsed. His mother had asked him to pick up something on his way back. He had complained that she always gave him errands at the worst possible time.

He hadn't known that the ordinary little argument might become one of his last memories of her.

"Any luck?"

Aarav turned.

Meera was standing beside him.

He shook his head.

She sat down on the step.

"I haven't heard from my family either."

Aarav stared at the phone in his hand.

"What if they're—"

"Don't."

Meera spoke quietly.

"Don't finish that sentence. We don't know anything yet."

"But we don't know if they're alive."

"Exactly. We don't know."

She looked at him.

"So until we know, we keep trying."

Aarav nodded, though the fear didn't leave him.

Across the courtyard, Arjun had fallen asleep against Nandini's shoulder. She stroked his hair while watching the others move around the building.

Every few minutes, she glanced at her ankle.

Aarav noticed.

He also noticed that she was sweating more than before.

---

At approximately noon, the first argument began.

It started when Dev suggested they should leave the building before nightfall.

"The entrance won't hold forever," he said. "And we can't live on biscuits and bottled water."

"Where do you want to go?" Kabir asked. "The street is full of infected people."

"Somewhere with more supplies. A hospital, maybe."

Sana stared at him.

"Have you listened to anything happening outside? Hospitals are overwhelmed."

"Then a pharmacy."

"And if it's already been looted?"

"We can't just sit here!"

"At least here we have a gate and walls."

Rishi leaned against the wall.

"I vote for the place where nobody is trying to eat us."

"That's not a plan," Dev replied.

"It's a very good starting point."

Aarav listened to them argue.

They were all right in different ways.

Staying meant risking starvation, dehydration, and the possibility that the infected would enter the building.

Leaving meant walking into a city they barely recognized, with no reliable information and no guarantee of finding somewhere safer.

He looked at the street through the gate.

A single car moved slowly past the entrance.

Its windows were covered with sheets.

The driver kept one hand on the horn.

The sound echoed down the street.

From several directions came answering screams.

Then people started running.

A woman appeared at the far end of the road, carrying a backpack. Behind her, three infected figures stumbled between the abandoned vehicles.

She ran toward the building.

"HELP!"

Rishi immediately backed away from the gate.

"Oh, no."

The woman reached the entrance and grabbed the bars.

"PLEASE! LET ME IN!"

Aarav rushed forward.

"How many are following you?"

"Please! They're right behind me!"

He looked beyond her.

Two infected figures had turned into the street. The third was dragging one leg but moving faster than it should have been able to.

"Open it!" Meera shouted.

"Wait!" Kabir yelled. "We need to know if she's bitten!"

The woman shook the gate desperately.

"I'm not bitten! Please!"

Aarav looked at the others.

There wasn't time for a long argument.

"Get ready," he said. "We're opening it just enough to pull her through. Nobody lets go of the barricade."

Dev and Kabir grabbed the wooden table.

Rishi took hold of the gate.

Aarav reached for the latch.

"On three. One. Two. Three!"

They pulled the gate open just wide enough for the woman to squeeze through.

She stumbled into the courtyard.

Aarav grabbed her backpack and dragged her away from the entrance while the others slammed the gate shut.

An infected man crashed against the bars an instant later.

The woman collapsed on the ground, gasping for air.

"Are you hurt?" Sana asked.

"No. No, I'm okay."

"Have you been bitten?"

"No!"

"Show us your arms."

The woman hesitated.

"Please, I just need a minute."

"Show us," Aarav repeated.

She slowly rolled up her sleeves.

Scratches covered her forearms, but there were no obvious bite marks.

Sana checked her neck while Meera examined the back of her hands.

Nothing.

"What's your name?" Aarav asked.

"Priya."

"Where did you come from?"

"Near the main road. I was trying to get to my brother's apartment, but there were too many of them."

She looked at the gate.

"What is this place?"

"A residential building," Kabir replied.

Priya stared at them.

"I know that. I mean, are you all staying here?"

"For now," Aarav said.

She slowly nodded.

Then her eyes landed on Nandini.

Priya's expression changed.

"She's been bitten."

The courtyard went silent.

Nandini clutched Arjun closer.

Priya took a step backward.

"You can't keep her here!"

Sana stood up.

"She's injured. We're looking after her."

"She's infected!"

"We don't know that for sure," Meera replied.

Priya looked at the bandage around Nandini's ankle.

"I saw people like her yesterday. They looked fine at first. Then they started shaking, screaming, attacking anyone who came close."

Nandini's face had gone pale.

"Please," she whispered. "I don't want to hurt anyone."

Priya shook her head.

"You don't understand. My brother was bitten. I stayed with him all night because he said he was fine. He even joked with me. He asked for water."

Her voice broke.

"Then, just before sunrise, he tried to bite me."

Nobody spoke.

Priya wiped her eyes with the back of her hand.

"I had to lock him in his own bedroom."

Aarav looked at Nandini.

She was trembling now.

Arjun held her hand.

"Mumma, are you sick?"

Nandini forced herself to smile.

"I'm just tired, baby."

But even the child seemed unconvinced.

---

That afternoon, the heat became unbearable.

The courtyard offered little shade, and the air felt heavy. The infected outside had mostly stopped making noise, which somehow made the silence worse.

Nandini's condition continued to deteriorate.

She complained of chills despite the heat. Her breathing became uneven, and she struggled to keep her eyes open.

Dev checked the time.

"We can't diagnose her. We don't know how this infection works, and we don't have proper medical equipment."

"Can we at least take her to a hospital?" Meera asked.

Priya shook her head.

"Not now. I passed an ambulance on the main road. Nobody was driving it. The doors were open, and there was blood everywhere."

Sana looked at Aarav.

"We need to isolate her somewhere safe. For her and for everyone else."

Nandini heard her.

Tears rolled down her cheeks.

"You want to lock me away?"

"No," Sana replied gently. "We want to keep you safe while we figure out what's happening."

Nandini looked at her son.

"And what happens to him?"

Nobody answered.

Arjun began crying.

"Please don't leave Mumma!"

Meera hugged him.

"We won't leave you alone."

Aarav looked around the courtyard. They needed a separate room with a door, clean water, and enough space for Nandini and Arjun. The ground-floor storage room was too small, but one of the empty apartments on the first floor might work.

He hated every second of the decision.

Yet doing nothing was becoming more dangerous.

"Nandini," he said quietly, "we're going to move you to an empty apartment upstairs. Arjun can stay with you for now, but we'll keep the door open enough for us to check on you without getting too close. We'll bring you water and food."

She stared at him.

"And if I change?"

Aarav struggled to find an answer.

"If anything happens, we'll deal with it then."

It wasn't a promise that everything would be fine.

It was the only honest thing he could offer.

---

By evening, Priya had helped them prepare the empty apartment.

They found a mattress, a bedsheet, a few sealed water bottles, and a small fan that no longer worked. Sana cleaned the room as best she could while Dev placed the first-aid supplies nearby.

Nandini was moved upstairs with Arjun beside her.

Before they closed the door, she caught Aarav's wrist.

"Please," she whispered. "If I stop recognizing him, don't let me hurt my son."

Aarav looked into her frightened eyes.

"We'll protect him."

She nodded slowly.

Then she pulled Arjun close.

The door was left slightly open.

Aarav stood in the corridor for several seconds before turning away.

Downstairs, the others were preparing for another night.

Priya had told them about a small grocery shop two streets away that might still have supplies. Dev wanted to search it at first light. Kabir thought they should find a vehicle instead. Rishi insisted that nobody should leave until they had a better idea of what was happening outside.

Sana listened to all of them, then looked at Aarav.

"We can't survive by arguing every time something goes wrong."

"I know."

"We need rules. We need a plan. And we need to trust each other."

Aarav looked around at his friends.

They were exhausted, frightened, and hungry. They had no weapons worth mentioning, no reliable communication, and no idea how long the city would remain cut off.

But they were still together.

For now, that had to count for something.

A distant siren wailed beyond the buildings.

Then another.

Aarav walked to the gate and peered through the bars.

The street was almost empty.

At the far end, a figure stood beneath a flickering streetlight.

It wasn't moving.

Aarav narrowed his eyes.

The figure wore a dark shirt and trousers. Its head was tilted slightly to one side, and its arms hung loosely at its sides.

For several seconds, Aarav watched it.

Then the figure slowly raised its head.

Even from a distance, Aarav could see its face turning toward the building.

Toward him.

He stepped back from the gate.

"Everyone," he whispered.

His friends turned.

"What is it?" Kabir asked.

Aarav pointed toward the street.

The figure had started walking toward them.

Behind it, more shapes were emerging from the darkness.

One.

Three.

Five.

Then too many to count.

The city had been quiet all afternoon.

Now the infected were gathering again.

And night was falling.
