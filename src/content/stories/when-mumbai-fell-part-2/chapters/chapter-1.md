# Chapter 1: The First Morning

The morning arrived without hope.

Aarav sat on the cold courtyard floor, his back pressed against a wall, staring at the narrow entrance they had blocked with two plastic chairs and a broken wooden table. Beyond it, Mumbai was waking up to a world that no longer made sense.

There were no school buses rumbling through the streets. No crowded local trains announcing another exhausting day. No familiar noise of people arguing with auto-rickshaw drivers or rushing to catch the last few minutes before work.

Instead, there were distant screams.

And sirens.

And somewhere far away, the sound of something exploding.

Aarav hadn't slept.

Neither had Meera, who sat beside him with her knees pulled against her chest. Her phone rested in her hands, its screen lighting up every few seconds as she tried calling her mother again.

No answer.

Across the courtyard, Sana stood near the staircase, looking through a gap in the building's metal gate. Kabir and Dev were whispering beside the entrance, while Rishi lay on a thin mattress they'd found in an abandoned ground-floor room.

Nobody had spoken much since sunrise.

They were all waiting for someone to tell them this was a nightmare.

Nobody did.

"Any network?" Aarav asked quietly.

Meera shook her head.

"I got one bar for about ten seconds. Then it disappeared."

"Try messaging instead of calling," Dev suggested. "Messages might go through when the network comes back."

"I've sent her twenty messages."

Meera's voice cracked on the last word.

Aarav looked away.

He wanted to tell her everything would be fine. He wanted to promise that her mother was safe, that they would find their families, and that someone in the government would eventually explain what was happening.

But he couldn't make himself say any of it.

He had no idea whether his own mother was alive.

That thought had been sitting inside his chest all night, growing heavier every time he looked at his phone.

A sudden thud echoed from somewhere outside the courtyard.

Everyone froze.

Another thud followed.

Then another.

Rishi sat upright on the mattress.

"Please tell me that was a door."

Nobody answered.

Kabir slowly approached the entrance and peered through a gap between the wooden table and the wall.

The street beyond the gate was narrow and cluttered with abandoned vehicles. A scooter lay on its side near a parked car. Broken glass glittered across the road beneath the pale morning light.

At the far end of the street, a man was standing beside a white hatchback.

His shirt was stained dark with blood.

His head hung at an unnatural angle.

Kabir narrowed his eyes.

"There's someone outside."

"Someone alive?" Sana asked.

"I don't know."

The man suddenly slammed both hands against the car window.

Once.

Twice.

On the third strike, the glass shattered.

Kabir stumbled backward.

The man climbed halfway through the broken window, disappeared inside the car, and began thrashing around.

A few seconds later, he emerged again.

Something moved in his mouth.

He was chewing.

Rishi covered his lips with one hand.

"Oh, God."

The man turned toward their building.

For one terrifying moment, Kabir thought he had seen them.

Then the man started walking toward the gate.

Slowly at first.

Then faster.

His feet dragged against the road. His mouth hung open, and a dark line of blood ran from his chin to his neck.

"Move away from the entrance," Aarav whispered.

They backed away together.

The man reached the metal gate and struck it with both hands.

The entire frame shook.

Everyone flinched.

He struck it again.

And again.

His face pressed between the bars, his cloudy eyes moving without focus as he released a wet, broken growl.

Meera covered her mouth.

"He's going to break it."

"Then we need to reinforce it," Dev said.

"With what?" Rishi snapped. "Another plastic chair?"

"Stop it," Sana said sharply. "Both of you."

The infected man slammed his forehead against the gate.

A thin crack appeared in the glass panel beside the entrance.

Aarav looked around the courtyard, searching for anything useful. Near the staircase, he noticed a rusted iron pipe leaning against a wall. Beside it sat a pile of discarded wooden planks.

"Dev, Kabir. Help me with those."

They rushed to the pile.

Together, the three boys dragged the heaviest planks toward the gate and wedged them between the metal bars and the wall. Dev found a length of electrical wire and used it to secure the broken wooden table against the lower section.

The infected man kept pushing.

The gate rattled violently.

"Faster!" Aarav shouted.

Kabir twisted the wire until his fingers hurt. Dev shoved a plank into place, then kicked a heavy stone beneath the table to stop it from sliding.

Finally, the gate stopped moving as much.

The infected man remained outside, clawing at the bars.

They retreated to the opposite side of the courtyard.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Rishi let out a nervous laugh.

"Okay. New rule. Nobody goes anywhere near that gate."

"That was already the rule," Meera muttered.

"I'm just making it official."

Nobody laughed with him.

Aarav looked at the others.

"We can't stay here forever."

"We can't go outside either," Sana replied.

"We need water, food, and a way to contact our families. We have some biscuits and bottles, but that's not going to last."

"Then we search the building," Dev said. "There might be supplies in the other flats."

Kabir glanced toward the staircase.

"What if there are more of them upstairs?"

"Then we don't open any doors unless we have to," Aarav answered. "We check the common areas first. Quietly."

Meera finally lowered her phone.

"What about the people who live here?"

The question silenced them.

They had entered the courtyard during the night because it seemed safer than the street. They hadn't checked every apartment. They didn't know whether anyone else was hiding inside.

Or whether someone had already turned.

"We should find out if anyone needs help," Sana said.

"And risk opening a door to one of those things?" Rishi asked.

"We can't just ignore people."

"We also can't save everyone if we get ourselves killed."

Sana stared at him.

For once, Rishi had no joke to hide behind.

Aarav rubbed his face with both hands.

"We'll check the staircase and the ground floor. If we hear anything suspicious, we leave. Nobody separates from the group."

They gathered what little they had.

Two half-full water bottles.

A packet of biscuits.

A small first-aid kit.

One torch with a flickering battery.

And a kitchen knife Dev had found in the abandoned room.

It didn't feel like enough to survive a normal day, let alone the end of the world.

They started toward the staircase.

Halfway there, a phone rang.

Everyone stopped.

The sound came from somewhere above them.

One short ring.

Then silence.

Another ring followed.

Meera looked at Aarav.

"Someone's phone."

They waited.

The phone rang a third time.

It was coming from the first-floor landing.

Aarav raised the torch, even though daylight was beginning to spill through the courtyard's open roof.

"Stay behind me."

They climbed slowly, avoiding the loose steps that creaked under their weight. At the landing, they found a brown handbag lying beside a closed apartment door.

A phone vibrated inside it.

The screen showed an incoming call.

MOM.

Meera reached for it, but Aarav caught her wrist.

"Wait."

"What?"

He pointed toward the door.

A thin line of blood had seeped from underneath it.

The phone kept ringing.

From somewhere inside the apartment came a faint scraping sound.

Then a woman's voice.

"Please..."

Everyone went still.

The voice came again, weak and trembling.

"Please, somebody help me."

Meera's eyes filled with tears.

Aarav stared at the door, his hand tightening around the iron pipe.

They had spent the entire night trying to reach their families.

Now, for the first time since the outbreak began, someone was asking them for help.

And they had no idea what was waiting on the other side.
