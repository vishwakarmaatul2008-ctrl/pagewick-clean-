# Chapter 4: When the Gates Break

"Everyone, get away from the gate!"

Aarav's voice cut through the courtyard.

The others rushed toward him. Beyond the metal bars, the street had transformed into a nightmare. Infected figures wandered between abandoned cars, bumping into one another before turning toward the residential building.

There were at least a dozen.

Maybe more.

And they were getting closer.

Rishi stared through the bars.

"Bro, why are there so many?"

"Because this is a city of twenty million people," Kabir replied nervously. "If this thing spreads everywhere, there could be thousands of them."

"Could you not say that right now?"

"I'm just saying!"

"Both of you, shut up!" Sana snapped.

Aarav scanned the entrance. The wooden table and planks were holding the gate in place, but he could already see the metal bars bending slightly as the first infected reached them.

One of them slammed its shoulder against the gate.

Another followed.

Then a third.

The entire entrance rattled.

"How long will that hold?" Meera asked.

Aarav had no answer.

"Dev, help me reinforce it!"

Dev grabbed the remaining wooden planks. Together, they wedged them between the gate and the wall while Kabir pushed the table forward with his shoulder.

Rishi rushed to help, muttering something under his breath.

The infected kept pushing.

Metal scraped against concrete.

A bolt popped loose and struck the ground.

Everyone froze.

"That's not good," Dev whispered.

"No kidding," Rishi replied.

Another violent impact shook the entrance.

The gate bent inward.

Aarav looked around the courtyard.

"We need to get upstairs. Now."

"What about the gate?" Kabir asked.

"If it breaks, we can't stop them here."

They abandoned the barricade and hurried toward the staircase. Sana helped Meera carry their remaining supplies, while Dev grabbed the first-aid kit and the iron pipe.

Before they reached the first landing, a horrible metallic crash echoed through the building.

The gate had broken.

Then came the sound of dragging feet.

Dozens of them.

Moving into the courtyard.

"KEEP GOING!" Aarav shouted.

They climbed to the first floor and rushed into the empty apartment beside Nandini's room. Dev slammed the door behind them while Kabir and Rishi shoved a heavy cupboard against it.

Something struck the apartment entrance.

The door shook.

Then came another impact.

Meera covered her ears.

"They're inside!"

"Keep your voices down," Sana whispered.

The pounding continued for several seconds before fading away.

They waited.

A distant crash came from the ground floor, followed by a series of guttural growls.

The infected were spreading through the building.

Aarav slowly lowered the iron pipe.

"We can't stay here."

Kabir stared at him.

"Where exactly are we supposed to go? The stairs lead straight to them."

Aarav looked toward the apartment's balcony.

It overlooked the narrow street behind the building. The drop to the ground was too dangerous, but a neighbouring building stood close enough that their balconies nearly touched.

A narrow concrete ledge connected part of the two structures.

Dev followed his gaze.

"No."

"We haven't even said anything yet," Aarav replied.

"I know that look. You're thinking about crossing that ledge."

"It's either that or wait here until they reach us."

"That ledge is barely wide enough for one person!"

"And the staircase is full of infected people."

Dev rubbed his forehead.

"I hate this city now."

Despite everything, Kabir almost laughed.

Almost.

Then a scream came from the corridor.

Everyone turned toward the door.

It was followed by the sound of something crashing against the wall.

Nandini's apartment.

Meera went pale.

"Arjun."

She immediately rushed toward the entrance, but Aarav grabbed her arm.

"Wait!"

"He's up there!"

"I know!"

"Then let me go!"

Another scream rang through the corridor.

This time, it was a child's voice.

"MUMMA!"

Meera tore herself free and ran toward Nandini's room.

"MEERA!" Aarav shouted.

The others followed.

They reached the apartment door and found it wide open.

Inside, Nandini was lying beside the mattress, struggling to crawl toward her son.

Arjun stood near the far wall, crying.

A figure crouched between them.

Nandini's husband.

His clothes were torn, and blood covered his mouth. His head jerked toward the sound of their footsteps.

For one brief moment, Nandini looked up at them.

"Help him!"

Her husband lunged.

Meera grabbed Arjun and pulled him backward just as the infected man slammed into the wall where the child had been standing.

The impact shook a framed photograph loose.

It shattered on the floor.

Aarav charged forward and struck the infected man's shoulder with the iron pipe.

The man barely reacted.

He spun around with a furious growl and grabbed Aarav's shirt.

Aarav stumbled.

Kabir seized the infected man's arm and pulled with all his strength. Dev struck him with a wooden plank, forcing him to release Aarav.

"GET OUT!" Dev yelled.

The infected man lunged again.

Sana dragged Nandini away from the mattress while Meera carried Arjun into the corridor.

Aarav backed toward the door, keeping the pipe between himself and the infected man.

Then he noticed something.

The man wasn't looking at him anymore.

He was staring at Nandini.

For a second, his expression changed.

A sound escaped his throat.

Something almost like a word.

"Naa..."

Nandini stopped struggling.

"Vikram?"

The infected man staggered toward her.

"Vikram, it's me!"

"NANDINI, DON'T!" Aarav shouted.

She reached out instinctively.

Her husband suddenly lunged.

Aarav struck him with the pipe, knocking him sideways into a table. Kabir seized Nandini's arm and pulled her through the doorway.

They slammed the door shut.

Dev and Rishi pushed a cupboard against it.

Something hit the other side almost immediately.

Nandini collapsed onto the floor.

For several seconds, she couldn't speak.

Then she began sobbing.

"He knew me," she whispered. "For one second, he knew me."

Nobody had an answer.

Arjun clung to Meera, crying so hard that he could barely breathe.

Aarav stared at the door.

He had seen infected people attack strangers. He had watched them tear through crowds without hesitation.

But that moment had been different.

If a small part of Vikram was still trapped inside that thing, what did that mean for everyone else?

And what would happen to Nandini?

A sudden thud interrupted his thoughts.

The cupboard moved.

Then another impact shook the door.

They couldn't stay.

Aarav looked at his friends.

"We're leaving through the balcony."

---

Crossing the ledge was worse than Aarav had imagined.

The concrete was narrow, cracked, and covered with dust. Three floors below, the alley was littered with rubbish and abandoned scooters.

The neighbouring building's balcony was only a few steps away, but the gap between the two structures looked enormous.

Dev stared down.

"Absolutely not."

Kabir looked at him.

"You have another idea?"

"Yes. A better one."

"Which is?"

Dev said nothing.

"Exactly," Kabir muttered.

Aarav went first.

He climbed over the balcony railing and lowered himself onto the ledge. The concrete scraped his palms as he steadied himself against the wall.

"Don't look down," Sana called.

"Wasn't planning to."

He moved sideways, one careful step at a time.

Halfway across, a sharp crack sounded beneath his foot.

A small piece of concrete broke away and fell into the alley.

Aarav froze.

Behind him, Rishi whispered, "Bro..."

"Don't move."

Aarav pressed himself against the wall and continued.

A few seconds later, he reached the neighbouring balcony.

He climbed over the railing and turned around.

"Come on. One at a time."

Dev crossed next, followed by Kabir and Rishi. Sana helped Meera bring Arjun across, keeping the boy close to the wall.

Nandini was last.

She was weak and struggling to balance, but she refused to let anyone carry her.

"I can do it," she whispered.

Sana held her hand while Aarav waited at the other end.

One step.

Then another.

Nandini's foot slipped.

She gasped and grabbed the wall.

Sana tightened her grip.

"I've got you!"

For one terrifying second, Nandini hung partly over the alley.

Then Aarav caught her wrist and pulled her toward the balcony.

Together, they dragged her over the railing.

She collapsed onto the floor, shaking.

Nobody spoke.

They had made it.

But they weren't safe yet.

---

The neighbouring building appeared abandoned.

Its ground-floor entrance was locked, and the lobby smelled of damp walls and stale food. Several apartments stood open, their interiors stripped of anything useful.

The group moved quietly through the corridors.

They found a bottle of antiseptic, some bandages, two packets of biscuits, and a small backpack.

There was no electricity.

No working lift.

No sign of other residents.

Aarav checked his phone.

No signal.

Meera tried hers.

Nothing.

Rishi sat on the staircase and buried his face in his hands.

"How did we go from worrying about college assignments to this?"

Kabir sat beside him.

"Remember when you said a zombie apocalypse would be fun?"

"Don't."

"You literally said you'd survive because you played video games."

"Bro, I said a lot of stupid things."

For a moment, the familiar exchange made them feel like themselves again.

Then a low growl rose from the floor below.

Both boys immediately stood.

Aarav raised his hand, signalling for silence.

The sound came again.

Something was moving inside the lobby.

Dev peered through the stairwell railing.

His face changed.

"There's one downstairs."

"Can we get out through the back?" Sana whispered.

"I don't know."

Aarav looked toward the upper floors.

"We go up. Find a room we can secure. We need to rest before we decide our next move."

They climbed to the third floor and entered an apartment with a solid wooden door. Kabir and Dev checked the rooms while Rishi pushed a sofa against the entrance.

For the first time in hours, they had a door between themselves and the infected.

It wasn't much.

But it was something.

---

Night fell.

They sat together in the living room, sharing the last biscuits and dividing the water into small portions.

Nandini rested against the wall while Arjun slept beside her. She had barely spoken since they escaped the first building.

Meera sat close to the boy, gently brushing his hair away from his forehead.

Sana watched Nandini carefully.

"How are you feeling?"

"Tired."

"Anything else?"

Nandini hesitated.

"My head hurts. And I'm cold."

Sana exchanged a glance with Aarav.

Nandini noticed.

"Don't look at me like that."

"We're just checking on you," Sana replied.

"No. You're waiting for me to change."

The room went silent.

Nandini wiped her eyes.

"I can see it in your faces. You think I'm going to become one of them."

Nobody denied it.

She looked down at her bandaged ankle.

"I don't want to die."

Aarav leaned forward.

"We're going to keep looking for help."

"And if there is no help?"

He had no answer.

Nandini looked at Arjun.

"Promise me something."

Meera moved closer.

"What?"

"If I become dangerous, don't let my son come near me."

Arjun stirred in his sleep.

Nandini pressed a hand over her mouth to keep from crying.

"He's all I have."

Aarav looked away.

He couldn't promise that everything would be fine. He couldn't promise that they would find a cure or that the infection would spare her.

But he could promise they wouldn't abandon the child.

"We'll protect him," he said.

Nandini nodded.

Outside, distant screams echoed between the buildings.

Aarav walked to the window and carefully moved the curtain aside.

Mumbai stretched into the darkness.

Fires burned in the distance. Smoke rose above the rooftops. A few vehicles moved through the streets before disappearing around corners.

Far away, the familiar outline of a railway station stood beneath flickering lights.

Then Aarav noticed something strange.

A line of headlights was moving along an elevated road.

Not randomly.

In formation.

Several military vehicles were travelling together, heading toward the eastern side of the city.

He watched them disappear behind the buildings.

"Guys," he whispered. "Come here."

The others gathered around the window.

"Is that the army?" Kabir asked.

"Looks like it," Dev replied.

Rishi leaned closer.

"Maybe they're coming to rescue people."

Aarav kept watching the distant road.

The vehicles hadn't stopped.

They hadn't turned toward the residential neighbourhoods.

They were heading away from the centre of the city.

Then the sound of gunfire drifted through the night.

Once.

Twice.

Then a rapid burst that echoed across Mumbai.

Arjun woke up crying.

Meera pulled him close.

Nandini stared at the window.

"What was that?"

Nobody answered.

Aarav slowly closed the curtain.

They had escaped the building.

They had survived another day.

But the city was changing faster than they could understand.

And somewhere beyond the smoke, the people who might have been able to help them were moving in the opposite direction.
