# Chapter 5: The Last Safe Place

The gunfire continued for several seconds before fading into the distance.

Nobody in the apartment moved.

Aarav stood beside the window, staring at the closed curtains. He could still hear the echoes in his head, each burst reminding him that the city was no longer governed by the rules he had grown up with.

Outside, Mumbai was burning.

Inside, seven people were trying to figure out how to survive another day.

"Maybe the army is clearing the infected," Kabir whispered.

"Then why are they moving away from us?" Dev asked.

"Maybe they're heading somewhere else first."

"Or maybe they're evacuating."

Rishi looked up. "Evacuating where? And what about everyone left behind?"

Nobody answered.

Meera sat on the floor beside Arjun. The boy had fallen asleep again, exhausted from crying. His small hand held the sleeve of her shirt, as if he was afraid that even in his sleep, she might disappear.

Nandini watched him with tired eyes.

"He used to be afraid of thunder," she whispered. "Whenever it rained, he'd run into our bedroom and hide between me and his father."

She paused.

"Last night, he watched his father try to kill me."

Meera lowered her eyes.

There was nothing she could say that would make that memory disappear.

Aarav finally turned away from the window.

"We need to move tomorrow."

Sana looked at him. "Where?"

"I don't know yet. But this building won't protect us forever."

Dev nodded.

"We need information first. We can't just wander through the streets hoping to find somewhere safe."

"Then we find a working radio," Kabir suggested. "Or a police station. Somewhere we can learn what's happening."

Rishi gave him a tired look.

"And how exactly are we going to reach a police station without getting eaten?"

"One problem at a time."

"That's easy for you to say."

"Guys," Sana interrupted. "We can argue tomorrow. Right now, we need sleep."

Nobody disagreed.

They took turns resting near the entrance. Aarav volunteered for the first watch, with Kabir taking over later in the night.

But sleep never came easily.

Every creak of the building sounded like approaching footsteps. Every distant scream made them wonder whether the infected had found another victim.

At some point, Aarav drifted off against the wall.

He dreamed about his mother calling him from somewhere across a crowded railway platform.

He could see her.

He could hear her.

But no matter how hard he ran, the distance between them never changed.

Then the crowd began screaming.

And when Aarav looked down, the people around him were no longer moving like people.

He woke with a sharp gasp.

The apartment was dark.

For one confused moment, he didn't remember where he was.

Then he heard a faint sound from the kitchen.

A metallic clink.

He grabbed the iron pipe and stood.

"Who's there?"

No answer.

He moved slowly toward the kitchen doorway.

A shadow shifted behind the counter.

Aarav raised the pipe.

"Come out!"

A figure emerged with both hands raised.

It was Rishi.

"Bro! It's me!"

Aarav lowered the pipe, his heart pounding.

"What are you doing?"

"Looking for water."

"You nearly got your head smashed."

"Well, excuse me for being thirsty."

Aarav rubbed his eyes.

"Go back to sleep."

Rishi opened a cabinet and found an empty glass.

"Couldn't sleep anyway."

For a moment, they stood in silence.

Then Rishi spoke more quietly.

"Do you think we'll find our families?"

Aarav stared at the floor.

"I don't know."

"That's not what I wanted to hear."

"It's the truth."

Rishi nodded slowly.

He wasn't smiling anymore.

"I keep thinking about yesterday. Before everything happened. We were complaining about our phones, college, money, all that stupid stuff."

He swallowed.

"Now I'd give anything to have one normal day again."

Aarav looked at him.

"Me too."

Neither of them said anything else.

They returned to their places and waited for morning.

---

At seven o'clock, the city looked worse.

From the window, Aarav could see abandoned cars blocking the road. Smoke rose from several buildings, and a distant column of black smoke stretched into the sky.

A crowd had gathered near a traffic signal.

At first, he thought they were survivors.

Then the crowd began moving in different directions, and he realized some of them were infected.

A man sprinted across the road while several figures chased him. He stumbled beside an overturned scooter, scrambled to his feet, and disappeared into a narrow alley.

The infected followed.

Aarav closed the curtain.

He had seen enough.

"Everyone, wake up."

The others slowly gathered in the living room.

Dev checked their supplies.

"Three water bottles left from the ones we opened. Five unopened. Two packets of biscuits. A little antiseptic, some bandages, and whatever we can find in the building."

"We need food," Kabir said.

"And a better place to stay," Sana added.

Rishi stretched his arms.

"And a shower. I would commit crimes for a shower right now."

Nobody laughed.

"Okay," he muttered. "Too soon."

Aarav took a breath.

"We're going to search the nearby grocery shop. Priya mentioned one two streets away."

Priya, who had spent most of the night sitting near the door, looked up.

"It's called Shree Ganesh Stores. It's on the corner near the medical clinic."

"Do you know another route?"

"There's a narrow lane behind the building. It connects to the main road near the shop."

"How many infected did you see?"

"Yesterday? A lot. Today, I don't know."

Dev looked concerned.

"We should go in a small group. If something happens, the rest of us can stay here."

"No," Aarav said. "If we split up, we might not find each other again."

"Then what do you suggest?"

"All six of us go. Nandini and Arjun stay here with Priya."

Nandini looked up from the mattress.

"You're leaving us?"

"We'll be back as soon as we can."

She glanced at her bandaged ankle.

"I understand."

Arjun was awake now, sitting beside her.

"Are you going to bring food?"

Aarav crouched in front of him.

"That's the plan."

"Can you bring chocolate?"

For the first time that morning, Aarav smiled.

"If we find some."

Arjun nodded solemnly, as if this were a serious agreement.

"Okay."

Meera adjusted the strap of her backpack.

"Let's go before it gets hotter."

They checked their supplies one last time.

Aarav carried the iron pipe. Dev took the hammer, Kabir carried the torch, and Rishi had a small kitchen knife tucked into his waistband. Sana and Meera carried the backpacks.

It wasn't much.

But it was all they had.

Before leaving, Aarav looked at Nandini.

"Keep the door locked. Don't open it unless you hear our voices and can confirm it's us."

Nandini nodded.

"And if you hear anything strange, hide with Arjun in the bedroom."

"What about you?"

"We'll be careful."

He didn't promise to return safely.

He had learned that promises were easy to make and impossible to guarantee.

Then the six friends stepped into the corridor.

The apartment door closed behind them.

---

The back lane was almost deserted.

Garbage bags lay scattered beside the walls, and a broken streetlight hung from its pole. A scooter had crashed into a parked car, leaving a long scratch along the vehicle's side.

The air smelled of smoke and rotting rubbish.

Meera pulled her shirt over her nose.

"How does the whole city smell like this already?"

"Because things are dying everywhere," Dev replied.

"Thanks. That makes me feel so much better."

They continued down the lane.

Aarav kept his eyes on the corners and doorways. Every few steps, he stopped to listen.

Nothing.

No footsteps.

No voices.

Only the distant rumble of traffic somewhere beyond the buildings.

When they reached the end of the lane, Kabir peered around the corner.

"The shop is across the road."

Aarav joined him.

Shree Ganesh Stores stood beneath a faded signboard. Its shutters were halfway down, and several packets of snacks lay scattered near the entrance.

A bicycle was abandoned beside the doorway.

The shop looked empty.

"Could be a trap," Rishi whispered.

"It's a grocery shop," Kabir replied. "Not a haunted house."

"Yesterday, I watched a dead man smash his face against a gate. At this point, anything can be a trap."

Sana looked through the gap beneath the shutter.

"I can see shelves inside."

"Any movement?" Aarav asked.

She shook her head.

They approached cautiously.

Dev crouched and lifted the shutter just enough for someone to crawl underneath.

"I'll go first," he said.

"No," Aarav replied. "We enter together and stay close."

They raised the shutter a little higher and slipped inside.

The shop was a mess.

Several shelves had been knocked over. Packets of biscuits, chips, and instant noodles covered the floor. A refrigerator stood against the back wall, its lights switched off.

Behind the counter, an old radio sat beside a calculator.

Meera immediately started collecting sealed food packets.

"Anything that hasn't been opened goes into the bags."

Kabir searched the shelves for bottled water.

Dev found a carton of biscuits beneath the counter.

Rishi, meanwhile, was staring at the radio.

"Do any of you know how to use one of these?"

"Turn it on," Sana said.

"Thank you, professor."

He twisted the knob.

A burst of static filled the shop.

Everyone jumped.

Rishi quickly lowered the volume.

He adjusted the dial slowly.

Static.

More static.

Then a voice emerged.

"...all residents are advised to remain indoors. Do not approach infected individuals. Avoid hospitals unless directed by emergency authorities..."

They crowded around the radio.

The voice continued.

"...several major transport routes remain blocked. Local railway services are suspended until further notice. Emergency shelters are being established in selected locations, but access may be restricted..."

Kabir leaned closer.

"Did they say shelters?"

Rishi adjusted the dial.

The signal crackled.

"...repeat, shelters are being established..."

Then the transmission broke apart.

"Come on!" Dev whispered.

Rishi twisted the knob again.

Nothing.

He struck the side of the radio lightly.

The voice returned for two seconds.

"...avoid the eastern industrial zone..."

Then silence.

Aarav stared at the radio.

"Why did they mention that area?"

"Maybe there's an outbreak there," Sana said.

"Or maybe they're evacuating people from it," Kabir suggested.

"Either way, we shouldn't go there," Meera replied.

A sudden crash came from the back of the shop.

Everyone froze.

Something had fallen behind the storage shelves.

Dev slowly raised the hammer.

Aarav signalled for silence.

Another sound followed.

A dragging noise.

Coming from the storeroom.

Rishi whispered, "Please tell me that's a rat."

A shadow appeared beneath the storeroom door.

Then a hand slid around its edge.

The fingers were stained with blood.

Meera backed away.

The door opened slowly.

A man emerged wearing a shopkeeper's apron. His face was pale, and a deep wound stretched across his forehead. One of his eyes had clouded over.

He stared at them.

For a moment, he didn't move.

Then his jaw dropped.

A guttural growl filled the shop.

"RUN!" Aarav shouted.

The infected shopkeeper charged.

Dev swung the hammer and struck a metal shelf instead of the man's head. The shelf toppled, sending packets and glass jars crashing across the floor.

Kabir grabbed Meera's backpack and pulled her toward the entrance.

Sana scrambled over a fallen crate.

The infected man slammed into Dev, knocking him against the counter.

Aarav rushed forward and struck the man's arm with the iron pipe.

The man turned toward him.

His mouth opened wider.

Aarav saw blood between his teeth.

He struck again, forcing the infected man backward long enough for Dev to escape.

"GET OUT!"

They scrambled toward the shutter.

Rishi slipped on a packet of flour and fell hard onto his knees.

"RISHI!" Kabir shouted.

The infected man lunged toward him.

Rishi kicked desperately, pushing himself backward across the floor.

Aarav grabbed his jacket and hauled him upright.

Together, they stumbled beneath the shutter just as the shopkeeper crashed into the counter.

Dev and Kabir pulled the shutter down.

Something slammed against it from inside.

Once.

Twice.

The metal shook.

The six friends ran.

They didn't stop until they reached the narrow lane behind the shop.

Rishi bent forward, gasping.

"I hate grocery shopping."

"Are you bitten?" Sana asked.

He looked at her.

"What?"

"Your arms. Your legs. Are you bitten?"

Rishi immediately checked himself.

His knees were scraped, and his palms were bleeding from the fall, but there were no obvious bite marks.

"I'm fine," he said. "Just scraped."

Aarav checked the others.

Everyone appeared to be unharmed.

They had lost the opportunity to collect most of the supplies, but they had managed to keep several packets of biscuits, some instant noodles, and two bottles of water.

It wasn't enough.

But it was something.

Dev looked back toward the shop.

"We can't go back there."

"No argument from me," Rishi replied.

Aarav glanced at the sky.

They had been gone less than an hour.

It felt like an entire day.

"We return to the apartment. We need to tell Priya about the radio transmission."

They started back.

None of them noticed the figure watching from the rooftop above the lane.

It stood perfectly still behind a water tank.

And when the six friends disappeared around the corner, the figure slowly lifted a radio to its mouth.

"Six survivors," a voice whispered through the device. "Moving west from the residential block."

A pause followed.

Then another voice answered.

"Do not approach them yet. Keep watching."

The figure lowered the radio.

Far below, the city continued to fall apart.

---

When the six friends returned to the neighbouring building, Priya was waiting near the entrance.

She looked relieved to see them.

"You're back."

"Mostly," Rishi replied. "The grocery shop tried to kill us."

Priya frowned.

"What happened?"

Aarav explained the attack and the radio transmission. When he mentioned the shelters and the warning about the eastern industrial zone, Priya's expression changed.

"What?" Sana asked.

Priya looked toward the staircase.

"My brother works in the eastern industrial area."

Nobody spoke.

"He was on a night shift when the outbreak started," she continued. "I haven't been able to contact him since yesterday."

Aarav watched her carefully.

"You think he might still be there?"

"I don't know."

She swallowed.

"But if those shelters are being established somewhere, maybe he knows where people are going."

Dev shook his head.

"We can't go searching for someone in an industrial zone when we barely survived a trip to a grocery shop."

"I'm not asking you to go right now," Priya replied. "I'm just telling you what I know."

Aarav looked at the others.

They had already lost one safe place. Their supplies were running low, and the city offered no reliable answers.

Now they had another lead.

And another reason to keep moving.

They climbed the stairs toward their apartment.

The moment they entered, Meera rushed to check on Nandini and Arjun.

Nandini was awake, but she looked worse.

Her face was flushed, and sweat dampened her hair. She was shivering beneath a blanket despite the heat.

Sana knelt beside her.

"Nandini? Can you hear me?"

Nandini nodded weakly.

"How are you feeling?"

"Cold."

Sana touched the back of her hand.

It was hot.

Aarav stood in the doorway.

He didn't need anyone to explain what they were all thinking.

Nandini looked at him.

"Did you find help?"

He hesitated.

"We found a radio. They're setting up shelters, but we don't know where yet."

"Will they help me?"

Aarav looked down.

"I hope so."

Nandini closed her eyes.

Arjun sat beside her, holding her hand.

"Mumma says we're going to find Papa."

Meera looked away.

The room became unbearably quiet.

Then Nandini opened her eyes again.

She stared at her son with a strange intensity.

"Arjun," she whispered. "Come closer."

He leaned toward her.

She gently touched his cheek.

"If anything happens to me, you must listen to Meera and Sana. Do you understand?"

Arjun shook his head.

"Nothing is going to happen."

Nandini's lips trembled.

"Promise me you'll listen."

The little boy began crying.

Aarav turned toward the window.

Outside, somewhere in the distance, a siren started wailing again.

But this time, it was followed by a sound none of them had heard before.

A long, continuous horn.

Deep.

Powerful.

Coming from somewhere beyond the city streets.

Kabir moved toward the window.

"What is that?"

Aarav pulled the curtain aside.

Far across the rooftops, a column of smoke rose near the railway line.

Then another horn sounded.

And another.

The noise rolled across the city like a warning.

Aarav stared into the distance.

Whatever was happening in Mumbai, it was getting worse.

And somewhere in the eastern industrial zone, someone was waiting for six survivors to make their next move.
