# Chapter 4 — Eleven Years Ago

Maya sat on the edge of the bed, staring at the photograph on her phone.

Her mother stood in front of Blackwood House.

June 17, 2003.

She had never seen the picture before.

Her mother had never mentioned this place.

Not once.

Maya opened her gallery and searched through every old photograph she had.

Nothing.

She searched her mother's old messages.

Nothing.

She opened the unknown number again.

The photograph was still there.

No name.

No explanation.

Just the picture.

Maya typed:

Who are you?

She waited.

No reply.

She typed again.

How do you know my mother?

Three dots appeared.

Maya stopped breathing.

Someone was typing.

Then the dots disappeared.

A message arrived.

Ask her yourself.

Maya stared at the screen.

Her hands went cold.

She slowly looked at the photograph.

"She's dead."

Another message appeared.

I know.

Maya's heart began pounding.

Then a third message.

But she left you something.

"What?"

No response.

Maya waited.

Nothing.

She threw the phone onto the bed.

"Enough."

She needed answers.

Real ones.

Not photographs.

Not whispers.

Not mysterious messages.

She stood and walked toward the hallway.

The locked room was still there.

Waiting.

---

Maya searched the house for almost an hour.

She found old cabinets, dusty books, family photographs, and boxes filled with documents.

Most of them were useless.

Bills.

Letters.

Newspapers.

Property papers.

Then she found a small wooden desk in the library.

One drawer was locked.

Maya tried pulling it.

Nothing.

She searched the desk.

Underneath it.

Behind it.

Inside the nearby cabinets.

Finally, she found a tiny brass key taped beneath the desk.

She stared at it.

"Seriously?"

She unlocked the drawer.

Inside was a collection of letters.

Most were tied together with a faded ribbon.

The first letter was dated May 2003.

Maya began reading.

It was written by her mother.

Her name appeared at the bottom.

Clara Reed.

Maya's fingers trembled.

She read the first line.

I don't know if I should stay.

Maya continued.

Her mother had written about coming to Blackwood House.

She had been twenty-four.

She had come there because of a woman named Evelyn's mother.

But the letters became increasingly strange.

Her mother wrote about hearing footsteps at night.

About seeing a girl standing at the end of the hallway.

About a room that didn't appear on the original house plans.

Then Maya found one sentence that made her stop.

They say the room chooses someone.

Maya read it again.

Then again.

Another letter.

June 12, 2003.

I saw her tonight.

Maya's eyes moved down the page.

She looks exactly like the girl in the old photograph.

Another.

June 15, 2003.

I finally understand why they are afraid of the room.

Maya's breathing became shallow.

The next line had been crossed out.

She could still make out a few words.

"Evelyn... necklace... daughter..."

Maya turned the page.

Nothing.

The rest of the letter had been torn away.

"Why?"

She searched through the remaining letters.

One was dated June 17, 2003.

The same date from the photograph.

Only three sentences remained.

If something happens to me, Maya must never come here.

Maya stopped.

Her own name.

She kept reading.

The room is not haunted.

A pause.

Then:

It remembers.

Maya's eyes widened.

The final sentence was written much darker than the others.

And it remembers her.

The letter ended there.

Maya dropped it onto the desk.

For a moment, she couldn't move.

Then she heard something.

A faint sound from upstairs.

A woman's voice.

"Maya..."

She looked toward the doorway.

"Maya..."

It sounded familiar.

Too familiar.

Her mother.

Maya stood so quickly that the chair fell backward.

"Mom?"

Silence.

She ran upstairs.

The hallway was empty.

"Mom?"

Nothing.

Then she heard it again.

From behind the locked door.

"Maya..."

Her mother's voice.

Maya walked toward the door.

Her hand slowly reached for the lock.

She stopped.

Her mother's letter echoed in her mind.

The room is not haunted.

It remembers.

Maya looked at the brass lock.

Then she noticed something.

There was a second keyhole.

A tiny silver key was already inside it.

Maya stared.

She hadn't put it there.

Slowly, she reached for the key.

Before she could touch it---

her phone vibrated.

A new message.

Unknown Number

Maya opened it.

Only four words:

Don't open the door.

She stared at the message.

Then at the key.

Then at the door.

Behind it, her mother's voice whispered again.

"Maya... please."

Maya's eyes filled with tears.

Because there was something she hadn't told anyone.

Not the property agent.

Not her friends.

Not even herself.

For the past eight years, she had occasionally dreamed about this house.

The same hallway.

The same locked door.

The same girl standing behind it.

And every single time, before waking up, the girl said the same thing.

"You promised you'd come back."

Maya slowly stepped away from the door.

Her phone vibrated one final time.

Another message appeared.

She remembers the promise too.

Maya looked at the locked room.

For the first time, she wasn't afraid of what might be inside.

She was afraid of what might happen if she didn't open it.
