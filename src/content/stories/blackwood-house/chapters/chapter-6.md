# Chapter 6 — Welcome Home

Maya stood completely still.

Broken glass covered the floor around her.

The words on the mirror remained:

YOU WERE NEVER COMING TO BLACKWOOD HOUSE FOR THE FIRST TIME.

Her mind refused to accept them.

She looked at the photograph again.

The little girl standing between her mother and Evelyn couldn't be anyone else.

It was Maya.

Six years old.

Wearing a white dress.

Wearing the silver necklace.

But Maya had no memory of ever being here.

She picked up another photograph from the floor.

Then another.

Each one showed the same house.

The same hallway.

The same girl.

Herself.

Different ages.

Six.

Eight.

Twelve.

Sixteen.

The photographs weren't memories.

They were moments she had forgotten.

A sound came from behind her.

"Maya."

She turned.

Her mother stood in the doorway.

Maya's breath disappeared.

"Mom?"

Clara looked exactly as she had in Maya's last memory of her.

The same gentle eyes.

The same dark hair.

The same small smile.

Maya's eyes filled with tears.

"You..."

Clara stepped into the room.

"I'm sorry."

Maya shook her head.

"No. No, you're dead."

"I know."

"Then how are you here?"

Her mother looked toward the broken mirror.

"Because you opened the door."

Maya wiped her tears.

"What is this place?"

Clara was silent for a moment.

"Do you remember when you were six?"

Maya shook her head.

"You told me you couldn't remember anything from that year."

"I don't."

"You couldn't remember because I asked them to take the memory away."

Maya stared at her.

"Who?"

Clara looked at the mirror.

"The house."

A cold wind passed through the room.

Maya stepped closer.

"Why would you bring me here?"

"I didn't."

"Then why am I here?"

Clara's expression broke.

"Because I failed."

Maya stared at her mother.

"Failed at what?"

"Keeping you away."

The room became silent.

Clara continued.

"When I came here in 2003, I found Evelyn. She had discovered what Blackwood House could do. It could preserve memories. It could show the past. And, under certain circumstances, it could bring people back."

Maya whispered:

"Bring them back?"

"Not exactly."

Clara looked at the photographs.

"The house doesn't bring people back. It keeps a part of them."

Maya understood.

"That's why the photographs..."

"Yes."

"That's why Evelyn is still here."

Clara nodded.

"And that's why you are here."

Maya's face went pale.

"What did you promise?"

Clara looked at her daughter.

"To return."

"Why?"

"Because when you were six, the house chose you."

Maya stepped backward.

"I don't understand."

"You entered this room."

Clara's voice trembled.

"You disappeared for three days."

Maya stared at her.

"Three days?"

"To you, it felt like a few minutes."

Maya's mind flashed.

A dark hallway.

A little girl holding her hand.

A silver necklace.

A voice telling her:

Don't be afraid.

She grabbed her head.

"I remember..."

Clara moved toward her.

"What?"

"Evelyn."

Maya looked up.

"She was here."

Clara nodded.

"You were playing together."

"And you came for me."

"I did."

"What happened?"

"I made a deal."

Maya's voice became barely audible.

"What deal?"

Clara took the necklace from Maya's hand.

"I gave the house my memories of you."

Maya stared at her.

"That's impossible."

"That's why you forgot this place."

Clara placed the necklace around Maya's neck.

"But the house kept one thing."

"What?"

Clara smiled sadly.

"You."

The walls began to shake.

The photographs fell one after another.

Evelyn appeared in the broken mirror.

Then the elderly woman.

Then dozens of other faces.

All the girls who had worn the necklace.

All watching Maya.

Clara stepped backward.

"It's time."

Maya grabbed her mother's hand.

"No. I'm not leaving you again."

"You have to."

"Why?"

"Because the house only opens the door once."

Maya looked at the mirror.

A doorway appeared inside it.

Beyond the doorway was darkness.

And in that darkness stood Evelyn.

She smiled.

Maya understood.

The house had never been trying to scare her.

It had been trying to bring her back.

Maya squeezed her mother's hand.

"Can you leave?"

Clara shook her head.

"Not unless you take my place."

Maya's eyes widened.

"No."

"It's the deal."

"Then I'll break it."

Clara smiled through her tears.

"You always were stubborn."

The house began to tremble violently.

The mirror cracked from top to bottom.

Evelyn reached through the broken glass.

Maya didn't run.

She stepped forward.

She grabbed Evelyn's hand.

The moment their fingers touched, everything disappeared.

The hallway.

The room.

The house.

The photographs.

And suddenly Maya remembered.

She remembered being six years old.

She remembered Evelyn.

She remembered her mother running into the room.

She remembered Clara crying.

And she remembered what she had promised.

Maya opened her eyes.

She was standing outside Blackwood House.

Morning sunlight filled the sky.

The house stood behind her.

Silent.

Empty.

Her mother was gone.

So was Evelyn.

Maya looked down.

The silver necklace was still around her neck.

But the pendant had changed.

There was a tiny engraving on the back.

She turned it over.

Three words were carved into the silver.

ONE MORE REMAINS.

Maya's phone vibrated.

She looked at the screen.

A message from the unknown number.

This time, there were only two words.

Look behind.

Maya slowly turned around.

Blackwood House was still there.

But one of the upstairs windows was now open.

A little girl stood behind it.

Maya's heart stopped.

The girl raised one hand.

And waved.

Maya looked at her reflection in the phone screen.

For a moment, she saw herself as a six-year-old girl.

Then the reflection smiled.

Not Maya.

Evelyn.

The screen went black.

And from inside the empty house came three quiet knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Maya looked toward the house.

She knew she should walk away.

Instead, she smiled faintly.

Because this time---

she remembered everything.

And Blackwood House had just made one mistake.

It had given Maya her memories back.

The End.
