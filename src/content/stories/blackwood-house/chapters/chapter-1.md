# Chapter 1 — The Room That Wasn't There

The house had been empty for eleven years.

At least, that was what everyone in the village said.

Maya stood at the bottom of the stone driveway, staring at the three-story house through the rain.

It was bigger than she had imagined.

Old.

Beautiful, in a way that made her uncomfortable.

Dark ivy covered most of the eastern wall, crawling around tall windows whose curtains had been drawn shut. The roof was uneven, and one chimney leaned slightly to the left.

There were no lights.

No cars.

No signs that anyone had lived there for years.

Yet Maya couldn't shake the feeling that someone was watching her.

She tightened her grip around the handle of her suitcase.

"You're sure this is the place?"

The driver nodded.

"Blackwood House."

He didn't get out of the car.

Maya noticed that.

"You aren't helping me take the bags?"

The driver looked toward the house.

Then back at her.

"No."

There was something strange in his voice.

Maya frowned.

"Why?"

He hesitated.

"Because I don't like staying here after sunset."

She almost laughed.

It was still afternoon.

"That's ridiculous."

The driver didn't smile.

"You'll understand."

He put the car into gear.

Maya stepped aside.

"Wait."

The car stopped.

She walked toward the driver's window.

"You said the owner was expecting me."

"He is."

"Then why didn't he come to meet me?"

The driver's eyes shifted toward the house again.

"He doesn't."

"Doesn't what?"

"Come outside."

Before Maya could ask anything else, the car drove away.

She watched it disappear down the wet road.

Then she turned toward the house.

For a few seconds, she stood completely still.

Rain tapped against her coat.

The wind moved through the trees.

And somewhere inside the house---

something knocked.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Maya's eyes moved toward the front door.

She told herself it was the wind.

Old houses made noises.

That was normal.

She picked up her suitcase and walked toward the entrance.

The key had been mailed to her two days earlier.

Along with a single sheet of paper.

There had been only one instruction written on it:

Do not enter the room at the end of the second-floor hallway.

Maya had thought it was a joke.

Now she wasn't so sure.

She unlocked the front door.

It opened with a long, low creak.

The smell hit her immediately.

Dust.

Old wood.

And something faintly sweet.

Like perfume.

She stepped inside.

The entrance hall was enormous.

A chandelier hung above her, covered in dust. Paintings lined the walls, their subjects staring into the distance with expressions that were impossible to read.

Maya shut the door behind her.

The sound echoed through the house.

"Hello?"

Her voice disappeared into the darkness.

No answer.

She took out her phone.

No signal.

Of course.

She sighed.

"Perfect."

Her boots clicked against the marble floor as she walked farther inside.

The house had been left almost exactly as it was.

A grandfather clock stood against the wall.

A piano sat beneath a sheet.

There were chairs covered in white fabric.

Everything looked as though someone had simply decided to leave one evening and never return.

Maya walked toward the staircase.

Then stopped.

Something was lying on the floor.

A photograph.

She crouched and picked it up.

It showed a family standing in front of the house.

A man.

A woman.

A little girl.

And a boy who looked about sixteen.

Maya turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

June 17, 2014.

She stared at the date.

Eleven years ago.

The year the house had supposedly been abandoned.

She turned the photograph over again.

Something bothered her.

She couldn't explain what.

She looked at the little girl.

Then at the boy.

Then---

Her breath caught.

There was someone standing in the upstairs window behind them.

A girl.

Barely visible through the glass.

Maya brought the photograph closer.

The girl appeared to be around her age.

Long dark hair.

Pale face.

And---

Maya froze.

The girl was wearing the exact same silver necklace Maya was wearing.

Her fingers immediately went to her throat.

The necklace had belonged to her mother.

Maya had worn it since she was sixteen.

There was no way the girl in the photograph could have been wearing it.

Unless---

She looked back toward the staircase.

For the first time, Maya noticed the portraits.

There were dozens of them.

But one wall was completely empty.

Except for a small brass plaque.

She walked toward it.

The plaque had only two words engraved into it:

MAYA REED

Her heart stopped.

She stared at the name.

Then at the photograph in her hand.

Then back at the plaque.

"No..."

She stepped backward.

The photograph slipped from her fingers.

It landed face-up on the marble floor.

The family was still there.

The house was still there.

The girl was still standing in the upstairs window.

But now Maya noticed something she hadn't seen before.

The girl wasn't looking at the camera.

She was looking directly at Maya.

And beneath the photograph, someone had written a sentence in black ink.

A sentence that definitely hadn't been there before.

WELCOME HOME, MAYA.
