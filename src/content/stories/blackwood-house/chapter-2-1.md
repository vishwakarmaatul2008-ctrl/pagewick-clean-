# Chapter 2 — The Second-Floor Hallway

Maya didn't move.

For several seconds, she simply stared at the photograph lying on the marble floor.

WELCOME HOME, MAYA.

The words were written in black ink beneath the family.

Her hands had begun to tremble.

She picked up the photograph again.

The writing was real.

Fresh.

She touched it with her thumb.

The ink hadn't smeared.

"That's impossible."

She looked around the entrance hall.

Nothing.

Only the steady ticking of the grandfather clock.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Maya suddenly remembered the driver's words.

I don't like staying here after sunset.

She looked toward the windows.

The sky outside was already turning gray.

She needed to find the person who owned this house.

Immediately.

She pulled out her phone and tried calling the number from the property agent.

No signal.

She walked toward the front door.

Before her hand reached the handle, something caught her attention.

A staircase.

She hadn't noticed it properly when she entered.

It curved upward into darkness.

Maya stared at it.

Second floor.

The forbidden room was up there.

She looked back at the photograph.

The girl in the window seemed to be watching her.

Maya swallowed.

"Fine."

She picked up her suitcase.

"I'm just going upstairs."

---

The second floor was colder.

Much colder.

Maya pulled her coat tighter around herself.

The hallway stretched almost the entire length of the house.

Five doors stood on the left.

Four on the right.

At the very end was a tall wooden door.

Unlike the others, it had no handle.

Only a metal lock.

Maya stopped.

That had to be it.

The room from the letter.

She took a few steps toward it.

Then stopped again.

Something was wrong.

There was a portrait hanging beside the door.

Maya raised her phone and used its flashlight.

The portrait showed the same family from the photograph.

The father.

The mother.

The boy.

The girl.

But there was something different.

In the photograph, the girl had been standing beside her parents.

In the painting, she was standing alone.

And beneath the painting was a name.

EVELYN BLACKWOOD

Maya stared at the girl.

Long dark hair.

Pale face.

That necklace.

Her necklace.

Maya instinctively touched her throat.

The silver chain was still there.

A tiny circular pendant rested against her skin.

She had never seen another one like it.

She stepped closer to Evelyn's portrait.

There was a small crack across the girl's face.

Someone had deliberately damaged it.

Maya lifted her phone to take a picture.

The screen flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then went completely black.

"What?"

She pressed the power button.

Nothing.

Her battery had been at eighty percent.

Maya frowned.

She put the phone into her pocket.

Then she heard footsteps.

Behind her.

Slow.

Heavy.

Step.

...

Step.

...

Step.

Maya turned.

The hallway was empty.

Her heart began pounding.

"Hello?"

No answer.

She looked toward the staircase.

Nothing.

She looked behind the nearest door.

Nothing.

Then---

Step.

Right behind her.

Maya spun around.

The hallway remained empty.

She backed away.

Her shoulder touched the wall.

And something cold brushed against her ear.

A whisper.

"You shouldn't have come back."

Maya's entire body froze.

She turned.

Nothing.

"No..."

She stumbled away from the wall.

"Who's there?"

Silence.

Then the grandfather clock downstairs began to strike.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Maya counted.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Eight.

Nine.

Ten.

Eleven.

Twelve.

She waited for the thirteenth strike.

It never came.

Instead, she heard something else.

A door opening.

At the end of the hallway.

Maya slowly looked toward the forbidden room.

The door was still closed.

But beneath it---

a thin line of warm yellow light appeared.

Maya stopped breathing.

Someone was inside.

She took one step forward.

Then another.

The light disappeared.

The door was silent again.

Maya stood there for a long moment.

Finally, she turned around.

She needed to leave.

Now.

But when she reached the staircase, she stopped.

There was a new photograph sitting on the floor.

She hadn't seen it before.

Maya crouched down.

Her fingers shook as she picked it up.

It showed the second-floor hallway.

The exact hallway where she was standing.

And at the end of it stood Maya.

Wearing the same clothes.

Holding the same photograph.

She stared at the image.

Then slowly turned around.

The hallway behind her was empty.

Maya looked back at the photograph.

There was one final detail.

Someone was standing behind the Maya in the photograph.

A girl with long dark hair.

Evelyn.

Maya's breathing became shallow.

She looked at the end of the hallway.

Nothing.

She looked at the photograph again.

Evelyn was smiling.

Then Maya noticed the date written on the back.

June 17, 2014.

The same date as the first photograph.

Her stomach dropped.

Because beneath the date was another sentence.

Written in the same black ink.

SHE HAS BEEN WAITING FOR YOU.
